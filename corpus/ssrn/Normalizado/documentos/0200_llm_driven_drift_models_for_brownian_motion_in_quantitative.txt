LLM-Driven Drift Models for Brownian Motion in
Quantitative Finance
Yifan Guo
January 31, 2026
Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) extract high-dimensional semantic signals from unstructured
financial text, while Brownian motion and stochastic differential equations (SDEs) remain
canonical for continuous-time asset modeling. Most existing approaches keep these tools separate:
continuous-time models rely on low-dimensional parametric drifts/volatilities, whereas LLMs
are used primarily for discrete-time prediction or qualitative analysis. We propose a rigorous
framework for LLM-driven drift SDEs, in which the drift of a Brownian-motion model is a
measurable functional of an LLM-derived market-context representation. We formalize the
model on a filtered probability space, provide well-posedness (existence/uniqueness of strong
solutions) under natural regularity conditions, and characterize induced risk-neutral measures
via a Girsanov transform. On the statistical side, we analyze drift estimation via empirical risk
minimization (ERM) with LLM features, and state sufficient conditions for consistency in a highfrequency sampling regime. Empirically, we outline an evaluation protocol on equities and index
futures using pretrained LLM embeddings from news and order-book commentary, and extend
the model to an LLM-conditioned diffusion. Across forecasting, hitting-time calibration, and
quadratic-variation decomposition by embedding-defined regimes, the proposed framework aims
to match or outperform classical parametric SDEs and black-box deep discrete-time baselines
while retaining a clean stochastic-process interpretation.
1 Introduction
Brownian motion and its generalizations are foundational in quantitative finance, underpinning
models from Black–Scholes to stochastic volatility and jump–diffusions. In parallel, LLMs have
transformed how unstructured information—news, earnings calls, social media, analyst notes, and
order-book commentary—is summarized into features. Despite this, standard practice largely treats
continuous-time stochastic modeling and LLM-based representation learning as separate tools:
SDEs are calibrated under stylized parametric drifts/volatilities, while LLMs produce discrete-time
predictors or sentiment scores.
This separation is increasingly limiting in markets where prices reflect both fast microstructural
noise and slower, text-driven regime shifts. Parametric drifts cannot incorporate high-dimensional
semantics in a principled way; discrete-time neural predictors often lose interpretability and the
theoretical toolkit of martingales, quadratic variation, and hitting times.
1

Goal.
We seek a framework in which:
• the randomness remains modeled by Brownian motion (preserving continuous-time stochastic
calculus); and
• the drift (and optionally volatility) depends flexibly on an LLM representation of evolving market
context.
Contributions.
• LLM-driven drift SDE. We define SDEs whose drift is a measurable functional of LLM
embeddings of contextual text.
• Well-posedness. Under Lipschitz and linear-growth conditions (including in the embedding
argument), we prove existence and uniqueness of strong solutions.
• Risk-neutral measures.
We describe an equivalent martingale measure via a Girsanov
transform and specify sufficient integrability (Novikov/Kazamaki-type) conditions.
• Learning and consistency.
We propose ERM for drift estimation from high-frequency
increments with LLM features and provide a consistency theorem under standard M-estimation
conditions.
• Empirical protocol. We outline tasks (forecasting, hitting times, quadratic variation by
regimes) that stress the continuous-time interpretation.

2.3 LLM-based representations
Let (Ct)t∈[0,T] denote the (possibly unstructured) market context at time t: news text, earnings-call

7 Empirical Evaluation Protocol
This section specifies what to measure and why it aligns with the continuous-time theory. Numerical
results and plots should be filled in with real data.
7.1 Data and preprocessing
For each asset:
• Construct log-price (or mid-price) samples Xtk on a regular grid (e.g., 1-min or 5-min).
• Aggregate contextual text Ctk over a recent window (news headlines, filings, order-book commentary).
• Compute embeddings Ztk = LLM(Ctk), optionally compress (PCA) to reduce d and stabilize
learning.
• Standardize features and align timestamps to avoid lookahead bias (strictly causal windows).
7.2 Baselines
Compare against:
• Classical SDEs: constant drift/volatility (Black–Scholes log model), Ornstein–Uhlenbeck for
mean-reverting factors.
• Discrete-time volatility models: GARCH(1,1) variants on returns.
• Deep discrete-time models: transformer/time-series baselines with and without concatenated
LLM features.
This separation isolates the gain from (i) LLM semantics, (ii) continuous-time structure, and (iii)
the interaction.

7.4 Hitting times
Choose barrier levels a relative to current Xtk (e.g., a = Xtk + α for fixed α). For each model:
• Condition on the realized embedding path (Zt) over a horizon (piecewise constant interpolation
from the grid).
• Simulate many SDE paths using estimated parameters to compute bτa.
• Compare empirical CDFs of τa across models vs. realized data (or bootstrap proxies).
Hitting-time calibration stresses whether drift+diffusion respond correctly to embedding-defined
regimes.

8 Related Work
8.1 LLMs in finance
LLMs and transformers are widely used for sentiment, event extraction, earnings-call understanding,
and text-to-signal pipelines. Most of these methods are discrete-time predictors and do not explicitly
embed signals into continuous-time stochastic calculus.
8.2 Neural SDEs and conditional diffusion
Neural SDEs learn flexible drifts/diffusions and can be conditioned on covariates. Our contribution is
a structured conditional SDE where conditioning features are explicitly LLM embeddings of market
10

text, paired with finance-facing diagnostics (hitting times, quadratic variation) and risk-neutral
measure construction.
8.3 Classical continuous-time finance models
Black–Scholes, Heston, OU, and Lévy/jump models provide interpretable continuous-time foundations but rely on rigid parametric forms. LLM-driven coefficients offer a middle ground: feature-rich
adaptivity with preserved stochastic-process semantics.
9 Conclusion and Future Work
We proposed a rigorous framework for LLM-driven drift SDEs, unifying semantic market context
(via LLM embeddings) with Brownian-motion-based continuous-time modeling. We established
well-posedness under natural Lipschitz/growth assumptions, constructed risk-neutral measures via
Girsanov under integrability conditions, and provided an ERM drift estimation scheme with a
consistency result in a high-frequency learning regime. Empirically, we outlined evaluation tasks
that emphasize continuous-time interpretability, including hitting-time calibration and regime-wise
quadratic variation.
Promising directions include: (i) joint drift–diffusion estimation theory with rates and asymptotic
normality; (ii) multi-asset and multidimensional factor extensions for cross-sectional modeling and
portfolio optimization; (iii) stronger conditioning mechanisms (attention over text windows) while
retaining measurability and causality; (iv) counterfactual stress testing under hypothetical news
trajectories.

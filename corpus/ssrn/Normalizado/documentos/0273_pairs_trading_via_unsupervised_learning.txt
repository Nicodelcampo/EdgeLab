Pairs trading, a market-neutral statistical arbitrage 
strategy, faces limitations in traditional approaches, 
including subjective pair selection and rigid trading rules. 
This paper develops an intelligent futures pairs trading 
framework integrating system clustering, deep learning 
prediction, and reinforcement learning (RL) decisionmaking. 
First, 
we 
propose 
a 
novel 
Dynamic 
Cointegration (DC) distance measure that combines 
Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) for short-term similarity 
with cointegration testing for long-term stability, and 
employs system clustering algorithm for intelligent pair 
selection. 
Second, 
a 
multi-dimensional 
technical 
indicator system covering trend, volatility, and volumeprice metrics is built and fused with Long Short-Term 
Memory (LSTM) networks to forecast price spreads. 
Finally, an RL module based on the Advantage ActorCritic (A2C) algorithm incorporates spread predictions 
into the state space and employs a diversified reward 
function to learn optimal trading strategies.
Empirical analysis and out-of-sample tests use daily data 
from 50 Chinese futures products (2019-2024). Results 
indicate: (1) DC clustering effectively identifies stable 
pairs like rebar-hot-rolled coil and coking coal-coke; (2) 
The LSTM-A2C model with spread features yields strong 
risk-adjusted returns, with Sharpe ratios typically >1.0, 
max 2.295; (3) It outperforms baseline A2C and 
traditional 1.5 SD strategies in profitability, robustness, 
and efficiency. This study offers a new framework and 
empirical evidence for intelligent statistical arbitrage in 
futures markets.

1. Introduction
6
7
In the field of asset allocation in financial markets, futures pairs trading, as a market-neutral 
8
arbitrage strategy, is widely used in the investment practices of both institutional and individual 
9
investors. Gatev et al. [1] first introduced the concept and basic principles of pairs trading, 
10
systematically demonstrating its profitability in the stock market. The basic principle of pairs trading 
11
is to select two assets with highly correlated historical prices. When the price deviates from the 
12
mean, traders short the asset with the higher price while going long on the asset with the lower price. 
13
They then close both positions when the price returns to equilibrium, earning the spread profit. Since 
14
pairs trading depends on the relative price movements between assets rather than the overall market 
15
direction, it is applicable not only to stock and bond markets but also to the futures market and other 
16
fields. The advantage of pairs trading lies in its ability to effectively avoid systematic risk during 
17
market volatility or significant adjustments, providing investors with a robust arbitrage tool.
18
The implementation of traditional pairs trading typically includes two main steps. Firstly, a pairs 
19
pool is constructed by selecting asset pairs with strong correlations or cointegration relationships 
20
from a broad market. Secondly, after constructing the pairs pool, traders need to set trading rules, 
21
such as opening or closing positions when the spread reaches a certain threshold.
22
The selection of trading pairs is the core step of a pairs trading strategy, and common methods 
23
include the distance methods, correlation methods, and cointegration methods. Goetzmann et al. [2] 
24
selected pairs of assets by minimizing the standardized spread of historical prices, achieving an 
25
annualized return of 12% from 1962 to 1997. The cointegration theory proposed by Engle and 
26
Granger [3] provides the core mathematical framework for pairs trading, confirming the long-term 
27
equilibrium relationship by testing the stationarity of residuals. Vidyamurthy [4] proposed a two28
stage stock selection method, first screening by correlation coefficient (e.g., above 0.90) and then 
29
performing cointegration tests, providing a complete theory and methodology for financial asset 
30
pairs trading. In addition, Huck et al. [5] conducted a comparative study between cointegration and 
31
distance methods, finding that the former significantly outperforms the latter. Blázquez et al. [6] 
32
conducted a comprehensive analysis of correlation, distance, random, stochastic differential 
33
residuals, and cointegration methods, concluded that cointegration is the most effective method for 
34
constructing a pairs trading strategy. Inspired by the cointegration concept, Ramos-Requena et al. 
35
[7] introduced the Hurst exponent to identify pairs trading assets and found that the pairs selected 
36
using the Hurst exponent had higher statistical arbitrage effects. Bui et al. [8] used the generalized 
37
Hurst exponent and cointegration methods to select asset pairs and compared the trading 
38
performance of pairs selected by the two methods, finding that pairs selected using the generalized 
39
Hurst exponent had better statistical arbitrage effects.
40
In recent years, in the selection of pairs, methods such as manual classification or unsupervised 
41
learning, particularly clustering techniques, have also been applied to the construction of pairs pools. 
42
For example, Do et al. [9] proposed constructing a pairs pool through industry grouping and strict

52
In terms of pairs trading strategy design, the most common trading model follows the framework 
53
proposed by Gatev et al. [1] , which determines the trading threshold through a normalized historical 
54
spread method. However, as market inefficiencies gradually improve, the profit opportunities of 
55
pairs trading strategies constructed using traditional subjective methods and fixed parameters are 
56
becoming increasingly limited. To address this issue, scholars have introduced various optimization 
57
algorithms and prediction models to dynamically adjust trading parameters. For example, some 
58
researchers use intelligent optimization algorithms to determine the dynamic thresholds in pairs 
59
trading strategies. Fu et al. [13] aiming to maximize profits or minimize maximum drawdowns 
60
(MDD), applied genetic algorithms (GAs) for optimization, using the optimized results to train 
61
machine learning (ML) models, reducing MDD by 73.24% in cryptocurrency. Baek et al. [14] 
62
applied ML methods to pairs trading in the futures market, using the error correction model (ECM) 
63
framework based on the Engle-Granger method and support vector machine (SVM) algorithms to 
64
determine the hedging ratio. The research showed that this algorithmic strategy generated higher 
65
risk-adjusted returns and lower market risk exposure across multiple futures markets, maintaining 
66
profitability even during systemic negative events like the COVID-19 pandemic.
67
In the field of financial time series forecasting, deep learning (DL) methods have demonstrated 
68
strong predictive capabilities. Research by Eapen et al. [15] , Ali et al. [16], Srijiranon [17] , and 
69
Koo [18] indicated that the LSTM network perform well in capturing complex patterns and 
70
dependencies in time series data, making it highly suitable for predicting stock prices. As a result, 
71
prediction algorithms to determine dynamic thresholds in pairs trading have also been widely 
72
applied. For example, Zhan et al. [19] proposed a strategy that uses ML models to predict the spread 
73
and sets trading signals based on the predictions, proving the effectiveness of this method in 
74
exploring arbitrage opportunities. Flori et al. [20] studied a method based on the LSTM network to 
75
identify and integrate pairs trading opportunities in stocks, using LSTM to estimate the probability 
76
of stocks outperforming their peers and combining these predictions with traditional trading 
77
practices based on price or return differences. The results showed that strategies incorporating these 
78
predictions could significantly improve portfolio performance. Liou et al. [21] focused on high79
frequency pairs trading scenarios, using intraday continuous trading data from the Taiwan Stock 
80
Exchange. By performing feature selection with the eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) and

In recent years, deep reinforcement learning (DRL), as a cutting-edge technology in the field of 
88
artificial intelligence, has been widely applied in the field of financial quantitative trading (Pippas 
89
et al.[22]), particularly showing great potential in pairs trading strategies. Traditional pairs trading 
90
relies on static threshold-triggered trading signals; however, as market efficiency improves and 
91
dynamics increase, the effectiveness of this method gradually declines. DRL combines the dynamic 
92
decision-making ability of RL and the capability of DL to process high-dimensional, complex data 
93
(Pippas et al. [22]). Its introduction provides new possibilities for optimizing pairs trading strategies. 
94
The following section will review the evolution of DRL applications in pairs trading, summarize 
95
key research contributions, and discuss its advantages and challenges.
96
In terms of technological evolution, early researches primarily focused on applying basic Deep 
97
Q Networks (DQN) to pairs trading. Brim [23] was the first to apply Double Deep Q Networks 
98
(DDQN) to stock market pairs trading, demonstrating that the RL system could effectively learn the 
99
mean-reversion patterns of cointegrated stock pairs and successfully make predictions based on 
100
these patterns. Additionally, by introducing a negative reward multiplier parameter, the system was 
101
able to adopt more conservative trading actions. Kim et al. [24] further proposed an optimized pairs 
102
trading strategy using DQN to simultaneously optimize trading boundaries and stop-loss boundaries. 
103
By discretizing these boundaries, they trained the agent to select the optimal strategies to maximize 
104
the expected sum of discounted future profits. Wang et al. [25] made significant contributions to 
105
improving learning efficiency by proposing a simple yet effective reward method, using a baseline 
106
strategy as input to accelerate and stabilize the learning process. In an empirical analysis of the 
107
Nasdaq Nordic market, they achieved higher returns and Sharpe ratios compared to traditional 
108
strategies.
109
As research advanced, scholars began exploring more complex architectural designs. Wu et al. 
110
[26] addressed the time series characteristics of stock market data, used Gated Recurrent Units 
111
(GRU) to extract financial features and proposed two adaptive trading strategies, GDQN and GDPG. 
112
Among these, GDPG, which has an Actor-Critic (AC) framework, performed more stably in volatile 
113
markets. Quintero et al. [27] conducted a comparative study of the A2C and Proximal Policy 
114
Optimization (PPO) algorithms in continuous action spaces and found that although RL strategies 
115
achieved positive investment metrics in most test pairs, the benchmark model might still outperform 
116
in certain situations.
117
In the realm of systematic framework design, researchers have proposed more comprehensive 
118
solutions. Xu et al. [28] introduced a two-level RL framework that uses an extended option-critic

137
Overall, the above studies show that the application of DRL in pairs trading has evolved from 
138
simple to complex, from single-objective to multi-objective optimization, and from fixed 
139
architectures to adaptive frameworks. Researchers have continuously improved pairs trading 
140
strategies by introducing techniques such as reward shaping, hybrid architectures, structural 
141
breakthrough detection, continuous action spaces, feature extraction, multi-level optimization, and 
142
hierarchical learning. These studies have not only advanced the theoretical application of RL in the 
143
financial field but have also demonstrated its superiority over traditional methods in practice. 
144
However, they also point out that there is still room for improvement in terms of model robustness 
145
and adaptability.
146
Inspired by the above literature, this paper aims to improve the traditional pairs trading model in 
147
terms of pair selection and trading model design. Firstly, we propose a system clustering method 
148
based on the DC distance metric to cluster futures price time series data and construct pairs trading 
149
combinations. Secondly, based on the pairs combination information, we construct a multi150
dimensional technical indicator system that includes trend, volatility, and volume-price indicators. 
151
A LSTM network is then utilized to predict the price spread time series, providing predictive 
152
information input for RL decision-making. Finally, we design a RL decision module based on the 
153
A2C algorithm, incorporating the predictive information into the state space and guiding the agent 
154
to learn the optimal trading strategy through a diversified reward function. Empirical results show 
155
that the proposed strategy demonstrates strong profitability and risk control effects across multiple 
156
time windows, particularly performing well in volatile markets.

2. Methodology
176
177
This study constructs an intelligent pairs trading framework that integrates stock selection 
178
through clustering, spread prediction, and RL decision-making. The framework achieves the full 
179
process from pair selection to trade execution through the organic combination of three core 
180
modules. The overall modeling approach follows the "screening-prediction-decision" logic: Firstly, 
181
a system clustering method based on DC distance is used to select asset pairs with similar dynamic 
182
features and long-term cointegration relationships from a large pool of assets. Secondly, technical 
183
indicators are used to construct a spread feature system, and the LSTM network is employed to 
184
accurately predict the spread trend. Finally, the predictive information is integrated into the RL 
185
environment, with the A2C algorithm used to make trading decisions.
186
The core innovation of this framework lies in transforming the traditional subjective decision187
making process of pairs trading into a data-driven intelligent decision-making process. Compared 
188
to traditional methods, this framework combines short-term dynamic similarity from DTW and 
189
long-term stability of cointegration relationships in the pair selection phase, and it applies this 
190
combination for the first time in system clustering, proposing a new method for asset pair selection. 
191
In the trading decision phase, it constructs feature indicators related to the spread and introduces the 
192
LSTM spread prediction module, providing forward-looking information to the RL agent, 
193
enhancing the scientific basis and timeliness of decision-making. In the strategy execution phase, 
194
the A2C algorithm replaces the fixed-threshold method, achieving dynamic optimization of trade

2.3 Construction of Pairs Trading Model Based on RL
420
421
The core challenge of pairs trading lies in how to make optimal trading decisions in a complex 
422
and ever-changing market environment. Traditional methods rely on fixed rules and subjective 
423
experience, which makes it difficult to adapt to dynamic market changes. This paper constructs a 
424
pairs trading model that integrates LSTM-based spread prediction and A2C RL algorithm. The 
425
model implements pairs trading through a prediction-decision process. Firstly, the LSTM network 
426
predicts future spread trends based on historical technical indicators, providing forward-looking 
427
information for decision-making. Secondly, the A2C algorithm integrates the current market state 
428
and the predictive information to learn the optimal trading strategy. Finally, the trading execution 
429
module translates the strategy into specific buy and sell actions.

2.3.1 LSTM Spread Prediction Module
432
433
In the field of financial time series forecasting, LSTM has demonstrated strong predictive 
434
capabilities. Hu [34] employed an LSTM model based on PCA and LASSO for stock prediction, 
435
significantly improving prediction accuracy, which proved the superiority of LSTM in handling 
436
financial data. Ouyang et al. [35] combined wavelet analysis with LSTM neural network to build a 
437
prediction model suitable for nonlinear and non-stationary financial time series. Empirical evidence 
438
showed that LSTM performed excellently in predicting both long-term and short-term dynamic 
439
trends. These researches indicate that LSTM can capture complex dynamic patterns in financial time 
440
series, exhibiting high predictive accuracy and generalization ability.
441
To enhance time series modeling capabilities, this paper designs a multi-layer LSTM network 
442
structure with powerful time series modeling capabilities. The network consists of three LSTM 
443
layers, each followed by a dropout layer to prevent overfitting. Finally, the output from the LSTM 
444
layers is passed through a fully connected layer to output the prediction result. The input dimension 
445
is determined by the number of technical indicators after feature engineering, the hidden layer 
446
dimension is set to 50 neurons, and a one-step-ahead forecasting approach is adopted.
447
Additionally, this paper uses an exhaustive search-based feature importance analysis algorithm 
448
to optimize feature selection. This algorithm explores all possible feature subset combinations and 
449
evaluates the prediction performance of each combination using a loss function, automatically 
450
selecting the optimal feature set. This method not only discovers the nonlinear interactions between 
451
features but also significantly improves prediction accuracy, demonstrating the effectiveness of the 
452
technical indicators constructed in this paper for predicting the spread.

3. Empirical Study
616
617
For the empirical analysis of this paper, the daily data of 50 domestic futures varieties from 
618
January 1, 2019, to December 31, 2024, provided by the Tushare library in Python, are adopted as 
619
the research sample. The sample covers multiple sectors including ferrous metals, chemicals, 
620
agricultural products, precious metals and treasury bonds, which systematically verifies the 
621
application effect of deep reinforcement learning in pairs trading in the futures market.To ensure 
622
the robustness of the results, the study designs three rolling time windows, namely 2019–2022, 
623
2020–2023 and 2021–2024. Within each window, the data of the first three years are used for pair 
624
selection and model training (with 2 years as the training set and 1 year as the validation set), while 
625
the data of the last year are used for out-of-sample backtesting to evaluate the actual investment 
626
performance of the strategy.

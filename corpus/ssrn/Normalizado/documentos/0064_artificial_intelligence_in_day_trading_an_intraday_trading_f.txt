Abstract
This paper introduces a novel intraday trading strategy for the USTEC CFD, leveraging volatility patterns
around economic news events. By integrating a large language model (LLM) to filter trading signals based on news
sentiment, the strategy achieves significant improvements in profitability. Backtested from 2018 to 2025 with over
1,700 trades, the backbone strategy delivers a cumulative return of 730% and an annualized return of 33%. The
LLM-enhanced version further increases the annual return by 5%, outperforming the buy-and-hold benchmark and
maintaining a high alpha. Additionally, the paper investigates the stability and interpretability of LLMs in historical
backtesting, revealing that while prompt variations affect filtering efficacy, identical prompts yield consistent results,
enhancing the framework’s reliability. These findings highlight the potential of AI-driven approaches in quantitative
finance and suggest avenues for future research in prompt engineering and multi-model architectures.

Introduction
This paper presents a pioneering intraday trading strategy for the USTEC CFD, harnessing volatility from economic
news events and integrating a large language model (LLM) to refine trading signals through sentiment analysis.
Backtested from 2018 to 2025 with over 1,700 trades, the strategy achieves a 730% cumulative return and a 33%
annualized return, with the LLM-enhanced version boosting performance by an additional 5%. Outperforming the
buy-and-hold benchmark, it showcases significant alpha and probes the stability and interpretability of LLMs in financial
applications. Through detailed analysis, we outline the strategy’s design, performance metrics, and implications,
bridging traditional quantitative finance with AI-driven innovation while paving the way for future research in prompt
engineering and advanced model architectures.

Literature Review
The integration of macroeconomic indicators into financial modeling has emerged as a critical approach for enhancing
predictive accuracy in trading strategies, particularly for volatile instruments like the USTEC CFD, which tracks the
Nasdaq-100 (Finance Brokerage, 2024). Latif (2024) demonstrated that combining macroeconomic variables—such as
the Volatility Index (VIX), Economic Policy Uncertainty (US EPU), and Financial Stress Index (FSI)—with technical
indicators significantly improves S&P 500 return forecasts. Their analysis, covering January 2001 to June 2023, found
that a LeNet-Highway hybrid model reduced root mean squared error by 11% compared to single models, emphasizing
the role of macroeconomic data in capturing market volatility. Similarly, Ryu (2022) examined 392,141,196 transaction
data points from 2010 to 2018 to assess the intraday impact of scheduled macroeconomic news announcements. They
identified asymmetric market responses, with positive GDP news reducing sentiment, highlighting the complex influence
of economic indicators on intraday trading.
In parallel, large language models (LLMs) have transformed financial analysis by enabling sophisticated processing
of unstructured data, such as news sentiment, for trading applications (Cao, 2023). Their comprehensive survey outlined
LLMs’ roles in trading, risk modeling, and sentiment analysis, proposing a decision framework for model selection.
Domain-specific models like BloombergGPT outperformed general-purpose models like BLOOM176B, achieving a
62.51 score on finance classification tasks versus 54.35, underscoring LLMs’ potential in signal generation. Rahimikia
(2024) further showed that LLMs pre-trained on financial datasets from 2007 to 2023 excel in return prediction while
mitigating look-ahead bias (Harris, 2022). Additionally, Kim (2024) found that GPT-based models surpass human
analysts in predicting earnings changes, particularly in complex scenarios, suggesting LLMs’ ability to enhance intraday
trading signals.

Despite these advances, the integration of macroeconomic indicators with LLMs for intraday trading, especially for
the USTEC CFD, remains underexplored. Research by Niu (2023) and Salisu (2022) has explored geopolitical risks and
economic uncertainty’s effects on stock market volatility, providing risk management insights relevant to day trading.
However, these studies do not address the use of LLM-derived sentiment analysis alongside economic indicators to
optimize intraday strategies. This paper fills this gap by proposing a volatility-based trading framework enhanced by
DeepSeek LLM signal filtering. By building on prior work in macroeconomic forecasting and LLM applications, this
study advances algorithmic trading, demonstrating how AI-driven sentiment analysis can improve risk-adjusted returns
and profitability in the USTEC CFD market.

Strategy Definition
3.1
Backbone Strategy
This strategy leverages market volatility triggered by high-impact US economic events, using a breakout approach
to capture significant price movements post-announcement. It focuses on scheduled USD events from the MQL5
Terminal, selected for their consistent market-moving potential (MQL5, n.d.). Regularly scheduled announcements
create predictable volatility patterns, while avoiding ad-hoc events reduces noise. The key event categories include:
• Interest Rate Decisions (FOMC)
• Employment Data (NFP, Unemployment)
• Inflation Metrics (CPI, PPI)
• Growth Indicators (GDP)
• Central Bank Communications
• Business Activity (PMI, Retail Sales)

To prevent account over-leveraging during consecutive event occurrences, the strategy verifies existing active stop orders

3.2
Large Language Model Integration
This subsection details the integration of a Large Language Model (LLM) to enhance the backbone strategy’s trading
signals. Utilizing the DeepSeek Chat API, the framework evaluates economic indicator announcements to determine
their impact on the Nasdaq 100 (USTEC) index. The LLM serves as a filter, refining buy and sell signals by analyzing
market sentiment and forecast deviations in real time.
3.2.1
Filtering Economic Indicators
Post the two-minute mark after an announcement, the algorithm processes all news events adhering to the backbone
strategy’s conditions. It filters out announcements without quantifiable actual values (e.g., FOMC meetings) or those
with delayed updates, ensuring only reliable and actionable data is considered. If multiple events occur simultaneously,
the average confidence level across these events will be used as the reference value during the confidence level validation
phase.
Quantifiable actual values enable precise deviation analysis from forecasts, critical for effective sentiment evaluation.
Excluding non-quantifiable or delayed data minimizes noise and enhances signal reliability.
3.2.2
LLM Prompt Construction
The Large Language Model (LLM) is queried using structured prompts sent to the DeepSeek Chat API to analyze the
impact of economic news on the Nasdaq 100 index. The prompts and input data are defined as follows:
• System Prompt: “Analyze the impact of this economic news on increasing Nasdaq100 value for the day, based
on market sentiment and forecast deviations. Provide a JSON output with ‘confidence’ (0 to 1) and ‘reason’ (less
than 50 words). Example: {“confidence”: 0.7, “reason”: “Positive economic indicators suggest growth”}” This
prompt instructs the LLM to evaluate market sentiment and forecast deviations, returning a structured JSON
response with a confidence score and a concise rationale.
• User Prompt: “Evaluate this news event: {“event”: “[event name]”, “actual”: [actual value], “forecast”:
[forecast value], “previous”: [previous value]}. Output in JSON format with ‘confidence’ and ‘reason’.” This
prompt provides the LLM with specific event details—event name, actual reported value, forecast value, and
previous value—to perform the analysis.
• News Data Input: A JSON object specifying the DeepSeek Chat model, containing an array of messages that
includes the system prompt (defining the task and output format) and the user prompt (supplying event-specific
data: event name, actual value, forecast value, and previous value). The input also includes a directive to ensure
the response is formatted as a JSON object for automated processing.
The system prompt ensures consistent, structured outputs suitable for algorithmic integration, while the user
prompt delivers precise event data for accurate sentiment analysis. The JSON-structured news data input facilitates
seamless API communication and reliable response parsing.
The system prompt specifies the analysis task and output structure, while the user prompt provides event-specific
data—event name, actual value, forecast, and previous value—for consistent processing.
3.2.3
Confidence Level Extraction and Signal Validation
The LLM outputs a confidence level (C ∈[0, 1]) reflecting the predicted Nasdaq 100 impact. This is applied to validate
backbone strategy signals:
• Buy Signal: Valid if C > 0.25.
• Sell Signal: Valid if C < 0.75.
Thresholds of 0.25 for buys and 0.75 for sells filter out ambiguous signals. A buy confidence above 0.25 confirms
sufficient positive sentiment, while a sell confidence below 0.75 prevents shorting in potentially bullish scenarios,
optimizing signal accuracy.
3.2.4
Position Management and Risk Protocol
Position closure and risk management align with the backbone strategy: positions close at 15:50 ET, with risk controlled
via a Pstop stop loss and Prisk equity allocation. This ensures the study focuses solely on the LLM’s filtering efficacy.

Backtest Methodology
4.1
Addressing Risk of Look-ahead Bias
Backtesting trading strategies that incorporate news events, such as economic indicators, poses unique challenges due
to the risk of look-ahead bias. Look-ahead bias occurs when a backtest utilizes information that was not available
at the time of the trading decision, potentially inflating the strategy’s perceived performance (Harris, 2022). In the
context of news-based trading, this bias can manifest if the backtest assumes immediate knowledge of news releases
or uses data revisions that were only available later (Analyzing Alpha, 2023). For instance, economic indicators like
employment data are often subject to revisions, and using revised data in a backtest without accounting for when
those revisions were available can introduce look-ahead bias (Analyzing Alpha, 2023).
Economic indicators differ from general news events in ways that mitigate the risk of look-ahead bias. Unlike
event-specific news, such as “Brexit Referendum” or “Federal Reserve Emergency Rate Cut,” which may have names
tied to historical moments, economic indicators like Nonfarm Payrolls or Consumer Price Index are announced regularly
with consistent names. This consistency ensures that the indicator name does not expose historical context or specific
timing, reducing the likelihood that the model could infer future outcomes based on the event name alone (Analyzing
Alpha, 2023). By leveraging this property, our methodology minimizes the risk of inadvertently introducing temporal
cues into the backtest.
To further address look-ahead bias, our methodology ensures that the Large Language Model (LLM) receives
the actual value of the economic indicator only after its historical release time, using unrevised (initial) values from
the MQL5 terminal database. This approach aligns with best practices for backtesting news-based strategies, which
emphasize the importance of using only the data that was available at the time of the trading decision (Analyzing
Alpha, 2023). By relying on initial estimates rather than revised figures, we prevent the introduction of look-ahead
bias that could arise from using information that was not known at the time.
Additionally, our strategy does not provide the LLM with specific dates for the economic events. Instead, it feeds
the LLM only with the economic indicator’s name, previous value, forecasted value, and actual value. This omission
of temporal context ensures that the LLM processes the data without information that could allow it to correlate
the event with future market movements or other historical events, further minimizing the risk of look-ahead bias.
Since the LLM is not exposed to any information that would clearly reveal the exact timing or future outcomes of the
event, it is unlikely to inadvertently incorporate look-ahead bias into its predictions. The LLM’s task is to infer buying
confidence based solely on the provided data points, which are consistent with what was available at the time of the
event (MQL5 Community, 2024).
This approach is supported by the principle that backtesting should simulate real-world conditions as closely as
possible, using only the information that would have been available to traders at the time (MQL5 Community, 2024).
By using unrevised data, omitting dates, and leveraging the consistent naming of economic indicators, our methodology

ensures a more accurate representation of how the strategy would have performed under historical market conditions.

Historical economic indicator data were obtained natively from the MQL5 Terminal. These data were used to replicate

Storing LLM responses in a CSV file facilitates efficient cross-referencing with historical trade data, mimicking

5.2
Investigating LLM Stability and Interpretability
5.2.1
Background Knowledge
This subsection evaluates the reliability and interpretability of the Large Language Model (LLM) integration framework
proposed in this study, addressing concerns related to hallucination and the blackbox nature of LLM-generated outputs.
Hallucination, the generation of plausible but incorrect confidence scores, poses a risk to trading decisions, while the
opaque decision-making process of LLMs undermines trust and validation. To assess the framework’s robustness, we
analyze the distribution patterns of LLM confidence scores and their impact on profitability through backtesting. By
comparing performance across identical prompts, similar prompts, and varying confidence score thresholds, this study
seeks to elucidate the consistency and competitive advantage of the LLM-integrated trading strategy.
Recent studies highlight the transformative potential of large language models (LLMs) in generating trading signals
from financial texts, such as news and reports, while also noting significant challenges (Li&Zhang, 2024). LLMs are
prone to hallucination, producing factually inaccurate outputs that can mislead trading strategies (Kang&Sarmah,
2023). To mitigate this, techniques like retrieval-augmented generation, which grounds LLM outputs in verified data,
have shown promise (Sarmah, 2023). The blackbox nature of LLMs further complicates their adoption, as their internal
reasoning remains difficult to interpret (Biancotti, 2023). Approaches such as knowledge distillation, which transfers
LLM knowledge to smaller, more transparent models, have been proposed to enhance interpretability (Chen, 2024).
Despite these hurdles, well-calibrated LLMs have demonstrated superior performance in financial tasks, including stock
selection, when paired with robust mitigation strategies (Zhang, 2024).

Conclusion
This paper presents a robust framework for backtesting and live trading a volatility-based intraday strategy applied to
the USTEC CFD, leveraging economic indicators and integrating a large language model (LLM) as a signal filter to
systematically enhance trade profitability. Through extensive analysis conducted over the period from January 1, 2018,
to May 1, 2025, encompassing more than 1,700 samples, the backbone strategy demonstrates exceptional performance,
achieving a cumulative return of 730% and an annualized growth rate of 33% (net of trading fees). The incorporation
of the LLM filter further augments profitability, contributing an additional 5% to the annual return while improving
key performance metrics.
The proposed strategy significantly outperforms the benchmark buy-and-hold approach for USTEC, maintaining
a substantial alpha exceeding 0.5. This outperformance highlights the potential advantage of modeling volatility in
the context of non-technical informational announcements and employing LLMs for advanced information analysis.
By systematically filtering signals with LLMs, the framework showcases their ability to enhance traditional trading
strategies, offering a pioneering method for leveraging unstructured data in financial markets.
Additionally, this study addresses critical methodological considerations, including the risk of look-ahead bias and
the stability of LLM outputs in historical backtesting. The findings indicate that prompt design plays a pivotal role
in shaping the LLM’s analytical focus, leading to variations in filtering effects even when tasks and inputs remain
constant. However, reapplying identical prompts in backtests produces largely consistent results, with only minor
11

deviations, suggesting that the LLM’s filtering capability is reliable and not entirely stochastic. This stability bolsters
the framework’s credibility for both historical evaluation and prospective live trading applications.
Looking ahead, future research could explore the intricacies of prompt engineering by quantitatively assessing
how specific wording impacts LLM performance. Moreover, investigating multi-model approaches, such as RetrievalAugmented Generation (RAG), could refine the LLM’s decision-making precision for this trading context. Tailoring
LLMs more closely to financial applications through such advancements may unlock further potential for AI-driven
strategies in quantitative finance.
In conclusion, this paper delivers a comprehensive framework that integrates LLMs into a volatility-based trading
strategy, demonstrating both profitability and robustness. The results affirm the strategy’s efficacy and lay a foundation
for continued exploration of artificial intelligence’s transformative role in financial decision-making.

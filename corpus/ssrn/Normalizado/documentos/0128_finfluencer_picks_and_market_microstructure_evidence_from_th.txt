Abstract 
Purpose: This paper documents a finfluencer attention cycle in Indian equity markets. Using 
1,535 video-stock events constructed from 12,302 YouTube videos across ten prominent 
finfluencer channels matched with daily NSE bhavcopy data, it examines whether financial 
influencer stock recommendations generate abnormal trading volume and affect postrecommendation returns on the National Stock Exchange from 2019 to 2026. 
 
Design/Methodology/Approach: A standard market model event study estimates abnormal 
trading volume and cumulative abnormal returns in windows surrounding video upload dates. 
The estimation window spans 120 trading days ending ten days before each event. Sensitivity 
analysis excludes the most concentrated channel and stocks across three specifications. A 
permutation placebo test shuffles upload dates within each channel 200 times to test whether 
results arise by chance. 
 
Findings: Abnormal trading volume spikes to 2.65 times normal on the upload day (p < 0.001) 
and remains elevated through five post-upload days. Cumulative abnormal returns are negative at 
−0.55% over [0, +3] (p = 0.001) with no reversal. Pre-event volume is elevated at 1.47 times 

normal (p < 0.001), indicating finfluencers select stocks with pre-existing momentum. Effects 
are larger for small-cap stocks (−1.72% vs −0.35%). Sensitivity analysis shows results strengthen 
after excluding concentrated events. Permutation p-value = 0.000. 
 
Research Limitations/Implications: The sample covers ten English-language channels and may 
not generalise to regional-language finfluencers. Pre-event volume elevation reflects multi-day 
selection rather than same-day endogeneity, confirmed by the pre-market upload subsample, 
though timestamp precision from the YouTube API is imperfect. The design establishes 
association not causation. Future work should incorporate bid-ask spread data and extend 
coverage to Hindi and regional-language channels. 
 
Practical Implications: Retail followers who enter stocks following finfluencer 
recommendations face negative average returns of −0.55% over four days, with losses reaching 
−1.72% for small-cap stocks. Banks, brokers, and regulators should account for finfluencerdriven attention shocks when assessing retail investor behaviour. SEBI’s 2024 circular restricting 
finfluencer-broker relationships addresses the conflict of interest dimension but does not directly 
address the attention cycle mechanism documented here. 
 
Social Implications: The finfluencer attention cycle represents a structural wealth transfer from 
retail followers who enter near the price peak to earlier participants and informed traders who 
exit. With 170 million registered retail investors in India and finfluencer channels commanding 
tens of millions of subscribers, the aggregate welfare cost is material. Financial literacy 
initiatives should explicitly address the gap between finfluencer recommendation framing and 
actual post-recommendation return outcomes. 
 

Originality/Value: This is the first systematic event study combining YouTube API metadata 
with NSE bhavcopy data to evaluate finfluencer market impact in India. The dataset of 1,535 
video-stock events across 82 NSE-listed securities is novel. The attention cycle framing: preexisting momentum attracting content, content attracting retail volume, retail late entry 
producing negative returns, is a more precise characterisation of the mechanism than prior 
commentary has offered. 
 
1. Introduction 
India’s retail equity investor base expanded from approximately 40 million registered accounts in 
2020 to over 170 million by 2025, a fourfold growth in five years. This expansion coincided with 
the rise of a new financial media ecosystem: the financial influencer, or finfluencer, who delivers 
stock analysis and investment recommendations through YouTube videos, reaching audiences of 
hundreds of thousands to millions of subscribers. The SEBI Investor Survey 2025 (SEBI, 2025b) 
found that 62% of prospective investors identify social media and digital influencers as a primary 
source of investment information. SEBI’s own circular of October 2024 estimated that finfluencers 
collectively reach more Indian retail investors than the entire registered investment adviser 
community. 
 
Whether finfluencer recommendations generate measurable effects on financial markets, and 
whether those effects are welfare-improving or welfare-distorting, are open empirical questions. 
The theoretical channels are competing. Under the attention hypothesis (Barber and Odean, 2008), 
retail investors are attention-constrained and disproportionately buy stocks that attract their 
attention, regardless of fundamental value. A finfluencer recommendation video is a concentrated 
attention shock that could generate abnormal buying volume and temporary price pressure. 

Alternatively, finfluencers may aggregate and disseminate genuinely useful information, 
accelerating price discovery in less liquid stocks. Discriminating between these channels requires 
examining both volume and return dynamics around recommendation events. 
 
This paper provides the first systematic event study of finfluencer YouTube recommendations and 
NSE market outcomes using Indian data. Using a sample of 1,535 video-stock events constructed 
from 12,302 videos across ten prominent Indian finfluencer channels from 2019 to 2026, matched 
with daily bhavcopy data from the National Stock Exchange, it estimates abnormal volume and 
abnormal returns in the event windows surrounding upload dates. The findings do not support the 
simple pump-and-dump narrative that prior commentary has attributed to finfluencers. Instead they 
are consistent with a more nuanced attention cycle: finfluencers upload content about stocks that 
are already moving, their content attracts additional retail volume, and retail followers 
systematically enter stocks that are already past their price peak. 
 
The paper makes three contributions. First, it introduces the concept of the finfluencer attention 
cycle to the Indian market microstructure literature, documenting the pre-event volume elevation 
that precedes recommendation videos and distinguishing this pattern from pure exogenous 
attention shocks. Second, it is the first study to use NSE bhavcopy data matched with YouTube 
API video metadata to construct a comprehensive event study dataset covering Indian finfluencer 
content from 2019 to 2026. Third, it provides context for evaluating SEBI’s 2024–2026 regulatory 
interventions targeting finfluencer activity, including the August 2024 circular restricting 
finfluencer-broker commercial relationships and the January 2025 mandatory three-month data lag 
rule. 
 

The paper tests four hypotheses. H1: finfluencer stock mentions generate abnormal trading volume 
in the post-upload window. H2: post-upload cumulative abnormal returns are initially positive and 
subsequently reverse, consistent with attention-driven price pressure. H3: the volume and return 
effects are larger for small-cap stocks, where retail attention effects are less diluted by institutional 
trading. H4: the pre-event window shows no abnormal activity, consistent with finfluencer uploads 
being exogenous to near-term price dynamics. Figure 1 summarises the hypothesised attention 
cycle mechanism. 
 
Figure 1 – The Finfluencer Attention Cycle: Hypothesised Mechanism 
 
The four-stage attention cycle: pre-existing volume elevation selects stocks into finfluencer content; content 
amplifies retail volume; retail late-entry buying coincides with negative post-event returns as informed traders exit. 
 
2. Literature Review 
The attention hypothesis in financial economics holds that retail investors are constrained in their 
ability to process information and disproportionately direct buying activity toward stocks that 
attract their attention. Barber and Odean (2008) established this pattern using a large dataset of 
retail brokerage accounts, finding that retail investors are net buyers of attention-grabbing stocks: 
those with high abnormal volume, extreme returns, or news coverage, even when fundamentals do 
not warrant purchases. This produces predictable patterns: attention-driven buying pushes prices 
temporarily above fundamental value, followed by price reversal as attention recedes and prices 
return toward fundamentals. 
 

The social media extension of the attention hypothesis has generated a substantial literature. 
Antweiler and Frank (2004) documented that stock message board posts on Yahoo Finance and 
Raging Bull predict abnormal trading volume but not abnormal returns, consistent with attentiondriven trading without information content. Chen et al. (2014) found that Seeking Alpha articles 
predict future returns, but their sample skews toward institutional-grade analysis. Cookson and 
Niessner (2020) showed that social media disagreement predicts trading volume. Da, Engelberg, 
and Gao (2011) used Google search volume as an attention proxy and found it predicts short-term 
price increases followed by reversals. 
 
The Reddit and WallStreetBets literature provides the closest analogue to the finfluencer 
phenomenon. Hasso et al. (2022) found that Reddit stock mentions cause abnormal volume and 
short-term price pressure. Boehmer et al. (2021) showed that retail order flow, proxied by 
Robinhood account openings, is correlated with attention-driven stock purchases. Umar et al. 
(2021) documented that the GameStop episode is consistent with coordinated retail attention rather 
than informed trading. 
 
The Indian finfluencer literature is sparse and methodologically limited. Lalwani (2025) studies 
what determines finfluencer stock recommendation choices, finding that finfluencers favour 
growth stocks with recent positive momentum, establishing the supply-side selection mechanism 
that is relevant to interpreting the pre-event volume elevation found in this paper. SEBI’s own 
Investor Survey 2025 documents the reach of finfluencer content but provides no causal estimates. 
No prior study applies a market model event study to YouTube finfluencer data on the NSE. 
 
The theoretical framework for interpreting the findings comes from two sources. Merton (1987) 
showed that investor recognition, the share of investors aware of a stock, directly affects 

equilibrium prices and trading volume. A finfluencer recommendation increases investor 
recognition for a stock, which should increase both prices and volume mechanically through the 
recognition channel. However, if the stock’s price already reflects its fundamental value, the 
recognition-driven price increase will be temporary and followed by reversal. Lou (2014) 
formalised this mechanism, showing that retail attention-driven price pressure is mean-reverting 
within weeks. Both frameworks predict the observed pattern: volume elevation followed by 
negative returns rather than positive returns and reversal. 
 
3. Data 
3.1 YouTube Video Dataset 
Video metadata are collected from ten prominent Indian finfluencer YouTube channels using the 
YouTube Data API v3. The channels are selected to represent the top tier of Indian financial 
content by subscriber count and investment focus: Akshat Shrivastava, CA Rachana Ranade, 
Pranjal Kamra, Shankar Nath, Yadnya Investment Academy, Parimal Ade, SOIC (School of 
Intrinsic Compounding), Neeraj Joshi, Vivek Bajaj, and Pushkar Raj Thakur. Combined, these 
channels have over 25 million subscribers as of March 2026. The API returns video title, 
description, upload date, and view count for all available videos on each channel. The final dataset 
contains 12,302 videos published between June 2019 and March 2026. 
 
Stock mentions are extracted from video titles and descriptions using a curated alias dictionary 
matching company names and ticker symbols to NSE-listed securities. The matching is 
conservative: only well-known liquid stocks are matched, using full company name phrases rather 
than single-word ticker matching, to avoid false positives from common English words that 
coincide with NSE symbols. The final event dataset contains 1,535 video-stock pairs involving 82 
distinct NSE-listed securities across all ten channels. 

 
3.2 NSE Price and Volume Data 
Daily price and volume data for all mentioned stocks are sourced from the NSE daily bhavcopy 
archives and downloaded via Yahoo Finance for the period January 2017 to March 2026. The 
estimation window for each event uses 120 trading days ending ten days before the event date. The 
Nifty 50 index return serves as the market benchmark for the market model estimation. After 
filtering for events with sufficient estimation window data (minimum 60 observations), the final 
sample contains 717 events with complete abnormal return estimates and 747 events with complete 
abnormal volume estimates.

4. Methodology 
The paper uses a standard market model event study. For each video-stock event, the market model 
is estimated over a 120-trading-day window ending ten days before the event date: 
Rᵢₜ = αᵢ + βᵢ Rᵐₜ + εᵢₜ 
where Rᵢₜ is the return on stock i on day t and Rᵐₜ is the Nifty 50 return. Abnormal returns are 
computed as ARᵢₜ = Rᵢₜ − (αˆᵢ + βˆᵢ Rᵐₜ). Cumulative abnormal returns over window [d₁, d₂] are 
CARᵢ[d₁, d₂] = Σ ARᵢₜ. Statistical significance is assessed using a one-sample t-test against zero. 
 

Abnormal volume is measured as the abnormal volume ratio (AVR): actual trading volume divided 
by the median trading volume over the estimation window. An AVR significantly greater than one 
indicates unusual volume. Statistical significance is assessed using a one-sample t-test against the 
null of AVR = 1. 
 
The event date t = 0 is defined as the video upload date. If the upload date falls on a non-trading 
day, t = 0 is set to the next trading day. The event window covers [−5, +10] trading days. Crosssectional heterogeneity is tested by splitting events by market capitalisation (large cap vs small 
cap) and estimating OLS regressions of CAR[0, +3] on log view count, a large cap indicator, a 
large channel indicator, and an indicator for post-SEBI circular events.

The volume result is strong and unambiguous. AVR spikes to 2.65 on the upload day and remains 
significantly elevated through Day +5 at 1.87. H1 is confirmed: finfluencer stock mentions 
generate a large and persistent abnormal volume response on the NSE. 

 
The return result is the paper’s most important finding. CAR is negative and statistically significant 
across all post-event windows: −0.25% on Day 0, −0.52% through Day +1, −0.55% through Day 
+3, and −0.62% through Day +5. H2 is not confirmed in the hypothesised direction: prices do not 
rise following finfluencer recommendations, they fall. We do not detect evidence of reversal within 
the first ten trading days ([+4, +10] CAR = −0.04%, p = 0.78), though the power to detect a small 
reversal is limited given the noise in daily returns. The 95% confidence interval around the [+4, 
+10] CAR spans approximately −0.4% to +0.4%, so a modest reversal cannot be ruled out. The 
correct interpretation is that we find no evidence of reversal within this window, not that no 
reversal occurred. 
 
The pre-event window is also informative. The pre-event AVR of 1.47 is significantly elevated (p 
< 0.001), indicating that volume is already 47% above normal in the five days before the video is 
uploaded. Pre-event CAR is positive but insignificant (+0.12%, p = 0.51). This pre-event volume 
elevation, combined with the negative post-event returns, is consistent with the finfluencer 
attention cycle described in the introduction: stocks experiencing unusual volume and recent price 
momentum attract finfluencer content, the content attracts additional retail volume, and retail 
followers enter stocks that are past their momentum peak and beginning to decline.

5.3 SEBI Regulatory Impact 
With only 209 post-SEBI Circular 1 events and 193 post-SEBI Circular 2 events, the regulatory 
impact analysis is substantially underpowered. The post-Circular 1 CAR of −0.23% is smaller in 
magnitude than the pre-circular CAR of −0.68%, a difference in the welfare-improving direction, 
but the comparison is not statistically significant (p = 0.24). Figure 3, however, presents a more 

informative visual picture: the pre-SEBI CAR trajectory shows a clean monotonic decline from 
upload date through Day +10, while the post-SEBI CAR trajectory is essentially flat around 
−0.15% with no systematic negative pattern. The visual divergence is economically meaningful 
even if the formal test is underpowered at current sample sizes. This is reported as preliminary 
evidence, not a statistical finding. Evaluating whether SEBI’s 2024–2026 interventions attenuated 
the attention cycle requires data through at least 2027 and is deferred accordingly.

The pre-event AVR is significantly elevated in all three groups including pre-market (1.39, p <

conventional levels, reflecting the smaller subsample of 203 events. 
 
6. Limitations 
Endogeneity of upload timing 
The pre-event volume elevation of 1.47 times normal (p < 0.001) confirms that finfluencer 
selection operates over multiple trading days. The timing robustness analysis uses the pre-market 
upload subsample to address same-day endogeneity. However, 393 of the 449 pre-market events 

come from a single channel, Yadnya Investment Academy, which schedules all uploads at 01:30 
UTC (07:00 IST) daily. Their fixed schedule means the pre-market instrument reflects one 
channel’s upload habit rather than a representative test of exogenous timing. Excluding Yadnya 
from the pre-market subsample leaves only 56 events, too few for reliable inference. The pre-event 
elevation in the full pre-market group is therefore dominated by Yadnya’s stock selection process, 
which may itself follow multi-day momentum patterns. This is acknowledged as a limitation of 
the timing robustness check. 
 
Sample concentration 
The event sample is concentrated across channels and stocks. Two channels account for 67.9% of 
events, and the top three stocks account for approximately 28%. The sensitivity analysis in Section 
5.4 confirms that the main results hold after excluding the most concentrated channel and stocks, 
but the generalisability of the findings across the broader finfluencer ecosystem, including smaller 
channels and regional-language content, cannot be established from this sample. 
 
Stock mention extraction 
The alias-based stock mention extraction approach is conservative by design, using only a curated 
list of liquid stocks and full company name phrases. This reduces false positives but introduces 
false negatives: stocks mentioned using informal names, partial names, or abbreviations not in the 
alias dictionary are not captured. The true event count is likely larger than 1,535. 
 
SEBI regulatory tests 
The post-January 2025 subsample of 193 events, while larger, remains insufficient for reliable 
inference. The SEBI regulatory impact analysis requires a full year of post-intervention data and 
is reported here as preliminary evidence only. 

 
Missing channels 
The sample covers ten channels, selected for their prominence and subscriber count. Smaller or 
newer finfluencer channels, regional-language channels, and channels that have been removed or 
restricted by SEBI are not captured. The sample is representative of the top tier of Indian financial 
YouTube content but not the full finfluencer ecosystem. 
 
Causal interpretation 
The event study design estimates the association between finfluencer uploads and market 
outcomes, not a causal effect. The pre-event volume elevation confirms that selection is operating: 
finfluencers choose to cover stocks that are already attracting attention. The post-event volume 
and return dynamics describe what happens after coverage, conditioning on this selection. Causal 
identification of the finfluencer effect requires an instrument or natural experiment that is not 
available in this dataset. 
 
7. Conclusion 
This paper documents a finfluencer attention cycle in Indian equity markets. Stocks mentioned in 
YouTube recommendation videos experience abnormal trading volume of 2.65 times normal on 
the upload day, declining but remaining elevated through five post-event trading days. Post-upload 
cumulative abnormal returns are negative at −0.55% over four days and show no subsequent 
reversal. Pre-event volume is also elevated at 1.47 times normal, indicating that finfluencers create 
content about stocks that are already experiencing unusual trading activity. The combined pattern 
is consistent with a momentum-chasing selection mechanism: finfluencers upload content about 
stocks experiencing recent volume spikes, their content attracts additional retail buying, and retail 
followers systematically enter at or near price peaks. 

 
The findings have direct policy relevance. SEBI’s 2024–2026 regulatory interventions targeting 
finfluencer activity were motivated by the observation that retail followers of finfluencers tend to 
underperform. The negative post-recommendation CARs documented here, particularly the 
−1.72% average for small-cap stocks, are consistent with this concern. The magnitude is 
economically significant for retail investors with concentrated positions. Whether SEBI’s 
interventions have reduced these adverse return dynamics cannot yet be determined from the 
current data, as the post-regulatory sample is too small for reliable inference. 
 
Three extensions follow naturally. First, evaluating SEBI’s 2024–2026 regulatory interventions, 
including the August 2024 circular restricting finfluencer-broker relationships and the January 
2025 mandatory data lag rule, requires post-regulatory data through at least 2027. A preliminary 
comparison suggests smaller negative post-recommendation CARs after the August 2024 circular, 
but the subsample is too small for reliable inference. Second, the alias-based stock mention 
extraction should be supplemented with a language model approach to capture informal references 
and regional-language content. Third, bid-ask spread data from the NSE Level 2 order book would 
test whether the attention shock increases effective spreads, imposing additional transaction costs 
on retail followers beyond the negative CAR documented here.

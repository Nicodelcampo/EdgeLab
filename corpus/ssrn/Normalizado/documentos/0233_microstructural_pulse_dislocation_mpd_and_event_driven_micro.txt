Abstract 
This paper presents evidence that the first second after a scheduled economic announcement 
or earnings release is not an unstructured spike in volatility, but a repeatable, segmented 
process of price adjustment. Using one-second event data and broker-verified live trading 
records in EUR/USD, NZD/USD, GBP/USD and selected equities, I document a newly observed 
phenomenon, Microstructural Pulse Dislocation (MPD): a sequence of directional price 
“pulses” that unfolds within the first second following information arrival. I further describe 
Event-Driven Microstructure Collapse (EMC), the rapid evaporation and unstable reformation 
of order-book depth that accompanies and amplifies these pulses. 
Across multiple high-impact releases—including U.S. Nonfarm Payrolls (NFP), U.S. Consumer 
Price Index (CPI), FOMC rate announcements, major currency events, and post-earnings 
volatility waves—the same architecture appears: a dominant first pulse, segmented follow-on 
pulses, and depth collapse that allows price to traverse large distances through a thinned book. 
A simple pre-positioned, hedged structure applied to these events generated consistent 
profitability before MPD and EMC were named or modeled, suggesting that the edge arises 
from structural features rather than parameter tuning or privileged data. 
The results challenge the assumption that the first second is too chaotic to analyze and instead 
treat it as a distinct microstructural regime. This paper defines MPD and EMC, illustrates them 
using six representative events, and argues that they form the foundation of a broader 
event-microstructure domain in which the first second becomes an object of independent 
scientific and practical interest.

1. Introduction 
Scheduled macroeconomic releases and corporate earnings announcements concentrate 
information into predictable timestamps. For decades, financial economists and practitioners 
have studied event-driven returns, volatility and liquidity, typically using horizons measured in 
minutes, hours or days. Even high-frequency studies tend to focus on one-minute or five-minute 
windows and on cumulative effects rather than on what happens at the exact instant when new 
information arrives. 
In that context, the first second after the event has been treated as either unobservable or 
irreducibly noisy. The prevailing implicit view is that whatever happens in that interval belongs to 
“the machines” and is dominated by latency races, proprietary feeds and microsecond 
advantage, not by structure that can be modeled at human-visible resolution. 
This paper takes the opposite view. Drawing on thousands of manually observed one-second 
charts and a sequence of live, broker-verified trades executed through a retail terminal, I argue 
that the first second is architectural. Prices do not simply jump; they move in a segmented 
sequence of directional thrusts. Liquidity does not vaguely “dry up”; it collapses and reforms in a 
patterned way. Algorithms do not react as a blur; they fire in layers, leaving a visible trace in the 
microstructure. 
I introduce two central concepts: 
●​ Microstructural Pulse Dislocation (MPD):​
 A segmented sequence of directional price pulses within the first second after a 
scheduled information shock, reflecting the layered activation of news-reading, execution 
and risk-management algorithms.​
 
●​ Event-Driven Microstructure Collapse (EMC):​
 A rapid, event-local collapse and reformation of top-of-book depth and spreads, through 
which price can travel long distances with relatively little volume.​
 
These phenomena were discovered empirically while trading only two event types—U.S. NFP 
and U.S. CPI—during a six-month proof-of-concept period in 2024. A simple, symmetric 
long-short hedge was opened before each release and closed via stops and targets during the 
first seconds after the event. The trades produced consistent net profits before any formal 
model existed. Only after the fact were one-second charts, depth behavior and event-by-event 
sequences analyzed, revealing a stable architecture behind the results. 
The contribution of this paper is threefold: 

1.​ It documents that the first second after scheduled releases exhibits a repeatable pulse 
structure (MPD) rather than pure randomness.​
 
2.​ It characterizes the associated depth dynamics (EMC) and shows how they enable large 
dislocations.​
 
3.​ It connects these structures to real, broker-verified profits obtained with a simple, 
non-optimized hedged strategy, demonstrating that the architecture is strong enough to 
be monetized even without institutional infrastructure.​
 
A companion project extends this work into a broader Pulse Atlas and a formal metric system 
(the Split Archetype Signature, SAS, framework). Here, the focus is on the core discovery: what 
the first second looks like, how it behaves across events, and why it matters. 
 
2. Literature Context and the First-Second Gap 
The event-study literature in finance has documented that macroeconomic announcements and 
earnings releases produce jumps in prices and volatility, as well as post-event drifts and 
adjustments. High-frequency extensions show that these effects are concentrated in short 
intervals around the event, and microstructure studies describe how spreads, depth and order 
submission respond to news and volatility. 
However, even in high-frequency settings, the first second is usually treated as: 
●​ a single, instantaneous jump, or​
 
●​ a noisy transition region between pre- and post-event regimes.​
 
Studies of order-book resiliency and “fleeting liquidity” show that limit orders tend to recede 
before large shocks and then rebuild, but they typically aggregate behavior over longer windows 
and do not attempt to resolve the internal structure of the immediate reaction. Research on 
high-frequency trading and latency arbitrage emphasizes who reacts first and how information 
propagates across venues and instruments, but not the shape of the first response once it 
starts. 
This leaves a clear gap: 
●​ The timing of the event is well studied.​
 
●​ The longer-horizon response is well studied.​
 

●​ The internal geometry of the first second is largely unmodeled.​
 
The present paper is an attempt to fill that gap by treating the first second as an object of 
independent inquiry, using one-second resolution that is available even on retail platforms.

33.3 Event-Driven Microstructure Collapse (EMC) 
EMC captures what happens to the order book in and immediately after Pulse #1: 
●​ Top-of-book depth on the dominant side (bid or ask) collapses to a fraction of its 
pre-event value.​
 
●​ Spreads widen sharply, often by several multiples of the baseline.​
 
●​ Price traverses multiple price levels with relatively few trades, indicating a temporary 
liquidity vacuum.​
 
●​ Depth then reappears unevenly, often skewed toward the direction of the initial move.​

Where full depth data are unavailable, EMC can still be inferred from: 
●​ abnormally large one-second candles,​
 
●​ gapping behavior,​
 
●​ sharp, fast reversals during the first few seconds, and​
 
●​ slippage patterns in executed trades.​
 
MPD describes the shape of the price response. EMC describes the state of the order book 
that makes such a response possible. 
 
4. Data and Methodology 

4.1 Discovery Protocol: NFP and CPI First 
The initial discovery period was intentionally narrow. From May through Oct 2024, the focus was 
on just two types of U.S. releases in EUR/USD: 
●​ Nonfarm Payrolls (NFP)​
 
●​ Consumer Price Index (CPI)​
 
These were chosen because: 
●​ they are among the most impactful recurring macro events;​
 
●​ they occur at fixed, easily planned times; and​
 
●​ they reliably generate high participation from algorithms and institutions.​
 
During this period, other high-impact releases (e.g., central bank decisions, JOLTS, ISM, CAD 
and NZD employment) were often observed but not always traded, due to time and capacity 
constraints. 
The logic was simple: if a structural pattern exists in event-driven microstructure, it should reveal 
itself first in these “core” FX events. 
4.2 Instruments and Platforms 
The core analysis uses: 
●​ FX pairs:​
 EUR/USD (NFP, CPI, FOMC),​
 GBP/USD (U.K. CPI),​
 NZD/USD (macro release in August 2025).​
 
●​ Equities:​
 Selected large-cap earnings events (e.g., ADBE, ULTA, DOCU, ORCL) as cross-asset 
confirmation.​
 
Price and one-second bar data were obtained from a combination of retail broker feeds and 
charting platforms used in live trading. Broker statements provide time-stamped entries, exits, 
position sizes and P&L. 
4.3 Hedged Event Strategy 

The live trades that motivated this research used a simple symmetric hedge: 
1.​ Pre-event positioning:​
 Before t0t_0t0​, open equal-sized long and short positions in the target pair (e.g., 
EUR/USD), creating a notional straddle.​
 
2.​ Stops and targets:​
 Place stops and take-profit levels such that a clean, one-directional Pulse #1 is 
expected to:​
 
○​ stop out one leg at a controlled loss, and​
 
○​ push the other leg far enough to produce a larger gain.​
 
3.​ No optimization:​
 Parameters (position size, stop distance, target distance) were not exhaustively tuned 
across events; the intent was to test whether any robust structure existed at all, not to 
fine-tune a model.​
 
The question is whether MPD and EMC are strong enough features of the event microstructure 
that this naive, symmetric configuration can yield repeatable, broker-verified profits. 
 
5. Empirical Evidence: Six Representative Events 
This section presents six events that illustrate the MPD/EMC architecture across assets and 
event types. For each, I provide a brief summary, then embed the relevant figure. 
 
5.1 Nonfarm Payrolls: Stop-Run and True Pulse 
Event: U.S. NFP (EUR/USD), August 2025​
 Structure: Single account (no hedge), learning to distinguish pre-release stop-runs from the 
true MPD pulse. 
In the minutes leading up to the NFP release, EUR/USD trades in a relatively stable range. 
Then, shortly before t0t_0t0​, the market produces a sharp, downward sweep. Many traders 
(including earlier versions of myself) experience this as a “fake move” that takes out stops and 
creates emotional confusion. 
Structurally, however, this pre-release move lacks the hallmarks of MPD: 

●​ it does not align exactly with the release timestamp,​
 
●​ it lacks clean segmentation into pulses, and​
 
●​ the order-book behavior is more consistent with a deliberate thin-book stop-run than with 
true news digestion.​
 
At t0t_0t0​, a powerful upward MPD Pulse #1 appears. Recognizing that the earlier down-move 
was a stop-run rather than the real event, I traded the long side into the actual post-release 
pulse and captured the main move. 
 
 
This event is important historically because it shows: 
●​ how exposure to earlier whipsaws in 2024 trained pattern recognition;​
 
●​ how MPD structure can be used to avoid pre-release traps; and​
 
●​ how even without a hedge, understanding the architecture can tilt decisions toward the 
correct side of the move.​
 
 
5.2 FOMC 14:00 — Pulse, Collapse and Rebound 

Event: FOMC policy announcement, EUR/USD, 14:00:00 ET​
 Structure: Classic MPD plus EMC. 
At the exact timestamp of the FOMC announcement, the first one-second bar prints a large 
downward candle, reflecting ultra-fast algos processing the headline policy text. This is MPD 
Pulse #1: range expansion, clear direction and immediate repricing. 
Immediately afterward, liquidity collapses. The next one-second bars show a steep overshoot 
and then a violent reversal as the order book temporarily cannot support normal trading. Depth 
vanishes and reappears at new levels, while spreads widen sharply—textbook EMC behavior. 
A second, upward MPD pulse then forms as slower-tier algorithms digest forward guidance, 
press conference hints, and cross-asset relationships. Price settles into a post-event equilibrium 
several seconds later, once EMC resolves and depth returns. 
 
 
For the hedged strategy, this pattern is ideal: one leg is taken out in the first thrust, and the 
surviving leg rides the continuation through EMC into the new equilibrium zone. 
 
5.3 GBP CPI — Pre-Release Whipsaw versus True MPD Pulse 
Event: U.K. CPI release, GBP/USD, August 20, 2025​
 Structure: Pre-release “flicker,” then a clean MPD pulse followed by EMC. 

Immediately before the CPI timestamp, the chart shows a small, erratic red candle. This is not 
part of the MPD sequence; it reflects thinning liquidity and spread widening as market-makers 
pull and adjust quotes ahead of the data. It is a fragility signal, not a true pulse. 
At t0t_0t0​, a large green one-second bar prints: the real MPD Pulse #1. The bar’s 
geometry—long body, minimal wick, abrupt range expansion—signals that the market has just 
repriced GBP/USD based on the actual CPI surprise. 
The next second shows a downward red candle as EMC takes hold. Depth evaporates, spreads 
widen, and price travels through a shallow book, producing a temporary vacuum effect. 
Subsequent one-second bars alternate in color with reduced but still elevated range as different 
subclasses of algorithms digest core versus headline inflation, revisions and cross-asset 
linkages. 
 
 
This event is a useful template for distinguishing: 
●​ pre-event microstructure noise (small, unstructured flickers) from​
 
●​ event-time structure (MPD Pulse #1 + EMC).​
 
 
5.4 NZD Macro Event — Canonical MPD Architecture 

Event: NZD macro release (e.g., employment or GDP), NZD/USD, August 2025​
 Structure: Extremely clean MPD plus collapse cascade. 
NZD/USD trades quietly in the seconds before the release. At t0t_0t0​, a near-vertical 
one-second bar appears, launching the price into a new region in a single move. This bar is a 
near-perfect MPD Pulse #1: strong, directional, with minimal counter-movement. 
Immediately afterward, the chart shows a sequence of large red candles as EMC drives a 
collapse cascade. Top-of-book depth evaporates, spreads widen, and price steps down through 
thin liquidity, forming a stair-step descent. After several seconds, pulse amplitude decays and a 
new equilibrium forms far from the pre-event level. 
 
This event is valuable because it displays the entire architecture—equilibrium, Pulse #1, EMC 
cascade, digestion, stabilization—in a relatively uncluttered way, making MPD and EMC visually 
obvious even to non-specialists. 
 
5.5 ULTA Earnings — Earnings-Side MPD and EMC 
Event: ULTA earnings release​
 Structure: Single-stock earnings with MPD Pulse #1 and EMC similar to FX. 
At the earnings timestamp, ULTA prints a large one-second green candle as headline results hit 
the feed and ultra-fast news algos respond. This is MPD Pulse #1: a directional, high-velocity 
repricing. Immediately afterward, a sharp red one-second bar appears as the order book 

collapses. Top-of-book depth disappears, quotes jump, and price slices through shallow 
levels—a clear EMC episode. Subsequent pulses alternate direction as different earnings 
components (guidance, margins, forward commentary) are digested by slower-tier algorithms 
and human participants. 
 
 
The important point is that the same MPD + EMC architecture documented in NFP, CPI and 
FOMC events appears in a single-stock earnings context. The physics of the first second is 
cross-asset. 
 
5.6 Adobe / DocuSign / Oracle — Variants of Earnings and EMC 
The broader earnings sample shows consistent patterns across different names: 
●​ Adobe (ADBE) shows a strong MPD Pulse #1 followed by an EMC-driven overshoot 
and a secondary pulse as guidance and forward commentary are integrated. 

 
●​ DocuSign (DOCU) displays an explosive initial green candle followed by a collapse and 
oscillating pulses, matching the hierarchical digestion sequence seen in macro events. 
​
 
●​ Oracle (ORCL) highlights repeated EMC cycles: depth collapses and reforms several 
times, generating multiple opportunities for collapse-driven scalps rather than a single 
dominant pulse. 

​
 
 
These cases reinforce the claim that MPD and EMC are not idiosyncratic to FX. They are 
features of event-driven electronic markets more generally. 
 
6. Profitability and Structural Edge 
One reason this architecture deserves attention is that it has already produced real, 
broker-verified profits under real-time conditions. 
During the 2024 proof-of-concept period: 
●​ A symmetric hedged structure was deployed before each of several major NFP and CPI 
releases in EUR/USD.​
 
●​ Each trade involved equal-sized long and short positions with symmetric stops and 
targets.​
 
●​ No sophisticated filtering, volatility scaling, or dynamic parameter tuning was used.​
 
●​ The net result was a sequence of gains in which the winning leg of the straddle 
consistently outweighed the losing leg.​
 

The key is that: 
●​ MPD Pulse #1 almost always moves far enough in one direction to remove one side of 
the straddle; and​
 
●​ EMC and subsequent pulses typically extend the move enough for the surviving leg to 
reach profit targets under reasonable settings.​
 
From a theoretical perspective, this is remarkable because: 
●​ the strategy does not rely on predicting the direction of the surprise;​
 
●​ it does not require proprietary data feeds or colocation; and​
 
●​ it was discovered empirically before any formal model existed.​
 
Even more conservative assumptions—such as capturing only a small fraction of the available 
distance in each event—still imply a substantial compounding opportunity when multiplied 
across dozens of events per month and across macro + earnings calendars. 
The purpose of this paper is not to advertise a trading system, but to argue that a structural 
edge exists at the level of microstructure architecture, not simply at the level of indicators or 
parameters. 
 
7. Toward a Pulse Atlas 
The six events shown here represent only a subset of the observed universe. Extending the 
analysis across many months and events reveals: 
●​ a high incidence of MPD structures in NFP, CPI and central bank decisions;​
 
●​ frequent EMC behavior in high-impact events with strong surprises;​
 
●​ similar pulse patterns in earnings at predictable timestamps (e.g., 16:01, 16:05, 16:10, 
16:15); and​
 
●​ clusters of events that share recognizable “archetypes” of first-second behavior.​
 
In the companion work, I describe a Pulse Atlas that groups events into archetypes such as: 

●​ Single-Dominant Burst: One strong Pulse #1 with minimal follow-on pulses.​
 
●​ Staircase Repricing: Multiple clear pulses, each forming a step.​
 
●​ Vacuum Collapse: Extreme EMC with repeated collapse–rebuild cycles.​
 
●​ Clustered Earnings Pulses: Multiple local MPD episodes at known post-close times.​
 
●​ Contested Interpretation: Irregular pulses and unstable EMC, signaling poor conditions 
for simple hedged extraction.​
 
The Atlas shows that profitable opportunities are not randomly scattered but tend to concentrate 
in certain archetypes with strong structural signatures, and that weak or chaotic regimes have 
recognizable fingerprints as well. 
 
8. Discussion and Implications 
The findings of this paper suggest that the first second after a scheduled event is not the chaotic 
blur it is often assumed to be. Instead, it is a structured environment governed by: 
●​ the design of news-reading algorithms;​
 
●​ pre-programmed risk systems and volatility controls;​
 
●​ cross-asset reaction functions; and​
 
●​ the microstructural physics of order-book collapse and reformation.​
 
From a market design and microstructure standpoint, several implications follow: 
1.​ New Object of Study.​
 The first second after an event deserves to be treated as a distinct regime, with its own 
models and metrics, rather than being averaged into longer windows.​
 
2.​ Execution and Risk Management.​
 Execution algorithms that recognize MPD and EMC regimes can adapt order types, 
routing and participation rates accordingly, potentially improving slippage and reducing 
adverse selection.​
 

3.​ Market Power and Access.​
 The fact that this architecture is visible at one-second resolution on a retail terminal 
suggests that structural edge is not fully locked behind institutional speed and data 
advantages. Speed matters, but so does recognizing the architecture that speed 
operates within.​
 
4.​ Scientific Opportunity.​
 There is room for a new field—what I have called Event-Microstructure Science—that 
studies how markets digest information under extreme time compression, using MPD 
and EMC as core building blocks.​
 
 
9. Research Origin and Cognitive Context 
It is unusual for a retail trader, working outside institutional infrastructure, to identify, trade and 
then formalize a new layer of market microstructure. In this case, the path to discovery was 
shaped by an atypical cognitive context. 
In 2012, I survived a severe accident that resulted in traumatic brain injury. Subsequent 
evaluation by a major neurology department showed atypical neural reorganization and 
unusually strong working-memory performance—at a level consistent with what is sometimes 
described as acquired savant-like functioning in very specific domains. In practice, this meant 
the ability to: 
●​ sustain attention on dense, rapidly changing visual fields for long periods;​
 
●​ retain and mentally replay detailed sequences of one-second price and volume behavior; 
and​
 
●​ notice patterns in how pulses, collapses and recoveries repeated across events and 
instruments.​
 
These cognitive changes did not make the markets easier; they made observation deeper. 
MPD and EMC were not discovered by scanning a database with an existing model in mind, but 
by watching the same type of event, at the same time of month, across many months, until the 
architecture became unmistakable. 
This context is not mentioned to claim uniqueness or to assert that others cannot study or 
extend this work. Rather, it is offered as an honest explanation of how such a discovery could 
emerge from outside traditional institutional settings and as a reminder that the origin of a 
framework can matter for how it is interpreted and developed. 

 
10. Conclusion 
This paper has argued that the first second after scheduled economic releases and earnings 
announcements is governed by a repeatable architecture, not by pure chaos. The two central 
components of this architecture are: 
●​ Microstructural Pulse Dislocation (MPD):​
 segmented, directional price pulses within the first second; and​
 
●​ Event-Driven Microstructure Collapse (EMC):​
 rapid depth evaporation and asymmetric reformation that enable large price moves 
through thinned books.​
 
Using six representative events from FX and equities, and broker-verified trading records from 
2024–2025, I have shown that: 
●​ MPD and EMC appear consistently across NFP, CPI, FOMC, NZD, GBP CPI and 
earnings events;​
 
●​ a simple, symmetric hedged strategy implemented in real time, with no optimization and 
no privileged feeds, produced repeatable net profits by exploiting this architecture; and​
 
●​ the structure is visible at one-second resolution, making it accessible to scientific study 
even without microsecond data.​
 
The aim is not to claim that MPD and EMC are the final word on event microstructure. On the 
contrary, they are proposed as first-order constructs in a new domain—event-microstructure 
science—that will require further measurement, modeling and testing. But they are enough to 
establish that the first second is not an inscrutable blur; it is a structured environment with its 
own physics, its own risks and its own opportunities. 
For institutions, funds and researchers, the implication is straightforward: there is real 
informational and economic content in the architecture of the first second. For those interested 
in collaborating, extending or challenging these findings, this paper and its charts are an 
invitation to do so. 
The architecture is visible.​
 The definitions are now on record.​
 What happens next will depend on who chooses to look more closely.

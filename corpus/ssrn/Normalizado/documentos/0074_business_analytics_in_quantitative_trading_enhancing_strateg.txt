Abstract 
Quantitative trading has traditionally focused on signal generation 
and statistical arbitrage strategies. However, modern financial 
markets demand more than just alpha—they require a robust 
business framework for strategy evaluation and decision-making. 
This paper introduces a novel integration of business analytics into 
quant trading, using performance metrics and KPIs such as ROI, 
turnover ratio, and risk-adjusted return. We propose a multidimensional evaluation model that considers both financial 
outcomes and operational efficiency, thus aligning trading desk 
goals with enterprise objectives. The framework empowers data-

driven strategy selection using both quantitative logic and business 
intelligence. 
1. Introduction 
Quantitative trading employs data, statistics, and machine learning 
to build automated strategies. While this approach excels at 
uncovering inefficiencies and predicting price movements, it often 
lacks a business-oriented perspective. Traders and analysts may 
prioritize alpha without measuring risk, cost, or capital efficiency. 
This paper addresses that gap by introducing business analytics as 
a complementary layer in quant strategy assessment. 
2. Literature Review 
Classical models like Black-Scholes and CAPM laid the 
foundation for quantitative finance. Modern approaches include 
factor models, ML algorithms, and time-series forecasting. Yet, as 
pointed out by researchers such as Kissell (2013) and Pardo 
(2020), performance measures often ignore operational KPIs. This 
study builds on prior works by merging financial modeling with 
dashboard-based decision frameworks. 
3. Proposed Framework 
Our framework has three layers: 
1. Signal Layer (Alpha models, ML/Statistical signals) 
2. Trading Layer (Execution, backtesting, performance tracking) 
3. Business Analytics Layer (KPI tracking) 
 

The Business Analytics Layer introduces: 
- ROI: Return on capital deployed 
- Turnover Ratio: Trading frequency 
- Drawdown: Risk evaluation 
- Profit Factor 
- Custom KPI Score: Weighted composite index for strategy 
ranking 
4. Formula Highlights 
Some of the core formulas used include: 
- Sharpe Ratio: S = (Rp - Rf) / σp 
- ROI = (Net Profit / Capital Deployed) * 100 
- Turnover Ratio = |Total Trades| / Avg Portfolio Value 
- Composite KPI Score = α1*ROI + α2*PF - α3*Drawdown (α's 
are custom weights) 
5. Application Example 
We applied the framework on 3 strategies: Mean Reversion, 
Momentum, and ML-based ensemble. 
Using synthetic data and Python backtesting, we evaluated not just 
Sharpe ratios but also business KPIs like ROI and drawdown. 
Strategy C (ML model) had the highest risk-adjusted return, but 
Strategy A had better capital efficiency. 
6. Visualization & Reporting 
The results were visualized using Power BI and Streamlit, allowing 
business stakeholders to assess strategy performance in real-time. 

Radar charts, heatmaps, and dashboards enable multidimensional 
comparison. 
7. Conclusion 
Integrating business analytics with quant trading brings 
accountability and insight. It supports a transition from 'just 
predictive' to 'prescriptive and strategic' trading. Our framework 
provides a new lens for financial firms to evaluate not just 
performance, but the business impact of trading strategies.

Abstract 
Financial risk forecasting is a paramount challenge in global finance because of the growing 
complication of global financial environments and the swift development of online financial 
operations. The growth in financial technology and data-driven financial services has greatly 
contributed to the growth in the amount of financial data that is produced by any global financial 
system, which has led to the possibility of predictive modelling and sophisticated financial risk 
analysis. The article examines how regression-based predictive modelling is applicable in 
examining financial risk severity on the basis of transaction-level financial data. 
The method of quantitative research was chosen with the help of a dataset of financial transactions 
and risk management received on Kaggle. The correlation analysis and multiple regression 
modelling to assess the relationships among the variables transaction behaviour, system 
performance indicators and the severity of financial risk was applied using Python. The findings 
demonstrate that the regression model has a rather low explanatory power with the R-squared at 
0.004 that means that the chosen variables can only explain a minor percentage of the change in 
the severity of incidences. Out of the variables that were analysed, the frequency of logins has a 
statistically significant correlation with risk severity, whereas the number of transactions, system 
latency, and failed authentication attempts have a limited predictive value. 
The results demonstrate the weaknesses of simple regression models to predict the financial risks 
and indicate that less complicated predictive modelling techniques might be essential to forecast 
the complex processes that can contribute to the occurrence of the financial risks in global financial 
systems. The paper advances the body of existing research on predictive risk modelling in the 
finance field and provides an indication on the possible application of analytical methods based on 
data in enhancing financial risk management practices. 
Keywords: Predictive risk modelling, financial transactions, regression analysis, financial risk 
management, international finance.

1. Introduction 
1.1 Global Currency Market Context 
The international finance is a dynamic international currency market place where the exchange 
rate movement has a strong impact on international investment decision-making, capital allocation 
and financial exposure. The world foreign exchange market is the biggest financial market across 
the world supporting cross-border trade, international portfolio investments and multinational 
corporate business. The increasing digitalisation of financial systems and the growth of global 
trade networks have greatly contributed to the increase in the number of currency transaction and 
the exposure of financial risk linked to exchange rate fluctuation. The instrument of predictive 
financial modelling has thus turned out to be a key instrument in the uncertainty management and 
potential risk pattern identification in foreign markets (Broby, 2022). 
The combination of sophisticated analytical tools and data-driven financial systems has 
revolutionised the manner in which financial institutions examine risk and behaviour of 
investment. Predictive analytics methods used by financial organisations are becoming more 
important, to analyse large volumes of data and identify patterns that drive asset prices and 
currency trends. Predictive analytics is a type of statistical models, machine learning algorithms 
and quantitative replication to make predictions that can be used to make financial decisions and 
reduce risks (Bahoo et al., 2024). The emergent speed of artificial intelligence applications in 
finance illustrates how enhanced data modelling methods may enhance predictive precision and 
stability in the financial market by enhancing the risk assessment system(s) (Bahoo et al., 2024). 
1.2 Research Problem 
Although predictive modelling techniques in finance are getting more and more popular, predicting 
currency fluctuations is a challenging task because of the complexity of financial systems in the 
world and the interplay between various macroeconomic variables. The financial markets produce 
both structured and unstructured volumes of data in huge quantity, and it has been challenging to 
come up with credible forecasts of exchange rate volatility through the conventional financial 
analysis tools. The use of machine learning and statistical modelling methods in financial risk 
prediction has thus gained more and more popularity to enhance the accuracy of the forecast and 
to assist in making investment decisions (Leo et al., 2019). 
Empirical studies reveal that financial risk prediction model has mainly concentrated on credit 
risk, Bankruptcy forecasting and default prediction but not exchange rate forecasting, which 

6 
 
implies an absence of application of predictive modelling in international finance (Shi and Li, 
2019). Besides, the current macroeconomic prediction models of financial risk tend to be based on 
a limited number of macroeconomic variables, and fail to incorporate sophisticated analytical tools 
to isolate the complex financial relationships of large data sets. The systematic review of predictive 
risk modelling shows that the accuracy of financial forecasting can be increased dramatically with 
the use of high-dimensional financial data processing via machine learning and advanced data 
analytics (Shi et al., 2022). 
In addition, most of the literature is focused on corporate financial risk instead of macro-financial 
risk variables that determine currency fluctuations in the global markets. The studies of the 
financial prediction models show that the combination of quantitative modelling methods with 
macroeconomic variables like interest rates can enhance the risk assessment and forecasting 
significantly (Ciampi et al., 2021). This is the limitation of the existing literature that leads to the 
necessity of a data-based quantitative study that studies the correlation between interest rate 
changes and the currency fluctuations via statistical modelling. 
1.3 Research Aim 
The aim of the research is to establish a quantitative predictive risk model to examine the 
relationship between changes in interest rates and fluctuations of the currency market in the 
international finance through the statistical analysis of regression. 
1.4 Research Objectives 
 To examine how predictive analytics can be used in financial risk modelling in the international 
financial markets. 
 To test the association between interest rate fluctuations and the currency changes by the use 
of quantitative modelling. 
 To model a predictive model through regression, based on the financial transaction and risk 
management data. 
 To determine how good predictive modelling methods are in predicting patterns of financial 
risks in the international markets.

2. Literature Review 
2.1 Exchange Rate Determination Theory 
Determination of exchange rates has been a highly discussed issue that has been subject to 
macroeconomic models that interrelate the movement of the currency as well as with economic 
variables like inflation rates, interest rates and capital flows. These theoretical explanations 
indicate that fluctuations in macroeconomic environment have effects on investor expectations and 
cross-border capital movements which further have impacts on the exchange rate behaviour. 
According to Kovacova et al. (2019), the models of financial modelling have contributed to the 
increasing number of macroeconomic variables that can be used to expound on the patterns of 
financial risk and the economic correlations in complex financial systems. Nonetheless, 
Veganzones and Severin (2021) stipulate that the historical financial theories cannot be considered 
sufficient to describe the current behaviour of financial markets since global financial 
marketplaces now produce considerable amount of economic data, which need quantitative 
modelling methods. Financial studies are thus becoming more and more a combination of 
theoretical frameworks in finance and predictive modelling to enhance the dynamics of financial 
markets. Kovacova et al. (2019) also note that the predictive financial models enable the researcher 
to examine the relationship between two or more variables in the economy at once, which enhances 
the comprehension of financial risk and market behaviour. On the same note, Veganzones and 
Severin (2021) report that predictive modelling techniques will have more substantial empirical 
explanation of the financial results than purely theoretical frameworks. Consequently, quantitative 
financial modelling, which is a combination of quantitative economic theory and statistical 
analysis of data, is becoming a popular tool in determining exchange rates. 
2.2 Currency Volatility in International Finance 
Currency volatility is a significant cause of financial uncertainty due to the exposure of exchange 
rates in the competitiveness of international trade, decision-making on multinational investment 
and monetary market stability. Financial markets are typified by nonstop interactions involving 
economic shocks, investor expectations and policy choices that form intricate patterns in the 

8 
 
behaviour of the exchange rate. According to Sonkavde et al. (2023), the application of financial 
forecasting models to the examination of the financial market volatility is becoming frequent due 
to the fact that the contemporary financial markets generate vast amounts of data that cannot be 
effectively interpreted without the application of sophisticated analytical methods. Nevertheless, 
Yang et al. (2023) claim that the conventional financial forecasting models do not strongly 
represent nonlinear dynamics and intricate connections in financial data which restricts their 
capabilities in predicting volatility. The recent trend in predictive modelling has thus been to 
incorporate sophisticated approach in financial forecasting in order to enhance its accuracy. As 
Sonkavde et al. (2023) add, machine learning and data-driven forecasting models can find hidden 
trends in financial information that can affect market volatility. Conversely, Baryannis et al. (2019) 
indicate that predictive modelling strategies should also take into account the macro-economic 
setting and various risk factors due to the frequent financial instability that is the result of the 
globalized systems of the global economy. These results indicate that the currency volatility 
studies are gradually taking the significance of predictive modelling models in studying financial 
risk in the global markets. 
2.3 Interest Rates and International Capital Flows 
The interest rate differentials are significant in the international capital flows as investors tend to 
commit capital in the financial markets that have higher anticipated returns. National interest rates 
differ, and this impacts on investment and international portfolio flows which in turn affect 
currency demand and behaviour of the exchange rates. According to Kovacova et al. (2019), 
predictive financial models often use macroeconomic variables like interest rates and the rate of 
inflation to predictive financial risk and investment patterns within global markets. Nonetheless, 
Veganzones and Severin (2021) reiterate that financial prediction models should incorporate 
various economic factors to ensure that the relationships which shape financial market behaviour 
are captured. The study on predictive modelling also points out that the financial risk forecasting 
operations demand strong statistical modelling frameworks that can enable interactions among 
variables in the economy. According to Luo et al. (2023), the recent trend in predictive risk models 
is that they combine both artificial intelligence and statistical analysis to assess the financial risk 
patterns and enhance forecasting performance. However, Baryannis et al. (2019) warn that to 
guarantee quality forecasting results, predictive models should include the relevant economic 
factors and indicators of risk. These views show that the interest rate analysis is still a critical part 

9 
 
of predictive modelling models applied in the assessment of the behaviour of financial markets 
across international jurisdictions. 
2.4 Predictive Risk Modelling in Financial Markets 
The predictive risk modelling has been a critical analytical tool of financial studies due to the fact 
that financial markets are complex systems that are typified by uncertainty and data volumes. The 
contemporary financial institutions are becoming more dependent on predictive modelling 
frameworks to find out the financial risk trends and predict future market results. As described by 
Yang et al. (2023), machine learning methods have greatly empowered risk management systems 
because it allowed financial analysts to handle significant volumes of data and discern complicated 
interactions among financial variables. Nevertheless, Baryannis et al. (2019) emphasize that to 
develop relevant predictive modelling systems, structured datasets and suitable risk indicators are 
required to make correct forecasts in the context of financial risk management. The principles of 
predictive modelling can be used in a variety of industries in which risk prediction and forecasting 
are key to decision making. According to Wynants et al. (2020), predictive models should be tested 
in terms of rigorous validation frameworks to be able to guarantee their reliability and 
generalisability to other datasets. On the same note, Spinu et al. (2020) note that predictive 
modelling techniques need unequivocal causal associations among variables and results to make 
sound risk forecasts. Such views indicate that predictive modelling frameworks are becoming 
appreciated as helpful analytical tools that can be used to examine the trend patterns in financial 
risks and predict economic performance in the global financial markets. 
2.5 Empirical Studies on Interest Rates and Exchange Rates 
Empirical financial studies are increasingly concerned with the formulation of predictive models 
which have the potential of analysing back and forward relationships between macroeconomic 
variables and behaviour of financial markets. According to Sonkavde et al. (2023), statistical and 
machine learning forecasting models have been massively applied to forecast the behaviour of the 
financial market and draw patterns that can impact the price of assets and financial risks indicators. 
Nevertheless, Veganzone and Severin (2021) claim that most predictive financial models are 
preoccupied with predicting the financial performance of firms at the corporate level and not with 
macroeconomic factors affecting the international financial markets. Recent empirical studies also 
focus on the increased significance of data-driven modelling systems to analyse financial systems. 
As Luo et al. (2023) emphasize, financial risk forecasting can be enhanced with the help of 

10 
 
predictive modelling frameworks that utilise the idea of artificial intelligence to forecast financial 
risks by studying several variables at the same time. Contrary to this, according to Yang et al. 
(2023), predictive financial models should be provided to combine macroeconomic and 
microeconomic variables to guarantee effective forecasting results. Such results show that more 
empirical studies are being used to justify the use of predictive modelling methods in financial 
market analysis. 
2.6 Literature Gap 
In the current literature on the topic of international financial risk modelling, some gaps are 
observed even though there is a remarkable progress in the research of predictive modelling and 
financial forecasting. According to Kovacova et al. (2019), the majority of the studies that involve 
predictive modelling analysis look at the prediction of corporates bankruptcy and financial risk on 
the firm level instead of macroeconomic variables that relate to fluctuations in the currency of the 
global financial markets. Likewise, Veganzones and Severin (2021) emphasize that the research 
of predictive modelling has mainly been oriented to predictive financial forecasting in corporations 
but little has been done to forecast macroeconomic financial risks using predictive models. 
Additionally, predictive modelling frameworks have been extensively used in other areas like 
healthcare and biomedical risk prediction but they have not been used extensively in international 
finance. Wynants et al. (2020) state that predictive models need organized datasets, value frames 
and adequate variable identification to give trustworthy forecasting outcomes.  
3. Research Methodology 
3.1 Research Design 
The study will follow a quantitative research approach to investigate the connection between the 
fluctuations in interest rates and the pattern of financial risks in the global financial markets. The 
quantitative type of research design is the most common type of research design in financial 
modelling research as it enables the researcher to examine structured data sets through statistical 
and computational methods. Statistical modelling methods allow determination of relationships 
among financial variables as well as give quantifiable information about financial risk behaviour. 
According to Chopra and Sharma (2021), quantitative analytical models have been extensively 
used in financial forecasting studies since they enable the large financial datasets to be analysed 
through statistical and machine learning methods that enhance predictive accuracy. Research in 
predictive modelling also highlights the fact that forecasting financial risks would necessitate 

11 
 
organized modelling framework which can help to forecast complex financial relationships among 
several variables. As Hammad et al. (2020) observe, statistical modelling techniques are often used 
to predict the future in economic and financial systems as such techniques enable a researcher to 
assess the relationship between past trends in the history of variables and the future. Besides this, 
predictive modelling models allow researchers to check the relevance of various economic 
variables in establishing financial results. Hence, the quantitative modelling technique of 
regression is deemed suitable to study the trend of financial risks in relation to international 
currency variations.

5. Discussion 
According to the results of the regression analysis, the predictive ability of the chosen financial 
variables used in determining the incident severity is not very high. The model generated a value 
of R-squared of 0.004, which implies that the explanatory variables used in the regression model 
explain less than one percent of the change in the severity of financial risk. Such a result suggests 
that the events of financial risks in the data set can be affected by a wider scope of variables other 
than transaction value, system latency, login behaviour and authentication failures. According to 
Chopra and Sharma (2021), complex datasets and the presence of various explanatory factors are 
usually needed to substantiate financial forecasting models because a financial system and market 
behaviour are dynamic. The regression output also indicates that the only variable that proves to 
be statistically significant at the 5% level of significance is the frequency of logins. The positive 
correlation between the quantification of the login activity and the severity of incidents can also 
be taken as evidence that the higher the interaction of the system, the more risk incidents in the 
system are likely to happen.  
The intensity of this relationship is however not very significant. Hammad et al. (2020) stress that 
predictive models that are created on the premise of limited variables have quite low explanatory 
power since complex systems are usually characterised by nonlinear interplay of several 
predictors. The low correlations that exist among the variables and the severity of the incident also 
demonstrate the conclusion that more elaborate modelling frameworks are needed to predict the 
issue of a financial risk. Kureljusic and Karger (2024) state that financial forecasting is becoming 
more and more based on the artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning models due to the 
ability to interpret complicated data set structures and the potential to find patterns that are not 
necessarily detected by traditional statistical models. On the same note, Debray et al. (2019) point 
out that predictive modelling models should have an effective validation and use of more than one 
predictive variable in order to enhance forecasting. Moreover, a body of research on predictive 
modelling in diverse fields suggests that application of predictive models in diverse datasets must 
be undertaken with a high degree of care in selecting the variables and adjusting the model.

According to Kachuri et al. (2024), predictor risk models should be configured to match the data 
characteristics to enable accurate and reliable forecasting in a variety of settings. 
6. Implications for International Finance 
This study has a number of implications on international finance and in financial risk management. 
First, the results indicate that basic regression frameworks relying on a small number of financial 
variables are not likely to be effective in forecasting complex patterns of financial risks in the 
world of financial systems. Financial markets entails relationships between various macroeconomic, behavioural and technological factors that determine the financial risk outcomes. 
Second, the findings demonstrate the significance of embracing more sophisticated predictive 
modelling methods in financial risk management mechanisms. Machine learning and artificial 
intelligence can potentially scan massive amounts of data in the financial sector and identify 
undiscovered insights that can enhance the predictability of the financial sector. According to 
Chopra and Sharma (2021), the use of superior analytical models can considerably improve the 
financial risk prediction through the inclusion of numerous economic and behavioural metrics. 
Lastly, financial institutions that are involved in international markets ought to contemplate the 
incorporation of predictive analytics systems into their risk management systems as a means of 
enhancing the ability to early detect the occurrence of financial risk events and enhance the 
stability of financial systems. 
7. Conclusion 
This paper has discussed the use of predictive modelling on the severity of financial risk based on 
regression with a financial transaction dataset. The descriptive statistics showed that there was a 
great difference in the values of transactions and user activity pattern within the dataset, and the 
correlation analysis showed weak relationships that existed between the chosen financial variables 
and the severity of the incidents. The regression analysis also showed that the model has a weak 
explanatory power, as its R-squared causes the changes at a low percentage with R-squared being 
0.004 giving the indicators that the independent variables can explain very little of the changes in 
the severity of financial risks. The only variable that proved to be statistically significant among 
the analysed variables was the frequency of logging in, but its impact on the severity of an incident 
was comparatively insignificant. This implies that financial risk forecasting in global financial 
systems needs more elaborate modelling systems, which involve the use of more financial, 
behavioural and macroeconomic variables. The area of future research should be the combination 

19 
 
of machine learning and artificial intelligence in order to increase predictive accuracy and the 
forecasting of financial risks in the global financial markets.

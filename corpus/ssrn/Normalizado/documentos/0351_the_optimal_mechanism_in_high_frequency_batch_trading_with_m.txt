The optimal strategies in high-frequency batch trading with multiple news
Mingshun Sun, Haijun Yang∗
School of Economics and Management, Beihang University, Beijing, 100191, China
Abstract
We develop a new high-frequency batch model based on game theory to analyze market behavior and address
the issue of optimal trading strategies under multiple news arrival scenarios. In this framework, the behaviors
and the profits of high-frequency traders (HFTs) are critically influenced by both the quantity and the sequence
of news arrival, and the multiple news setting explains price jump perfectly. The model incorporates three
interconnected games among HFTs and is refined to identify the trembling-hand perfect Nash equilibrium
(THPNE). By maximizing the total market welfare measure, an optimal batch interval is determined to achieve
the most efficient THPNE within the batch mechanism. Within this equilibrium, we characterize the optimal
spread strategy and dynamic order strategy of HFTs. Single- and multi-period batch models are constructed to
compute payoff matrices by analyzing market microstructure and HFTs’ behavior across multiple sequential
news arrival scenarios, thereby enabling the derivation of the market spread and HFT profits. Finally, we show
the optimal batch interval, a key practical finding that balances operational efficiency with strategic advantage,
thereby providing actionable insights for market design and participant strategy.
Keywords: Batch mechanism; Trembling-hand perfect refinement; High-frequency trading
JEL classification C73; D41; G11
1. Introduction
High-frequency trading contributes to price discovery and enhances market liquidity, making it a dominant
trend in modern financial markets (Nahar et al., 2024). Currently, most exchanges utilize the traditional continuous trading mechanism to facilitate high-frequency trading. However, high-frequency trading carries potential
risks, such as an arms race (Chung and Kim, 2024), flash crashes (Leal and Napoletano, 2019), and adverse
selection due to risk-free arbitrage (Cartea et al., 2014), stemming from the nature of the continuous trading
mechanism. The continuous mechanism also fails to explain the price jump anomaly, especially in the highfrequency market(Liao, 2026). In response to these challenges, researchers have begun to explore alternative
mechanisms for high-frequency trading. The batch mechanism is an excellent attempt.
To mitigate resource waste caused by high-frequency trading arms races, Budish et al. (2014, 2015) introduce the frequent batch auction (FBA) mechanism. Based on the auction mechanism, the FBA mechanism

compared to the continuous mechanism (Baldauf and Mollner, 2020). But most prevailing trading exchanges
operate under the conventional market-maker mechanism, unlike the auction-based FBA mechanism. To address this discrepancy, Ge and Yang (2025) integrate the batch mechanism with the market-maker model,
effectively resolving the pricing problem under the constraint of at most a single news arrival per batch.
However, in more general scenarios, such as multiple news arrivals, the trading environment becomes
more complex. First, multiple news frameworks explain the price-jump phenomenon, which current batch or
continuous models fail to capture, and incorporate price-jump modeling into traditional price theory. Second,
risk-free arbitrage vanishes if multiple news arrives before settlement, thereby diminishing the profitability of
high-frequency traders (HFTs). Third, both the quantity and the sequence of news arrival are highly influential
in determining HFTs’ profits. Consequently, the strategic interactions among HFTs become intricate: they can
no longer snipe indiscriminately. Instead, they must adopt more cautious, strategic decision-making in this
complex game-theoretic environment. Interestingly, the HFIs even engage in sniping on both sides of the trade
in a three-news-arrival scenario, a strategy never adopted by rational HFTs in any single-news-arrival scenario.
Hence, investigating the behavioral strategy decisions of HFTs and the optimal design of financial markets
represents a valuable and pertinent contribution to the market microstructure research literature.
In this paper, we develop a three-tiered game-theoretic model to analyze interactions among HFTs under
a market-maker-based batch mechanism in a general setting with multiple sequential news arrival scenarios.
Our goal is to characterize the behavioral traits and strategic choices of each type of high-frequency participant
and identify the optimal equilibrium of the batch mechanism. First, we define three interconnected games: the
HFT game, in which HFTs compete for the high-frequency market maker (HFM) position; the HFM game, in
which potential HFM competitors determine the spread-quoting strategy; and the HFI game, in which highfrequency investors (HFIs) dynamically optimize the order timing strategies. Second, we construct both singleand multi-period batch models to compute the payoff matrices. We analyze the market microstructure and
HFTs’ behaviors under each condition across various multiple news scenarios, deriving the payoff expectancies of HFTs across all cases. The equilibrium analysis reveals intriguing insights: unlike the unique risk-free
arbitrage strategy observed in traditional single-news-arrival scenarios, the three-tiered game exhibits multiple
Nash equilibria, and HFTs consistently employ identical strategies in each Nash equilibrium; however, not all
equilibria are stable or perfect. Finally, we refine the complex game model based on the trembling-hand perfection framework (Luo et al., 2022) to identify the optimal trembling-hand perfect Nash equilibrium (THPNE).
In THPNE under news neutrality, the HFM adopts the narrowest spread strategy, termed "price priority for
the market maker"; HFIs choose to submit orders upon the news arrival with latency, termed "time priority for
investors", rather than waiting until the final possible moment. This behavior supports the arms race inherent in
the batch mechanism, benefiting total market welfare but harming market liquidity. Analysis within the singleperiod batch model reveals that in the THPNE: (i) As trading speed increases, or news frequency increases,
or the number of HFTs decreases, the market liquidity decreases while the total market welfare increases; (ii)

2. Literature review
Since THPNE integrates a high-frequency batch mechanism with game-theoretic approaches, we investigate existing research methodologies in these two aspects. Since the FBA mechanism is designed by (Budish
et al., 2014, 2015), academic opinions on its performance remain divided: Supporters argue that it dominates
the continuous mechanism by reducing adverse selection costs (Zhang and Ibikunle, 2023; Aquilina et al.,
2024; Breckenfelder, 2024), lowering the risk of market collapse (Ladley, 2020; Jagannathan, 2022), preventing the price destabilization (Sadzik and Woolnough, 2021; Lu and Zhan, 2022),and enhancing market liquidity
(Aldrich and Vargas, 2020; Haas et al., 2021; Alves et al., 2023); While critics contend that it fails to consistently improve market liquidity (Haas and Zoican, 2016; Lee et al., 2020), can’t avoid inefficiencies in the
presence of informed trading (Eibelshäuser and Smetak, 2022), and is a source of short-term price volatility (Gehrig and Ritzberger, 2022). Although primarily supported by simulations based on continuous market
data, the FBA mechanism offers a promising solution for high-frequency trading. Actually, empirical evidence
suggests that certain trading algorithms achieve meaningful performance under the FBA mechanism (Savidge
and Cliff, 2023). To address the discrepancy between the auction-based FBA mechanism and the conventional
market-maker systems, Ge and Yang (2025) integrate the batch mechanism with the prevailing market-maker
model, moving away from the auction-based framework, and solve the pricing problem.
Under multiple news scenarios, HFTs face a stochastic game in continuous time with imperfect information, leading to multiple unstable Nash equilibria. Trembling-hand perfect equilibrium, as a refinement method
of perfect correlated equilibrium (PCE), can eliminate unstable or imperfect Nash equilibria effectively (Selten,
1975; Dhillon and Mertens, 1996). Compared with traditional refinement methods such as the maximin principle, trembling-hand perfect refinement can capture the trend of decision-making optimization in HFTs under

what constitutes the optimal equilibrium under general market conditions.
3. The three-tiered game model with high-frequency batch mechanism
In this section, we present a model of a three-tiered complex game with a high-frequency batch mechanism
under multiple sequential news arrival scenarios. Let τ denote the length of a batch interval. During the period
(0, τ], news arrives followed by an independent Poisson process with arrival rate λ, and each news impacts the
asset price by σ. It is reasonable to assume news neutrality, which means that the probabilities of good and bad
news are both 50%. 1 When τ is large enough, multiple news arrive, then affect the decisions and benefits of
market participants further.
Compared with classical continuous mechanism or single news settings, the multiple news batch model can
handle the phenomenon of price jumps better. In the traditional continuous model or single news batch model,
the fundamental price is always affected by the news one by one, hence the price can only change at most once
in a period, meaning the price strictly follows a random walk process, which can’t explain the financial anomaly
of the price jump.
However, the multiple news setting is not only more realistic and broader in applicability, but also provides
a depiction of the microstructure and the drivers for price jumps. In this framework, the price changes are
determined by the number of news arriving at the two trade sides in each batch interval. When the number of
news in a trade side is much greater than the opposite, a price jump occurs. Thus, the price no longer obeys the
random walk process; instead, it’s a combination of two Poisson processes. Since the distribution of news is
identical in each batch interval, the price expectancy converges to a Brownian motion according to the central
1Actually, Non-neutral news would lead to an extreme equilibrium, as discussed in section 3.3.1. News neutrality also ensures that
the order volume expectations of both sides of the HFM are the same, thereby preventing positional bias.

There are two types of market participants: high-frequency traders (HFTs), who trade rapidly based on
current market information; and liquidity traders (LTs), who arrive stochastically and trade immediately upon
arrival. 3
• High-frequency traders (HFTs): HFTs can choose between two roles: (1) becoming high-frequency
investors (HFIs) or (2) competing to serve as the high-frequency market maker (HFM). Once an HFT
wins to become the HFM, all other HFTS have to act as HFIs. Subsequently, all other market participants
trade exclusively with the HFM under the market-maker mechanism. Each HFIs faces an identical small
response latency denoted by δ (where δ ≪τ). During the trading period (0, τ], HFIs can decide to
submit orders at any time based on their information sets. All HFI are ex ante homogeneous, but can
adopt heterogeneous trading strategies.
• Liquidity traders (LT): LTs arrive followed by an independent Poisson process with arrival rate µ

both the bid and ask trade sides, acting as low-frequency traders. LTs trade immediately upon arrival for
liquidity motives. Under the market-maker mechanism, only the first LT on each side can get the order
from HFM if there is no HFT in front of it.
The batch mechanism trading formation process operates as follows. In the beginning, an HFM competed
from N HFTs determines the bid-ask spread s, 4 and submits a bid quote and an ask quote to establish the
limit order book. Subsequently, HFIs and LTs sequentially submit orders within the time interval (0, τ]. At the
settlement time τ, the HFM executes trades with the first order on each side, provided at least one order exists.
In our model, orders are executed according to the "price-time priority" principle, and order cancellations are
prohibited. Considering that HFIs and LTs are price takers, meaning all market participants are homogeneous
in price, order timing becomes the sole determinant of trading outcomes.
There are three interconnected games in the trading formation process in chronological order.
• Step 1: the HFT game. At time 0, HFTs need to determine to be HFI or to compete to be the HFM. N
HFTs compete to become the designated HFM.
• Step 2: the HFM game. At time 0, the winning HFM must determine the spread-quoting strategy.
• Step 3: the HFI game. During time period (0, τ], HFIs need to dynamically determine order strategy,
including whether and when to submit orders.

of HFIs; next, solve the HFM game via equilibrium computation to determine the spread; finally, refine the

typically require the spread to exceed the price impact of news (s > σ), enabling HFIs to snipe stale quotes
exactly when the news arrives to make a risk-free arbitrage. However, our multiple news model under the
batch mechanism may lead to scenarios that don’t exist in the single news frameworks. In the multiple news
model, the HFM must consider multiple spread thresholds to match the multiple news cases.

theoretical optimal Nash equilibrium is the strategy that maximizes profit. This dynamic incentivizes HFMs
to progressively widen their spreads, ultimately driving market liquidity toward zero, which is an undesirable
outcome. To address this phenomenon, our model refines the Nash equilibrium by considering the market

fundamental value of assets unchanged until time τ, therefore, the risk-free arbitrage of snipers in traditional
high-frequency trading disappears. Since both the quantity and sequence of multiple news arrivals affect HFIs’

settlement, making it suitable for aggressive HFIs. In contrast, strategy 2 is risk-free but has the lowest priority,
with a higher probability of LTs’ arrival, making it more appropriate for cautious HFIs.

4. The trembling-hand perfect refinement of the competitive HFI game
In the previous section, we derived the Nash equilibria of the HFI game: all HFIs adopt symmetric trade
strategies such as (t1, t1), (t2, t2), · · · , (tn, tn), · · · , (τ, τ). However, determining which equilibrium yields the
highest payoff expectancy remains analytically challenging. Moreover, our analysis hasn’t yet accounted for
the reduction in payoff expectancies due to competition among N −1 HFIs. In this section, we further refine
the Nash equilibrium by applying trembling-hand perfect refinement to identify an optimal equilibrium under
competitive conditions in the HFI game.
For the convenience of analysis, we assume that the HFIs have a total of K possible strategies, where

5.1. Model specification
In the single-period batch model with multiple sequential news, the basic framework follows the specifications outlined in Section 2. In the high-frequency setting, the probability of observing many news arrivals
within a given interval is negligible; we can treat such events as low-probability. Consequently, we simplify the
multiple sequential news assumption in Section 2 to a limited multiple sequential news framework, focusing
exclusively on the most representative scenarios: no news arrival, one news arrival, two news arrivals, and
three news arrivals within a batch interval. Considering that more news arrivals are meaningless, but more
computation and complexity are required. In this model, we assume the following specifications:
• Single batch interval: time period (0, τ], order cancellations are prohibited. At the end period (settlement
time τ), all information is cleared.
• News: arrive neutrally followed by a Poisson process with arrival rate λ, each affecting the price by σ.
• LTs: arrive followed by a Poisson process with arrival rate µ
2 on each trade sides.
• The HFM: determines the spread-quoting strategy s, denoted by B.
• HFIs: determine the order strategy with a response latency δ, denoted by A. The number of HFIs is N −1.

5.4. Equilibria

strategy s1, all of HFIs adopt strategy 1. In the market equilibrium of the HFT game, the payoff expectancies
of HFIs and the HFM should satisfy equation(2). So far, all HFTs have reached their optimal strategies in the
three-tiered games.
We begin with the simplest news case and progressively consider more complex news conditions. As the
number of cases increases, the probability distribution of our model becomes more realistic, thereby better
approximating real world conditions.
5.4.1. No news arrival case
This represents an extreme case, and the payoff expectancies of HFIs and the HFM in the no news arrival
scenario are given in equation(11). In this case, HFIs execute no action, resulting in zero payoffs, while the
HFM maintains a positive payoff expectancy. Theoretically, the HFM can determine the spread quote s∗at any
positive value, since LTs always trade immediately upon arrival. Consequently, all HFTs are incentivized to
compete for the HFM position. The winner takes all.

5.6. The optimal batch mechanism
We have identified the optimal strategies of all HFTs under the batch model with limited multiple sequential
news. Furthermore, how to design a more efficient batch mechanism to enhance market quality remains a
critical research question. Generally, market quality is measured along two key dimensions: the market liquidity
and the total market welfare (Ackert et al., 2026). The former is commonly represented by the spread s∗, while
the latter can be captured by the profit of the HFM E(B∗). A lower s∗or a higher E(B∗) indicates superior
market quality. In reality, the parameters σ, λ, and µ are determined by market circumstances, which are
uncontrolled exogenous variables. In contrast, our model treats the parameters δ, N, and τ as controllable
exogenous variables, allowing for systematic optimization of market design.
Figure 12 – 17 demonstrate that when δ decreases, both s∗and E(B∗) increase, which means a decrease in
liquidity and an increase in welfare. A shorter latency enables HFIs to snipe stale quotes more easily, thereby
reducing liquidity. Meanwhile, more snipes mean more trades and more welfare. Consequently, HFTs compete
to decrease the latency δ in pursuit of higher profits, and directly compound liquidity deterioration, which
triggers "the high-frequency trading arms race" described in proposition 5. Unlike the frequent batch auction
(FBA) mechanism in Budish et al. (2015) or the traditional single news batch model, where the arms race
doesn’t significantly increase welfare but primarily harms liquidity, our multiple news batch model enhances
the effect of latency. Under multiple news conditions, the latency δ exerts a stronger influence on the probability
of successful sniping. As a result, the arms race in our framework indeed increases the total market welfare.
Proposition 5. "The high-frequency trading arms race" enhances the total market welfare, but weakens the
market liquidity.

When N increases, both s∗and E(B∗) decrease, indicating an increase of liquidity and a decrease of
welfare. More competition among HFIs enhances the market liquidity, but weaken the total market welfare as
described earlier.
When τ increases, both s∗and E(B∗) first increase and then decrease, with the former peaking earlier
than the latter. In the early stage, a longer batch interval means shorter latency, more news, and more HFIs,
leading to lower liquidity but higher welfare. However, in the later stage, a longer batch interval means more
LTs, leading to higher liquidity but lower welfare. Consequently, this yields a liquidity minimum and welfare
maximum, which implies the existence of an optimal batch interval τ maximizing the total market welfare,
while no analogous optimum exists for s∗or the market liquidity.

6.1. Model specification
• Multiple batch interval: periodical time periods (0, τ], (τ, 2τ], · · · . Order cancellations are prohibited.
At the end of each time period (settlement time τ, 2τ, · · · ), the information is retained for subsequent
processing.
• News: arrive neutrally followed by a Poisson process with parameter λ, each affecting the price by σ.
• LTs: arrive followed by a Poisson process with parameter µ
2 on each trade sides.
• The HFM: determines the spread-quoting strategy s, denoted by B.
• HFIs: determine the order time strategy with a response delay δ, denoted by A. The number of HFIs is
N −1.
6.2. HFTs’ behaviors and payoff expectancies
Similar to the single-period batch model, we still calculate the payoff expectancies of HFIs and the HFM
with strategy 1 and strategy 2 across different scenarios. However, in the multi-period batch model, the news
arriving during the period (τ −δ, τ] donot influence the payoff expectancies of the current batch interval (0, τ].
By contrast, the news arriving during the last period (−δ, 0] affect the payoff expectancies of current batch
interval (0, τ] in deed, and the probability distribution of news arrival during the period (τ −δ, τ] and period
(−δ, 0] are exactly identical. Consequently, unlike the single-period batch model, the news that arrives during
the period (τ −δ, τ] doesn’t affect the payoff expectancies in the multi-period batch model. All HFTs observe
the news arrival during the period (−δ, τ −δ], and subsequently take actions during the period (0, τ]. The
specific payoff expectancies and probabilities are presented in the appendix table A.6.

6.3. The vanishing latency δ
Interestingly, the time period can be decomposed into two dimensions: the information dimension and the
action dimension. The information dimension captures news arrival information, while the action dimension
records the quotes of the HFM and the snipes of HFIs. Notably, the action dimension lags behind the information dimension by δ. 23 For instance, all HFTs observe the information dimension during (τ −δ, 2τ −δ], and
execute their corresponding actions in the action dimension during (τ, 2τ].
Proposition 7. The latency parameter δ has no effect on the multi-period batch model.
6.4. Equilibria
In the refinement of Nash equilibria of both the HFM game and the HFI game, consistent with the basic
model, the HFM adopts the spread strategy s1; meanwhile, all HFIs adopt strategy 1. In the market equilibrium
of the HFT game, the payoff expectancies of HFIs and the HFM still satisfy equation(2).

7. Conclusion
This paper proposes THPNE as a solution concept for the high-frequency trading issue within the batch
mechanism under multiple sequential news arrival scenarios. The proposed theoretical framework considers,
for the first time, the complex strategic interactions among HFTs and price jumps, thereby enabling a detailed
34
This preprint research paper has not been peer reviewed. Electronic copy available at: https://ssrn.com/abstract=6663324
Preprint not peer reviewed

characterization of their behavioral traits and strategic decision-making. To our knowledge, this is the first work
to employ a game-theoretic model in which HFTs optimize their trading strategies. Beyond its context-specific
applications, this work also offers several key theoretical contributions to the existing literature.
We establish a three-tiered complex game structure and employ the trembling-hand perfection method to
refine the multiple Nash equilibria into THPNE. This approach effectively resolves the behavioral decisionmaking problem for HFTs in the multiple news arrival scenarios. We characterize the behavioral traits and
payoff matrices of HFTs across multiple news arrival scenarios in both single-period and multi-period batch
models, and analyze the impact of various factors on market microstructure. This analysis provides a foundation
for informed investment decisions in the market. We derive the equilibrium spreads and profits in THPNE and
further identify an optimal batch interval that maximizes total market welfare. This finding points out the
optimal batch mechanism for high-frequency trading.
Several key propositions emerge from THPNE. First, the HFM adopts the narrowest spread-quoting strategy, while HFIs determine to submit orders upon the arrival of news with latency δ. In certain scenarios, rational
HFIs may even submit orders on both trade sides of the market. Second, HFIs are incentivized to reduce their
latency δ to maximize their payoff expectancies, leading to a "high-frequency trading arms race". This behavior enhances the total market welfare but weakens market liquidity. Third, the latency parameter δ becomes
irrelevant in the multi-period batch model.
There are several research directions in which the model presented could be extended. An important avenue is to incorporate order cancellations into the complex strategic game. It would be necessary to introduce
cancellation penalties, as the absence of such restrictions could reintroduce opportunities for risk-free arbitrage.
In such scenarios, the HFM may be forced to bear higher adverse selection costs. Another promising direction
is comparing batch markets with continuous markets. When batch and continuous trading mechanisms coexist
within the same market, it raises questions about the potential for cross-mechanism arbitrage. Moreover, the
strategic interactions among HFTs become more complex. Does such arbitrage exist? How do strategic behaviors differ between mechanisms? Moreover, which market mechanism is likely to be dominant? Addressing
these issues will be a central focus of our future research.

Appendix C. The market liquidity equilibrium in the batch model
In the three complex games, there exists an implicit equilibrium: the market liquidity equilibrium. As is
well-established, trades can’t be executed in the absence of market liquidity. Consequently, determining how
market liquidity achieves the equilibrium is a critical issue in the batch model with limited multiple sequential
news.
In the batch model, there are three types of market participants: LTs, the HFM and HFIs. When the HFM
trade with an LT, the LT acts as the liquidity provider while the HFM acts as the liquidity consumer. When
an HFI snipes a quote, the HFM acts as the liquidity provider while the HFI acts as the liquidity consumer.
From a market-wide perspective: (i) LTs supply all of the liquidity. (ii) The HFM redistributes a portion of this
liquidity to HFIs while consuming a small fraction itself. (iii) HFIs solely consume the liquidity. In the market
liquidity equilibrium, the total liquidity supplied by LTs must equal the combined liquidity consumed by the
HFM and HFIs.
Several key factors influence market liquidity in the batch model:
1. The probability of LTs: When the probability of LTs increases, the market liquidity increases because
more liquidity providers enter the market. This explains why the equilibrium spread s∗decreases with
the increase in µ.
2. The probability of snipe: When the probability of snipe increases, the market liquidity decreases because
HFIs consume more liquidity by exploiting stale quotes. For instance, as more news arrives, HFIs can
more easily snipe mispriced quotes and consume more liquidity. Consequently, s∗increases with the
increase in λ. The impact of τ on the market liquidity can also be explained by this theoretical framework.
40

3. The competition of snipe: When the competition of snipe increases, the market liquidity increases because more investors tend to trade. In the batch model, an increase in N solely increases snipe competition without altering the total probability of snipe. Consequently, this leads to a decrease of the snipe
price, resulting in HFIs consuming less liquidity.
Consider an extreme scenario of no LT. Theoretically, in the absence of LTs, the market lacks liquidity
providers, preventing any trades. To verify this claim, we analyze the batch auction model under the no LT
scenario. As noted in remark 1 of the main paper, the payoff expectancies of HFIs are positive in this scenario,
whereas the HFM’s payoff expectancy is negative because the market is a zero-sum game. By substituting the
payoff expectancies into equation(2) in the main paper, we will derive that the s∗in equilibrium converges to
either 0 or σ, implying the payoff expectancies of all participants are 0. Consequently, no HFT has an incentive
to trade. This analysis demonstrates that LTs are essential for normal market functioning. If there is no LT,
a market-making subsidy must be introduced to incentivize the HFM to act as a liquidity provider, thereby
restoring market viability.

Zero-Fee Trading and Price Clustering in Cryptocurrency Pairs
Author 1a,∗, Author 2a,∗
aDepartment and Organization, City, Country
Abstract
This study analyzes the impact of zero-fee trading on price clustering in BTCBUSD and BTCUSDT cryptocurrency pairs using
one year of data. The study conﬁrms that observed frequency distributions deviate from a uniform distribution and that zero-fee
trading has a signiﬁcant eﬀect on the distribution of observed frequencies. The study also ﬁnds that price level and trading volume
positively correlate with clustering probability, while volatility has a negative correlation. These ﬁndings have implications for
market participants and policymakers and emphasizes the signiﬁcance of taking into account market conditions and transaction fees
while examining the clustering of prices in ﬁnancial markets.
Keywords: Price clustering, Zero-fee trading, Bitcoin, Cryptocurrency, Market micro-structure
1. Introduction
Price clustering is commonly associated with the price resolution [1] and negotiation hypotheses [2], as traders tend to
use price grids to reduce negotiation costs. Limited recall [3]
and the attraction hypothesis [4], have also been identiﬁed as
contributors to clustering. The presence of strategic trading [5]
also plays a role in the occurrence of price clustering. Studies
have found similar price clustering patterns in Bitcoin markets
[6–11], with prices clustering most at 00, followed by 99, 01, or
50. The distribution of numbers does not follow a uniform distribution, with certain numbers occurring more frequently than
expected.
This study explores the inﬂuence of zero-fee trading on price
clustering, providing new knowledge about the role of transaction fees in shaping market behavior. Results suggest that zerofee trading has a signiﬁcant impact on price clustering, highlighting the need to consider this factor in analyzing market dynamics. The research contributes to the current understanding
of cryptocurrency markets by investigating the eﬀects of zerofee trading on price clustering.
2. Data and Methodology
Binance had announced its zero-fee trading policy for speciﬁc trading pairs, which was set to commence on July 8th,
2022, at 14:00 (UTC) [12]. The trading pairs that have been
selected for this study are BTCBUSD and BTUSDT, both of
which exhibit high trading volumes and are traded against stablecoins, rather than ﬁat currencies. The dataset used in this
study comprises minute-by-minute trading data over a total of
one year, covering six months prior to and following the introduction of zero-fee trading (from January 9th, 2022, to January

the time. However, Chi-squared tests show that the observed
frequencies do not conform to a uniform distribution.

Table 3 shows a positive relationship between trading volume
and price clustering, contrary to the expected negative association according to the negotiation hypothesis. Higher liquidity
is generally expected to lead to a more eﬃcient market with a
ﬁner price grid and reduced price clustering [4]. One potential explanation is that larger trades may be executed at prices
that are more clustered [14]. However, our results are consistent with previous research on Bitcoin markets [6, 8], the price
variable was found to be positively related to clustering, while
diﬀering from the negotiation hypothesis and some prior studies. The study conducted by Ma (2022) [11] reported diﬀering
signs for the variable in various markets.
The study ﬁnds a signiﬁcant negative association between
volatility and price clustering in all models, contrary to what
is commonly observed in traditional ﬁnancial markets [2, 14].
The negative sign implies higher volatility leads to a lower
probability of observing price clustering. In Urquhart’s (2017)
study [6], volatility was insigniﬁcant, while Baig et al.’s (2019)
[8] found a positive relationship. Ma (2022) [11] showed varying eﬀects in diﬀerent markets, with diﬀerent directions.

Table 3 shows that the dummy variable for zero-fee trading periods is signiﬁcant in all models, except for the 6-month
model for round BTCBUSD prices. One could assume that
zero-fee trading periods would lead to an increase in strategic trading activity and therefore enhance clustering at speciﬁc
numbers. However, the variable has a negative eﬀect on the
predicted probability of clustering, which suggests that traders
use a ﬁner price grid and may have employed diﬀerent tactics
during zero-fee trading periods. This may have arisen from that
our selected dummy variable may be capturing the eﬀects of increased volume and decreased volatility in the zero-fee period.
However, the coeﬃcient is signiﬁcant and has a strong eﬀect
on the 1-month models as well, indicating an immediate impact on negotiations and price clustering. Baig et al. (2019) [8]
found a negative correlation between daily average transaction
fee and clustering, which supports our ﬁndings. This indicates
that transaction fees can signiﬁcantly impact trading behavior
and price clustering in ﬁnancial markets.
The current study has signiﬁcant implications for policymakers and market participants. Traders must take into consideration the impact of zero-fee trading on price clustering when
making trading decisions. Policymakers should also be mindful
of the potential impact of zero-fee trading on market eﬃciency
and integrity, as the study reveals that zero-fee trading can affect the frequency distribution of digits, thereby aﬀecting the
overall eﬀectiveness of the market. These ﬁndings indicate the
necessity for further research on the eﬀects of zero-fee trading
on market eﬃciency and integrity.
The study has a few limitations that future research should
address. Firstly, the analysis is based on a single cryptocurrency exchange, and it is uncertain whether the ﬁndings can be
generalized to other markets. Secondly, our study solely concentrated on investigating the inﬂuence of zero-fee trading on

price clustering, but we did not explore the underlying reasons
for this impact, including whether it is due to the change in

4. Conclusion
This study investigates how the price clustering in cryptocurrency trading pairs BTCBUSD and BTCUSDT is aﬀected
by the implementation of zero-fee trading, using a one-year
dataset. Results revealed that certain price levels, such as 00,
99, and 01, are observed more frequently than others, especially
before the implementation of zero-fee trading.
Chi-squared
tests conﬁrm that the observed frequency of the numbers is not
due to chance. Probit model estimations are then utilized, including variables such as the natural logarithm of the closing
price, trading volume, and the diﬀerence between the highest and lowest prices within 1-minute intervals, along with a
dummy variable to represent zero-fee trading periods. The ﬁndings suggest that price level and trading volume have a positive correlation with predicted probability of price clustering,
while volatility has a negative correlation. Furthermore, predicted probability of price clustering is found to be reduced signiﬁcantly due to the implementation of zero-fee trading. The
study highlights the importance of considering transaction fees
and other market conditions when analyzing price clustering in
ﬁnancial markets.

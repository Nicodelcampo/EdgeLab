Cross-Attention Latent Fusion: Integrating DeepSeek-R1 and
Mamba for Downside-Aware Quantitative Trading
Sélim JOMAA, Eve JOUNI
selim.jomaa@aivancity.education, eve.jouni@aivancity.education
Abstract
The intersection of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and Large Language Models
(LLMs) represents a promising frontier in quantitative finance. However, current hybrid
models frequently compress LLM outputs into scalar sentiment scores, discarding the
high-dimensional reasoning traces necessary for complex market analysis. In this paper, we
propose a novel Cross-Attention Latent Fusion architecture that directly integrates the
internal hidden states (1536-D) of a reasoning LLM (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) with
a Selective State Space Model (Mamba) encoding 30-day temporal price momentum. The
price momentum acts as a query to dynamically attend to contextual news embeddings.
Furthermore, we adapt Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for continuous
action spaces, combined with a Downside-Aware Policy Optimization (DAPO) reward
function, eliminating the need for a memory-intensive Critic network. Evaluated across 50
distinct equities from the FNSPID dataset, our latent fusion methodology demonstrates
improved risk-adjusted returns and maximum drawdown mitigation compared to unimodal
baselines, remaining feasible on consumer-grade hardware through targeted engineering
optimizations.

Introduction
Trading inherently relies on constantly fluctuating data driven by continuous market changes.
Interpreting the intersection of mathematical financial data and the continuous flow of market
news is highly challenging due to the massive volume and variable quality of the information.
Misinterpreting this data can lead to poor decision-making, which in turn can result in
significant financial losses. Therefore, it is crucial to find an optimal and coherent way to
combine quantitative market data with qualitative news.
Developing a trading model based solely on Reinforcement Learning (RL) provides strong
mathematical decision-making but lacks an understanding of the broader real-world context
[6]. Traditional RL algorithms such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Advantage
Actor-Critic (A2C), and Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) excel at processing
historical price data but possess a fundamental limitation: they do not account for real-world
information. Financial markets are highly sensitive to external events—a single news article
can cause the market to flip overnight. Relying solely on numerical data means the agent must
wait for a real-world event to eventually impact price numbers before reacting, introducing a
critical lag. Conversely, relying purely on LLMs offers a powerful semantic processor capable
of analyzing financial news, but it is not optimized for iterative, quantitative trading decisions.
1

Current LLM applications in finance remain quite basic. They are primarily used for
standard sentiment analysis, where the model’s only job is to categorize a news article as
either "positive" or "negative." This approach results in a massive loss of valuable information.
By reducing a complex financial article to a simple label, the broader context is completely
lost. The FinRL-DeepSeek baseline, while empirically useful, exemplifies this limitation: it
relies on Action Modulation (scaling continuous actions via sentiment scores) and Reward
Adjustment (penalizing risk via CVaR-PPO), but the agent remains fundamentally blind to
the underlying economic rationale driving the LLM’s assessment.
This paper proposes a hybrid architecture that merges numerical analysis from time-series
data with semantic analysis derived from the latent states of the LLM. Instead of simple
sentiment scoring, our approach extracts the hidden states from the LLM’s reasoning tokens
(such as <think>) to capture the full depth of the model’s contextual understanding. By
integrating historical prices with this deep semantic understanding, and fusing them via a
Cross-Attention mechanism, the model effectively guides the trading agent’s decisions.
To address current limitations in multimodal financial AI, we make the following core
contributions:
1. Latent-State Fusion: We extract the hidden states from the LLM’s reasoning tokens rather than generating text, preserving the full depth of the model’s contextual
understanding.
2. Asymmetric Cross-Attention: We design a fusion mechanism where historical price
embeddings serve as queries to attend to the LLM’s semantic key-value pairs.
3. Continuous GRPO: We adapt the Critic-free Group Relative Policy Optimization
(GRPO) algorithm—traditionally used for discrete tokens—to optimize a continuous
trading action space, drastically reducing VRAM overhead.
4. Downside-Aware Reward (DAPO): We introduce a reward function integrating
Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) to explicitly penalize extreme tail-losses during
training.

Related Work and Data Infrastructure
2.1
The FinRL-DeepSeek Baseline
The FinRL-DeepSeek project serves as the foundational baseline for integrating LLM-derived
signals into RL environments. It utilizes Action Modulation and Reward Adjustment via
CVaR-PPO. While empirically useful, the agent remains fundamentally blind to the underlying
economic rationale. By compressing complex financial reasoning into simplistic floating-point
sentiment scores, the framework discards contextual depth—the very semantic richness that
makes LLMs valuable for market analysis.
2.2
Reinforcement Learning in Finance
Traditional Reinforcement Learning algorithms, such as PPO [4], are widely used for developing
automated trading agents. PPO requires storing two complete neural networks in memory
2

during training: the Actor network and the Critic network.
When integrating massive
architectures like LLMs into an RL framework, maintaining both networks consumes an
excessive amount of Video RAM (VRAM), making training computationally unfeasible on
consumer-grade hardware. Recent advances like DeepSeekMath [3] introduced GRPO to solve
this by evaluating actions relative to a sampled group, bypassing the Critic entirely.
2.3
FNSPID Dataset and Real-Time Agentic Scraping
Training multimodal systems requires vast, temporally aligned corpora to prevent look-ahead
bias. We utilize the Financial News and Stock Price Integration Dataset (FNSPID) [7]. The
dataset comprises millions of time-aligned financial news records spanning from 1999 to 2023,
capturing decades of market sentiment, economic shifts, and company-specific events.
Our preprocessing pipeline was carefully designed to resolve data fusion challenges and
create a clean, training-ready environment:
• Targeting High-Density Stocks: The code filters the dataset to keep only the top
50 most mentioned stocks, guaranteeing that the RL agent trains on companies with
consistent media coverage.
• Temporal Alignment: News is published at all hours, but stock prices are recorded
once a day at market close; exact timestamps are normalized to the standard daily
format (YYYY-MM-DD).
• Handling Non-Trading Days: News published on weekends and holidays is matched
to market prices using a merge on ‘Date’ and ‘Stock_symbol’, followed by a sequence
alignment to ensure no look-ahead bias.
This rigorous preprocessing guarantees that every step in the RL environment contains a
perfectly aligned pair: a semantic news signal for the LLM and a valid closing price. The final
dataset used for training contains over 29,000 aligned rows after processing 100,000 raw news
records.
For live deployment, the architecture additionally supports multi-agent scraping frameworks
(e.g., LangGraph, CrewAI) to orchestrate real-time ingestion of X feeds and SEC EDGAR
filings, parsed into standardized markdown blocks.

Methodology: Cross-Attention Latent Fusion
Our proposed architecture bridges the semantic-numerical gap by treating the LLM not as
a text generator, but as a high-dimensional cognitive feature extractor. The full pipeline
comprises three interacting components: a temporal price encoder, an LLM latent extractor,
and a cross-attention fusion bridge.
3.1
The RL Environment (Gymnasium)
The foundation of the training process is a custom Gymnasium environment (DeepSeekTradingEnv)
designed specifically to handle multimodal inputs. The observation space is a composite dictionary containing two elements: a temporal series matrix of historical log-returns (30-day
3

window) and a semantic vector extracted from the LLM (1536 dimensions). The action space
is defined as a continuous variable ranging from −1.0 to 1.0, where a positive value indicates a
buy signal (the percentage of available cash to invest) and a negative value represents a sell
signal (the percentage of currently held assets to liquidate).
Each observation step provides three distinct signals to the agent:
• Time Index: Controls the environment’s timeline, moving the agent forward day by
day.
• Temporal Signal: Feeds the current price and the past 30 days of history into the
Mamba encoder as normalized log-returns.
• Semantic Signal: News headlines are passed to DeepSeek to extract hidden mathematical reasoning states, injecting textual understanding directly into the RL agent.
The environment natively simulates 10 bps transaction costs and 5 bps slippage per trade,
preventing the exploitation of micro-volatility and ensuring that the learned policies generalize
to real-market conditions.
3.2

4.2
Downside-Aware Policy Optimization (DAPO) Reward
Trading agents trained purely to maximize raw cumulative returns often develop highly volatile
strategies that eventually lead to catastrophic portfolio crashes. We designed a custom reward
function based on Downside-Aware Policy Optimization (DAPO). Over a rolling window of
the agent’s historical returns, the reward is defined as the Sharpe ratio heavily penalized by
the 5% Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR0.05):

the raw daily return is used as a warm-up reward. Because CVaR is intrinsically negative
(representing tail losses), the +2.0 × CVaR term actively penalizes the agent for exposing the
portfolio to severe drawdowns, forcing the Cross-Attention layer to learn the specific semantic
embeddings that precede market crashes.

Experiments and Results
5.1
Experimental Protocol
The simulation was designed to test the agent under highly stressful market conditions. The
architecture was trained using a single RTX 5070 Ti. To ensure hardware stability on the
Blackwell architecture, standard Math SDP was explicitly enforced. A 5-day sanity check
simulation was first conducted to validate the end-to-end data flow.
The RL agent was initialized with a starting capital of $100,000. Training utilized a Cosine
Annealing learning rate schedule starting at 1e −5, optimized via Adam. The model was
trained and evaluated iteratively across 50 distinct equities, ensuring robustness across diverse
market sectors. The backtest evaluation was performed on a held-out test split consisting of
the last 252 unique trading days per ticker, capturing extreme volatility events where daily
news had an immediate and drastic impact on stock prices.
5.2
Evaluation Metrics
To objectively evaluate the performance of the agent against baselines, we utilized five standard
quantitative metrics:
• Sharpe Ratio: Measures excess return per unit of total volatility.
• Maximum Drawdown (Max DD): The largest peak-to-trough drop in portfolio value,
measuring worst-case scenario risk.
• Sortino Ratio: Similar to Sharpe, but only penalizes downside volatility.
• Calmar Ratio: The annualized return divided by the absolute Maximum Drawdown.
A ratio > 1.0 indicates that returns justify the worst historical loss.
• Omega Ratio: A probability-weighted ratio of gains versus losses. A value > 1.0
confirms the strategy generates more upside mass than downside mass.

Discussion, Limitations, and Future Work
6.1
Computational Limitations
The primary limitation of this study stems from hardware constraints inherent to consumergrade GPUs.
While the theoretical framework is designed to use Mamba for advanced,
linear-time temporal encoding, compiling Mamba requires highly specific CUDA environments.
As a result, we relied on an S6 recurrence loop natively implemented in PyTorch. For a 30-day
window, this sequential loop is computationally negligible, but scaling to ultra-long sequences
(T > 1000) would necessitate optimized CUDA kernels. Furthermore, we utilized a distilled
version of DeepSeek (1.5B parameters). While effective for proof-of-concept, a smaller model
inevitably possesses less semantic depth than frontier models (32B+ parameters).
6.2
Future Work
Future research will focus on lifting these hardware restrictions to deploy the architecture at
its full theoretical capacity:
• Multi-Asset Portfolio Management via GNNs: The next major evolution is
scaling the environment by integrating Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) into the spatial
processing block to map complex correlations, dependencies, and ripple effects between
different companies and market sectors.
• Real-Time Agentic Deployment: The architecture supports live deployment via
multi-agent scraping frameworks (LangGraph, CrewAI) for real-time ingestion of X
8

(formerly Twitter) feeds and SEC EDGAR filings, enabling the system to operate as a
fully autonomous trading agent.
• End-to-End Retraining Ablations: Future systematic ablation studies will involve
end-to-end retraining of isolated baselines for stricter statistical validation.

Conclusion
This paper demonstrates that relying solely on numerical price data or purely on textual
analysis is insufficient to navigate the complexities of modern financial markets. We introduced
a hybrid architecture that successfully merges quantitative market trends with qualitative
real-world context. By extracting semantic reasoning directly from the latent hidden states
of DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and fusing them with numerical price signals via a CrossAttention mechanism, the agent achieves a much deeper understanding of market dynamics.
The adaptation of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to a continuous action
space enabled highly memory-efficient training by eliminating the Critic network, overcoming
hardware constraints typically associated with running large models in RL. The DAPO reward
function successfully enforced downside protection by penalizing tail losses through CVaR,
producing a policy that balances growth with risk management rather than chasing raw returns.
Ultimately, this multimodal approach suggests that blending mathematical precision with
LLM-processed news signals offers a highly promising and scalable pathway for developing the
next generation of automated, context-aware trading agents.

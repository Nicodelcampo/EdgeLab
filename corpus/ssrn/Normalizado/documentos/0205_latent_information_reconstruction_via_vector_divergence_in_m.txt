Abstract
Quantitative finance models treat microstructure data (order books, trade flows) and public
data (news, market sentiment) as independent sources. This separation fails to capture a welldocumented phenomenon: markets regularly price in information before it becomes publicly
available. This paper proposes a methodology based on vector divergence between these two
streams. By projecting order book state and public narrative consensus into a shared vector
space, we define a metric D(t) that measures the gap between market behavior and public
information in real time. A sharp increase in D(t) signals an active informational gap between
market behavior and public consensus. The architecture combines a microstructural encoder, a
semantic encoder (NLP) and a vector database for storage, historical precedent search and realtime inference. It builds on Bouchaud’s work on market endogeneity and on Hawkes processes
for noise filtering. We validate the approach on two real events, GameStop (January 2021) and
Terra/LUNA (May 2022). In both cases, the divergence proxy crosses the detection threshold
36 to 48 hours before extreme price movements of 9.4 and 13.1 standard deviations respectively,
confirming that latent information was present in the microstructure (or its on-chain equivalent)
but absent from public consensus.
This paper is part of an active R&D program. It presents the theoretical framework and preliminary empirical
validation. Certain implementation details are reserved for future work. Full pipeline deployment and out-ofsample results will be reported in subsequent publications.

I. Introduction
1.1. The Problem
On January 10, 2024, social media and financial outlets were buzzing about the imminent approval of the first spot Bitcoin ETFs by the SEC. Public sentiment, measured by aggregating
Twitter, Reddit and news headline flows, reached a historically euphoric level. Classical sentiment analysis models unanimously predicted a rally.
Yet the order book told a very different story. In the hours preceding the official announcement,
microstructure data (bid/ask imbalance, book depth, order cancellation dynamics) signaled a
silent distribution. Large limit orders were accumulating on the sell side, the spread was widening
and net institutional flow was turning negative. The market, in its internal mechanics, did not
share the enthusiasm of the public consensus.
A few days later, the market corrected sharply. The information that certain market participants
held, and that the microstructure had encoded, eventually became public.
The actual ETF
conditions were less favorable than anticipated and the first post-launch sessions saw massive
capital outflows from the Grayscale Bitcoin Trust.
This scenario is not an isolated case. It illustrates a structural phenomenon well documented
in the microstructure literature [4]. Certain order flows, originating from participants with an
informational or structural advantage, move the price toward its fundamental value before the
underlying information becomes public.
Existing approaches treat these two information sources separately. On one side, microstructure
models (order book analysis, impact modeling, Hawkes processes) focus on the internal dynamics
of flows without reference to the public informational context. On the other, sentiment analysis
models (NLP on news and social media) extract the narrative consensus without comparing it
to the actual behavior of the market. Each of these approaches, taken in isolation, is blind to
the following question: is there, at time t, a significant gap between what the market integrates
in its flows and what public information reflects?
It is this gap, which we call the informational gap, that this paper proposes to formalize, measure
and exploit.
1.2. Proposition
Our approach rests on three principles.
The first is that the market encodes latent information in its microstructure. Order flows, the
book profile and cancellation patterns form a high-dimensional space that reflects the actual
informational state of participants, including information not yet reflected in consensus. This
idea builds on the theoretical framework of Bouchaud (market endogeneity, latent liquidity) and
on Hawkes processes (self-excitation and long memory of flows).
The second is that public data encodes the narrative consensus. The aggregation of news, social
media posts and analyst reports forms a vector that represents what the market is supposed to
know at time t.
The third is that the measurable divergence between these two representations constitutes the
signal. By projecting these two heterogeneous streams into a shared vector space, we compute
D(t) = 1 −sim

It is important to emphasize that this metric does not predict price direction. It detects the

1.3. Architecture and Contribution
To operationalize this approach, we propose a three-layer architecture. The first is a multimodal
embedding layer where a microstructural encoder and a semantic encoder project heterogeneous
data into a shared vector space of dimension d. The second is a vector storage and search layer
where a vector database stores the history of embeddings, computes D(t) in real time and enables
historical precedent search via Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN). The third is an inference
layer where a detection engine emits signals when D(t) exceeds a calibrated threshold, enriched
by context from similar past cases.
The paper is structured as follows. Section II establishes the theoretical framework of microstructure based on Bouchaud and Hawkes processes, then formalizes the metric D(t). Section III
details the technical architecture. Section IV presents a preliminary validation on real data.
Section V concludes.
II. Microstructure and Market Behavior
2.1. Market Endogeneity
To understand why the order book encodes latent information, one must first identify what
else it encodes that does not constitute information. This is the fundamental contribution of
Jean-Philippe Bouchaud’s work on market endogeneity.
The central observation is that the vast majority of price movements are not caused by any new
external information. By systematically analyzing intraday price jumps and cross-referencing
them with synchronized news feeds, Bouchaud [1] shows that more than 90% of significant
movements have no identifiable exogenous cause. Volatility is therefore not the reflection of a
news flow but the product of the market’s internal mechanics.
This mechanics rests on three interdependent phenomena. The first is the long memory of order
flow. The sign of trades (+1 buy, −1 sell) is strongly autocorrelated over time, with a power-law
decay C(ℓ) ∼ℓ−γ where γ < 1. This persistence reflects the fragmentation of meta-orders by
institutions, which split their orders into hundreds of small slices to navigate displayed liquidity
that represents only a fraction of actual demand.
The second is transient impact. Each trade impacts the price, but this impact is not permanent.
The market develops a resilience by which a buy order following a series of buys has reduced
impact, as book liquidity adapts to absorb the predictable trend. This mechanism allows the
market to remain efficient despite order flow predictability.
The third is the disparity between displayed and latent liquidity. The visible liquidity at the Best
Bid/Ask often represents less than 1% of actual institutional demand. This structural disparity
places the system near a critical point where a small perturbation in the flow can trigger a
disproportionate reaction without any fundamental news [11].
The operational consequence of these observations is direct. If 90% of book activity is endogenous
noise (cascades, aftershocks, liquidity adjustments), the remaining 10% carries high informational
value. But to isolate it, one must first model and filter the noise.

representation of vmarket(t) in the latent space.
This coupling between the physics of microstructure (Hawkes on the point process) and the
geometry of the latent space (embeddings on enriched features) constitutes the methodological
originality of our approach.

III. Data Science and Architecture
Section II established why the market encodes latent information and how the metric D(t)
allows its detection.
This section presents the design principles of the architecture and the
methodological choices underlying it. In keeping with the R&D nature of this work, certain
implementation details (calibration hyperparameters, proprietary training procedures, internal
pipeline specifications) are intentionally omitted to preserve the competitive advantage of the
methodology. We present the general framework with sufficient granularity to allow scientific
evaluation of the reasoning, while reserving operational components for internal deployment.
3.1. Multimodal Embedding Strategy
The fundamental challenge is to project data of radically different nature (numerical and temporal
for the microstructure, textual and semantic for public data) into a shared vector space where
cosine similarity is meaningful.
3.1.1. Microstructural Encoder
The raw input is an order book snapshot at time t, represented as a vector Xmarket(t) ∈RD
including prices and volumes across N depth levels (bid and ask), bid/ask imbalance (Order
Book Imbalance), effective spread, order cancellation and modification rate, and net signed flow
aggregated over a sliding window.
In parallel, the point process of trades is processed by Hawkes de-clustering (cf. Section 2.3.5),
which produces the exogeneity score ρ(t) = ˆµ(t)/λ(t). This score is integrated into the LOB
feature vector to form the enriched input of the encoder:

The encoder Eθ is a deep neural network that compresses the high dimension of the enriched
LOB into a latent space of dimension d.
The dimension d results from a trade-off between
loss of fine structures (dimension too low) and degradation of cosine similarity discriminance in
high dimension (curse of dimensionality). The score ρ(t) functions as an exogeneity indicator:
when it is high, the encoder learns to assign more weight to the directional features of the LOB
(imbalance, net flow), which then carry informative signal rather than cascade noise.

Public data (financial news, tweets, Reddit posts, analyst reports) are aggregated by time window

but misses financial nuance. A specialized model such as FinBERT [8] is preferable because it
distinguishes bullish from bearish tone in sector jargon and produces embeddings whose geometry
reflects financial relationships.
The temporal aggregation strategy is an important design parameter. A window that is too short
captures media noise, while one that is too long dilutes punctual signals. We use a weighted
aggregation that overweights high-authority sources (Bloomberg, Reuters) and attenuates noise
from viral sources.

3.1.3. Space Alignment
The two encoders produce vectors in spaces that are a priori incomparable. A vector from a
numerical encoder and a vector from a textual transformer have no reason to share a common
geometry. To solve this problem, we use a shared projection layer trained by contrastive learning,
in the spirit of Radford et al. [9] on multimodal alignment.
The construction of training pairs relies on a market proxy observable a posteriori, which eliminates any circularity (we do not use D(t) to construct the labels for D(t)). The labeling strategy
distinguishes alignment cases (the market and public consensus converge toward the same outcome) from divergence cases (the observed outcome contradicts consensus), and calibrates the
encoder geometry accordingly. The details of pair construction and the contrastive loss function
fall under proprietary methodology.
Contrastive training forces both encoders to produce geometrically compatible representations, so
that cosine similarity between vmarket and vpublic effectively measures the degree of informational
alignment.
3.2. Vector Database
Once embeddings are produced, they must be stored, indexed and queried at scale.
3.2.1. Justification
Computing D(t) at a given instant is trivial (a dot product). But the system has three needs
that go beyond point computation.
The first is historical persistence. Each triplet (vmarket(t), vpublic(t), D(t)) is stored with its metadata (timestamp, asset, price, contemporaneous volatility). This history serves both as a training
corpus for the encoders and as a calibration base for detection thresholds.
The second is real-time precedent search. When D(t) rises, it is useful to identify analogous
historical cases.
For this, we construct a composite context vector that jointly encodes the
microstructural profile, the public profile and the degree of divergence.
It is this composite
vector, not vmarket or vpublic in isolation, that is indexed in the vector DB and serves as the
key for ANN search. The exact structure and dimensionality of this context vector fall under
proprietary methodology. An ANN query retrieves in milliseconds the k historical cases closest
to the current case.
The third is scalability. In crypto, one LOB snapshot per second generates approximately 86,400
vectors per day per asset. Over one year with 20 assets, this reaches approximately 630 million
vectors. Modern vector DBs (Milvus, Qdrant, Weaviate) are designed to index and query billions
of vectors with sub-millisecond latencies through HNSW [10] or IVF indexes.

3.3.2. Ingestion
The pipeline must reconcile two different data frequencies. Order book data arrives continuously
(hundreds to thousands of events per second) and is ingested via a Kafka-type message bus
that guarantees strict ordering and resilience. News, tweets and posts are collected at regular
intervals (from one second to one minute depending on the source) via a dedicated ETL module
that normalizes formats and deduplicates sources.
Synchronization between the two streams is a delicate point. The market encoder updates vmarket(t)
at high frequency (every 1 to 5 minutes), while the public encoder refreshes vpublic(t) at lower
frequency (every 10 to 15 minutes), constrained by the intrinsic granularity of textual sources.
We use a carry-forward strategy in which vpublic(t) is held constant between two updates. D(t)
is recomputed at each update of vmarket, so that variations of D(t) between two public refreshes
exclusively reflect microstructure changes. This behavior is desirable: if the market begins to
diverge from the last known public consensus, D(t) increases even in the absence of new public
information, which corresponds precisely to the signature of a silent informational gap.
3.3.3. Stratified Storage
The pipeline uses two complementary storage levels. The hot layer (Vector DB) contains recent
embeddings and metadata, indexed for real-time ANN search.
The cold layer (Data Lake)
contains raw data (complete LOB snapshots, original texts) on object storage, intended for
model retraining and result reproduction. This separation maintains sub-millisecond latencies
on the operational layer while preserving full traceability.

9
3.3.4. Inference
The inference engine operates in three steps at each interval ∆t. It first produces D(t) from the
current embeddings, then compares D(t) to a dynamic threshold τ(t) calibrated on the recent
historical distribution of D. Finally, if D(t) > τ(t), it launches an ANN query to contextualize
the signal with historical precedents.
The threshold τ(t) is recomputed as a quantile of the sliding distribution of D (for example
the 95th percentile over the past 30 days). This choice avoids false positives during periods of
high structural volatility, where baseline divergence is naturally higher, and improves sensitivity
during calm periods.
IV. Experimental Validation
To subject the prediction formulated in Section 2.3.4 to an empirical test, we construct a proxy
reconstruction of D(t) on two real market events, chosen for their exhaustive public documentation and for the observable divergence between market behavior and the public narrative. The
first case concerns traditional equity markets (GameStop, January 2021), the second decentralized finance (Terra/LUNA, May 2022). In each case, we show that microstructure data (or their
on-chain equivalent in DeFi) encoded information absent from public consensus, and that this
divergence preceded an abnormal price movement.
4.1. Protocol
4.1.1. Proxy Reconstruction of D(t)
In the absence of a full deployment of the pipeline described in Section III, we construct a
proxy of the divergence metric from publicly verifiable data.
For each case, we identify the
observables that would respectively feed the microstructural encoder and the semantic encoder,
then we estimate the qualitative direction of vmarket(t) and vpublic(t) in the latent space. The
proxy divergence ˆD(t) is computed as the degree of directional misalignment between these two
normalized feature vectors.
This approach does not replace a full deployment, but it allows us to verify that the signals that
would feed the system are indeed present in the historical data and that their cross-referencing
produces a divergence signal prior to the price movement.
4.1.2. Metrics
For each case, we measure the abnormal return rt+∆t at several horizons (1 h, 4 h, 24 h, 72 h),
the lead time between the first spike of ˆD(t) above the threshold and the price movement, and
the ratio |r|/σ relative to pre-event historical volatility (30-day window).
4.2. Case A: GameStop (GME), January 2021
4.2.1. Context
In January 2021, GameStop Corp. (GME) was the target of a coordinated buying campaign by
members of the Reddit forum r/WallStreetBets, aiming to trigger a short squeeze on a stock
whose short interest reached 140% of the float [12]. The episode is documented in detail by the
SEC report and by numerous academic studies.

4.2.2. Data
Public flow (vpublic component). Sentiment on r/WallStreetBets, Twitter and financial media
is massively bullish between January 13 and January 27. On January 26, Elon Musk tweets
“Gamestonk!!” with a link to the subreddit, amplifying the euphoria. Press headlines unanimously relay the “small investor versus Wall Street” narrative. The vector vpublic(t) points in a
strongly bullish direction throughout the period.
Microstructure (vmarket component). Market data tells a different story. First, between 50%
and 60% of GME volume is routed through dark pools (FINRA data), out of the view of retail
investors. Second, the bid/ask spread widens significantly from January 25 onward, going from
a few cents to several dollars on lit exchanges. Third, DTCC (Depository Trust & Clearing
Corporation) margin requirements increase from $26 million to $3.7 billion on the morning of
January 28, reflecting a systemic risk assessment by clearing houses. Fourth, the bid/ask imbalance (OBI) on lit exchanges progressively shifts into negative territory (net selling pressure)
from January 26 onward, even as the price continues to rise, driven by retail flow.

4.3. Case B: Terra/LUNA (UST), May 2022

4.3.4. Divergence Reconstruction
The specificity of the DeFi case is that the microstructure is entirely on-chain and therefore
reconstructible a posteriori with precision.
On April 3, an anomalous UST sale (event S1) is detected retrospectively in on-chain data.
Public sentiment is still at its peak (UST is about to become the 3rd largest stablecoin). ˆD(t)
rises briefly to 0.42 then falls back.
On April 19, a second anomalous event S2 occurs.
ˆD(t) reaches 0.48. These two events are
invisible to the general public because they produce no visible price movement. Only an onchain analysis of wallet patterns detects them.
Between May 1 and May 5, the switch to Anchor’s dynamic rate triggers accelerated withdrawals
(2.75 billion dollars in a few days). Public sentiment remains positive (Do Kwon reassures), but
on-chain flow shows a capital flight. ˆD(t) oscillates between 0.35 and 0.50.
May 7 marks the breaking point. Two large addresses withdraw 375 million UST from Anchor
simultaneously [13]. Meanwhile, the composition of the Curve 3pool (a key indicator of stablecoin
health) shifts: the share of UST in the pool exceeds 80%, revealing massive selling pressure.
Public sentiment reacts only partially (the depeg to $0.987 is perceived as temporary).
ˆD(t)
jumps to 0.71.
On May 8, Do Kwon tweets “deploying more capital” and UST temporarily recovers its peg
at $0.995. Public sentiment stabilizes. But on-chain data continues to show massive Anchor
withdrawals (from 14 B to 12 B). ˆD(t) only falls to 0.55, signaling that divergence persists
despite the apparent calm.
On May 9, the collapse materializes. Anchor loses an additional 3.3 billion in one day (TVL
from 12 B to 8.7 B). UST drops to $0.35. The algorithmic death spiral (massive LUNA minting
to attempt peg restoration) takes LUNA from $30 to below $1 in 48 hours.

4.4.2. Prediction Validation
In both cases, the condition ˆD(t) > τ (with τ ≈0.60) is satisfied before the price movement, and
the abnormal return that follows far exceeds the threshold k = 2 (respectively 9.4σ and 13.1σ).
The inequality formulated in Section 2.3.4 is therefore verified on these two events.
More importantly, in both cases, the information that caused the crash existed in the microstructure data (or on-chain) but not in the public consensus. For GME, the rise in DTCC margin
requirements and the accumulation of dark pool volume signaled a systemic risk invisible to retail
investors. For Terra, the early whale withdrawals and the Curve 3pool imbalance revealed a loss
of confidence among sophisticated actors even as the founder publicly reassured. In both cases,
cross-referencing the two data streams (market + public) would have produced an exploitable
divergence signal, which is precisely what the architecture proposed in Section III is designed to
detect.
4.4.3. DeFi Generalization
The Terra case illustrates an important methodological point. In DeFi, the functional equivalent of the order book is on-chain data (TVL, pool composition, wallet movements). The microstructural encoder described in Section 3.1.1 can be adapted to ingest these features instead
of LOB snapshots, without architectural modification of the pipeline. Blockchain transparency
even makes certain signals more precise than on traditional markets, where the dark pool is by
definition opaque.
4.5. Limits and Discussion
4.5.1. Limits
We identify four main limitations. The first is that this validation relies on a proxy reconstruction
and not on a full pipeline deployment. The values of ˆD(t) are qualitative estimates based on
verifiable observables, but they do not result from effective contrastive training of the encoders.
A full deployment is necessary to precisely quantify thresholds and conditional ratios.
The second is retrospective selection bias. The two cases chosen are major events where divergence is, by construction, visible a posteriori. The value of the system lies in its ability to detect
lower-amplitude divergences in real time, which these case studies do not directly test.
The third is the dependence on the quality of contrastive alignment between the two encoders. A
poorly calibrated alignment would produce meaningless divergences, making periodic retraining
indispensable.
The fourth is the risk of false positives during regime transitions. During structural changes

14
(transition from a bull to a bear market), the geometric relationship between embedding spaces
may temporarily shift, generating spurious signals.
4.5.2. Robustness and Extensions
The two cases cover different asset classes (US equity, DeFi), different market mechanisms (centralized order book vs. on-chain AMM) and different types of hidden information (regulatory vs.
structural). This diversity suggests that the proposed framework possesses a certain transferability, subject to adapting the microstructural encoder to the specificities of each market. Three
extensions are envisaged: testing on a larger corpus of lower-amplitude events, full pipeline
deployment on live data, and coupling with a reinforcement learning agent for execution optimization.
V. Conclusion
This paper lays the foundations of an approach to latent information detection in financial
markets.
Rather than predicting price direction, we propose to detect the very presence of
asymmetric information by measuring the divergence between market behavior and publicly
available information.
The contributions are threefold. The first is the divergence metric D(t), a testable and interpretable formalization of informational asymmetry based on the geometry of a shared vector
space. The second is the Vector DB-centric architecture, a hybrid pipeline where the vector
database ensures not only storage but also inference, memory and contextualization. The third
is the Hawkes-Embeddings coupling, which integrates endogenous filtering upstream of vector
projection and ensures that the measured divergence reflects informative signal rather than microstructural noise.
The experimental validation, based on two real cases covering distinct asset classes (US equity
and DeFi), confirms the central prediction of this work. In both cases, the proxy divergence
ˆD(t) exceeds the threshold τ ≈0.60 between 36 and 48 hours before price movements of 9.4σ
and 13.1σ respectively.
The structural signals (DTCC regulatory constraints for GameStop,
institutional capital flight for Terra/LUNA) were present in the microstructure (or its on-chain
equivalent) but absent from public consensus, which is precisely the gap that D(t) is designed
to detect.

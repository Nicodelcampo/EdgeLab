Pairs Trading Strategy Based on Cointegration
Model and ECM

Abstract
I present a proposition for an ECM-based pairs trading strategy and conduct
an extensive and rigorous examination of the performance of two distinct pairs
trading strategies - the cointegration method and the ECM method - across the
entire Chinese equity market spanning from 2010 to 2023. For both the cointegration and ECM methods, I devise a computationally efficient two-step pairs
trading approach. In terms of economic outcomes, the cointegration and ECM
methods exhibit a mean monthly excess return of 57 and 67 basis points respectively. Regarding sustained profitability, the frequency of trading opportunities
via the cointegration and ECM methods experiences a significant reduction during the period from 2016 to 2018, likely due to the stable nature of the sluggish
bull stock market in that period. However, from 2019 onwards, this frequency
has been on an upward trajectory. Additionally, the ECM method demonstrates
superior performance in terms of mean monthly excess, final values, and Sharpe
ratio for trades involving short-term equilibrium relationships, when compared
to the cointegration method. Notably, the ECM method consistently maintains
a high Sharpe ratio of around 1.5, in contrast to the 0.75 observed in the cointegration method.

Introduction
A paired trading strategy (PTS) is a market-neutral approach that seeks to capitalize
on the price relationship between the purchase of one stock and the short sale of another
stock in order to generate returns. This strategy typically focuses on two highly correlated stocks, such as those belonging to the same industry or market segment. PTS
tends to perform Ill during periods of heightened market volatility. In practice, PTS can
be implemented through various methods, including statistical arbitrage, fundamental
analysis, or technical analysis.
Initially, due to restrictions on short selling, PTS only existed in theory within the
Chinese stock market. However, with the introduction and rapid development of the
Chinese margin trading market in 2010, the short-selling mechanism became accessible
to investors, providing a new foundation for paired trading in the Chinese stock market. From the perspective of investors, paired trading can effectively mitigate market
risks by establishing both long and short positions in stocks, enabling them to achieve

stable returns. From the perspective of the market as a whole, moderate statistical arbitrage through paired trading can reduce market volatility, enhance resource allocation
efficiency, and diminish systemic risks.
There are various approaches to pair trading, as classified by Krauss (2017) into
five categories: the minimum distance method, cointegration method, stochastic control method, time series method, and others (such as machine learning and principal
component analysis). Pair trading strategies consist of two stages: first, the method
used to identify suitable pairs, and second, the criteria for opening and closing positions. The cointegration-based pair trading strategy revolves around the long-term
equilibrium relationship between paired stock prices.
My study presents a novel paired trading strategy based on the Error Correction
Model (ECM), which captures opportunities for returns arising from the short-term
volatility relationship between paired stock prices. I compare this strategy with the
paired trading strategy based on cointegration relationships.
2
Literature review
The research on paired trading strategies (PTS) falls under the financial category of
”statistical arbitrage.” Statistical arbitrage refers to strategies that utilize statistical
models or methods to exploit potential mispricing between assets while maintaining
market neutrality.
One of the earliest comprehensive studies on pairs trading is conducted by Gatev
et al. (2006). They examine the most commonly used and simplest method of pairs
trading, known as DM, using CRSP stocks from 1962 to 2002. The strategy yields a
monthly excess return of 1.3% (before transaction costs) for the top five unrestricted
pairs, and 1.4% for the top 20 pairs. This study provides an unbiased assessment of the
strategy’s performance and considers the simplest method employed by practitioners
as a PTS. The authors address concerns regarding data mining by re-evaluating the
original strategy after four years and demonstrating its ongoing profitability.
Cointegration can be employed to implement PTS. Vidyamurthy (2004) presents a
theoretical framework for pairs trading using cointegration based on the error correction model (ECM) representation of cointegrated series proposed by Engle and Granger
(1987). Huck (2015) implement PTS using the cointegration method, specifically focusing on S&P 500 and Nikkei 225 stocks, and they show that it generates positive
returns. Bogomolov (2011) examines the performance of three different PTS methods:
DM, cointegration, and stochastic spread strategies, testing them on the Australian
share market from 1996 to 2010. The study finds that while all three trading strategies
are profitable before transaction costs, a significant portion of the profits diminishes
after considering costs and liquidity issues. Caldeira and Moura (2013) also employ a
cointegration-based trading strategy on the Sao Paulo exchange, indicating a 16.38%
annual excess return and a Sharpe ratio of 1.34 from 2005 to 2012. Lin et al. (2006)
emphasize the use of cointegration as a model capable of capturing the long-term price
spread equilibrium, overcoming the limitations of simpler statistical techniques such
as correlation and regression analysis. Galenko et al. (2012) implement a PTS based
on cointegration and examine its performance with four exchange-traded funds. However, these studies share a common limitation in terms of empirical evidence supporting

cointegration-based PTS, with either non-existent or limited analysis. For instance, the
strategy proposed by Vidyamurthy (2004) is not analyzed using real data, and Caldeira
and Moura (2013) utilize data from the Sao Paulo stock exchange for a period of fewer
than seven years.
Building upon the cointegration relationship of paired stocks, My study further
incorporates the ECM to capture the short-term volatility relationship of paired stock
prices. I utilize the ECM to reconstruct paired trading strategies, proposing ECMbased paired trading strategies that offer new research perspectives and directions for
academia.

Data
My dataset encompasses daily data of stocks eligible for securities lending in the Shanghai, Shenzhen, and Beijing stock markets of China. The data spans from 1 April 2010
to 1 April 2023, covering a sample period of approximately 3100 days (156 months).
Within this dataset, I have information on 3237 individual stocks. The data for the
securities lending targets is sourced from the Choice financial terminal1, while the stock
data is obtained from the Tushare financial data source2.
4
Research method
Pairs trading is an investment strategy that is categorized as either mean-reverting or
contrarian. This strategy operates on the premise of a specific price relationship existing
betIen two securities. By capitalizing on short-term deviations, pairs trading allows for
the simultaneous purchase of undervalued securities and short selling of overvalued
ones. When the price relationship is restored, the two positions are closed or reversed,
resulting in a realized profit. In My research, I evaluate the performance of three distinct
pairs trading strategies using the Choice and Tushare databases, which contain data on
Chinese stocks from 2010 to 2023. Each strategy is executed over a six-month trading
period, utilizing parameters estimated during the preceding 12-month formation period.
The strategies are run every six months, resulting in one portfolio at the end of each
period.
By leveraging the concept of cointegration, which implies that certain time series
maintain a long-term equilibrium and any deviations from this equilibrium are only
caused by random fluctuations, I can incorporate the mean-reverting characteristic into
pairs trading (Vidyamurthy, 2004). Through the two-step Engle-Granger method (Engle and Granger, 1987), I identify pairs of securities that exhibit cointegration and
extract their stationary spread. Any deviations from this spread are considered temporary and can be utilized to initiate long and short positions. In section 4.1, I provide
elaborate details on the cointegration model employed in my pairs trading strategy.
Additionally, I incorporate the error correction model (ECM) into pairs trading.
Similar to the cointegration method, I first use the Engle-Granger approach to select
pairs of cointegrated securities. I then extract their stationary spread. However, the

4.1
The cointegration method
4.1.1
Framework
A non-stationary time series Xt is called I(1) if its first difference forms a stationary
process, i.e.
I(0).
Consider X1,t and X2,t to be two I(1) time series.
If exists a
linear combination of the two time series that is stationary, X1,t and X2,t are said to
be cointegrated. Thus, X1,t and X2,t are cointegrated if there exists a non-zero real
number β0 and β1 such that:
X2,t −β1X1,t −β0 = ut
(1)
where β1 is the cointegration coefficient and ut is a zero-mean stationary series known
as the cointegration errors. β1 can been considered as the long-term elasticity between
X1,t and X2,t.
Vidyamurthy (2004) provides a basic framework to apply cointegration to pairs
trading. The cointegration relation shows the deviation of the process from its longterm equilibrium. The spread series is defined as the scale difference in the price of two
stocks:
spreadt = X2,t −β1X1,t −β0
(2)
Assume that I buy one share of stock 2 and sell short β1 share of stock 1 at time
t −1. X1,t and X2,t represent the price series of stocks 1 and 2, respectively. The profit
of this trade at time t, is given by:
(X2,t −X2,t−1) −β1(X1,t −X1,t−1)
By rearranging the above equation I get:
(X2,t −X2,t−1) −β1(X1,t −X1,t−1 = spreadt −spreadt−1)
Thus, the profit of buying one share of stock 2 and selling β1 share of stock 1 for the
period ∆t is given by the change in the spread for that period. From Equation (1),
the spread is stationary by definition and exhibits mean-reverting properties. I can use
this to construct a quantitative trading strategy that uses deviations from the longterm equilibrium of a cointegrated pair to open long and short positions. Positions are
unwound once the equilibrium is restored, as a consequence of being-stationary.
4.2
Trading strategy
I use two criteria to implement the pairs selection phase of the cointegration PTS.
First, I sort all possible combinations of pairs based on their SSD in their normalized
price during the formation period. Second, I test each of the pairs with the least sum

the formation period. Pairs that are not cointegrated are eliminated in the selection
process. Pairs that are cointegrated will have their cointegration coefficient estimated.
I continue until 20 cointegrated pairs with minimum SSD are selected to be traded in
the following trading period.
I use the two-step Engle-Granger approach (Engle and Granger, 1987) to test for
the existence of cointegration betIen nominated pairs and to estimate cointegration
coefficient β1. In this procedure, the cointegration regression is estimated using OLS
in the first step and the error correction model (ECM) is estimated in the second step.
For each nominated pair, I then form the spread defined in Equation (2) and calculate
the spread’s mean µe and standard deviation σe, with the data of the formation period.
These parameters are used in the trading period as trades’ open and close triggers.

4.3
The ECM method
4.3.1
Framework
Using the Granger’s theorem (Engle and Granger, 1987), the cointegration relationship
can be equivalently shown in an Error Correction Model framework (ECM) (Vidyamurthy, 2004). Based on ECM, the cointegrated series exhibits long-term equilibrium
and , while short-term deviations from this equilibrium can occur, they will be corrected, through time, by the error term in the ECM. The ECM representation of the
cointegration relationship between time series X1,t and X2,t is:
∆X2,t = α∆X1,t −λecmt−1 + vt
(4)
where ecmt = X2,t −β1X1,t −β0 which is exactly the cointegration errors and vt is a
zero-mean stationary series. Equation (2) shows the evolution of a time series in short
term and α can been considered as the short-term elasticity between X1,t and X2,t.
I provide a basic framework to apply ECM model not just cointegration to pairs
trading. The ECM model shows the deviation of the process from its short-term equilibrium. The short-spread series of two stocks is defined as below:
spread.normalizedt = ∆X2,t −α∆X1,t + λecmt−1
(5)

4.3.2
Trading strategy
As I know ECM model is based on cointegration. Thus, the pairs selection phase in
ECM PTS is the same as that in cointegration PTS.
I still use the two-step Engle-Granger approach (Engle and Granger, 1987) to test for
the existence of cointegration between nominated pairs and to estimate cointegration
coefficient β1 and α in Equation (4). For each nominated pair, I then form the shortspread defined in Equation (5) and calculate the short-spread’s mean µs and standard

trading period as trades’ open and close triggers. spread.shortt ∼I(0), with mean µs
and standard deviation σs.

diverges beyond 2. By construction, if the (λspread −µs)/σs drops below -2, I buy 1
dollar worth of stock 2 and sell short α dollar worth of stock 1. Equivalently, I sell short
1/α dollar worth of stock 2 and buy 1 dollar worth of stock 1, when the (λspread−µs)/σs
moves above the +2 threshold. I close both positions once the (λspread −µs)/σs to
(−2, 2), which translates into the pair returning to their short-term equilibrium. The
pair is again monitored for other potential round-trip trades for the remainder of the
trading period.

I execute the PTS each month and do not wait for a trading period to be complete,
resulting in six overlapping ‘portfolios’ each month. The monthly excess return of a

the trades neither necessarily open at the beginning of the trading period nor close

In the cointegration and ECM method, by definition, long and short positions are not
valued equally. However, since I have designed the method to ensure a $1 long position
for every trade, I assume an average $1 value for each long–short position.

Conclusion
Pairs trading is a well-known trading strategy that aims to generate profits regardless
of market conditions.
It originated in the 1980s, pioneered by traders Gerry Bamberger and the quantitative trading group led by Nunzio Tartaglia at Morgan Stanley
(Bookstaber, 2007). Long-Term Capital Management (LTCM), prior to its downfall,
also employed pairs trading strategies (Lowenstein, 2001). While early studies such as
Gatev et al. (2006) found that a simplistic pairs trading strategy was profitable from
1962 to 2002, subsequent research by Do and Faff (2012, 2010) revealed a decline in

profitability when accounting for transaction costs. To enhance the strategy, other researchers (Vidyamurthy, 2004) introduced more sophisticated methodologies utilizing
cointegration frameworks.
In this study, I propose a novel approach to improve pairs trading strategies by
incorporating Error Correction Model (ECM) based on cointegration. Both the cointegration method and ECM method utilize spread to develop the trading strategy.
However, while the spread in the cointegration method is linked to its own distribution, the spread in the ECM method relates to the distribution of residuals in ECM.
Theoretically, the ECM method has the potential to capture more short-term opportunities. Thus, this study aims to investigate the following questions: Can ECM-based
pairs trading strategies lead to increased profits in the Chinese market over a long-term
period? Are these profits sustainable?
To address these questions, I examine and compare the performance of two pairs
trading strategies, namely the cointegration method and the ECM method, using daily
shortable China stock data from April 2010 to April 2023. The findings indicate that
the ECM method yields a slightly higher monthly return and a marginally smaller
standard deviation compared to the cointegration method.
Furthermore, the ECM
method demonstrates a significantly higher Sharpe ratio.
In conclusion, pairs trading strategies remain profitable and robust even in highly
volatile market conditions. The ECM method, proposed in this study, exhibits superior
performance compared to the traditional cointegration method. Pairs trading is essentially a statistical arbitrage process, and the limited arbitrage ability of traditional
paired trading strategies based solely on long-term equilibrium relationships hinders
the exploitation of short-term income opportunities. By incorporating an ECM to capture short-term fluctuations, pairs trading strategies can achieve enhanced performance.
These findings suggest that there is still considerable arbitrage potential in the Chinese
stock market, indicating that stock prices may not fully reflect market information.
This suggests that the Chinese stock market is not yet fully efficient, or even weakly
efficient.

Optimal Pairs Trading: A Mean–Variance Approach Incorporating a
Stop-Loss Mechanism
Ziyi Chen1,∗, Jia-wen Gu1
Abstract— In this paper, we study the optimal pairs trading problem within a continuous-time mean–variance framework. The spread between two risky assets is modeled as
an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) process. To mitigate potential
losses arising from a non-converging spread, we incorporate
a stop-loss mechanism. Owing to the time inconsistency of the
mean–variance criterion, we derive an analytical equilibrium
strategy by solving the extended HJB equation. Furthermore,
we conduct numerical experiments to illustrate the equilibrium
strategy and assess its performance.
I. INTRODUCTION
Pairs trading is a well-known arbitrage strategy that involves simultaneously taking long and short positions in
two correlated assets. Traders select a pair of assets with
a high historical correlation, and when the price difference
(spread) between them deviates from the historical mean,
they anticipate that the spread will revert. Specifically, the
trader goes long on the asset that is undervalued and short
on the overvalued asset. Once the spread reverts to its mean,
both positions are closed for a profit. It can generate stable
profits regardless of whether the market is in a bear or bull
phase (See [1]).
Aside from using statistical methods to design trading
strategies (e.g., [2], [3], [4]), some researchers have focused
on developing mathematical trading rules for pairs trading by
utilizing stochastic control methods. Existing studies can be
broadly categorized into two main types: those addressing
continuous-time scenarios (e.g., [5], [6], [7]) and those
focusing on discrete-time scenarios (e.g., [8], [9], [10]).
Traditionally, pairs trading strategies rely on the assumption that the spread between the two assets will revert to its
historical mean. However, unexpected shifts in asset correlations can cause the spread to fail to converge within the
investment horizon, thereby exposing investors to significant
risks. To mitigate these risks, a stop-loss mechanism is often
introduced as a risk management tool (see [8]).
In this paper, we study the optimal pairs trading strategy
in continuous time under the mean-variance criterion, incorporating a stop-loss mechanism. Specifically, we assume that
when the spread fails to revert to the long-term mean and
deviates beyond the stop-loss threshold, forced liquidation
halts further trading. Given the time inconsistency introduced
by the mean-variance criterion, we adopt the equilibrium
strategy approach proposed by [11], which provides a locally

IV. NUMERICAL SIMULATION
In this section, we employ numerical simulations to investigate optimal pairs trading strategies (In what follows, we
still refer to the equilibrium strategy as the optimal strategy.).
Our analysis focuses on two key aspects of the problem.
First, we explore the characteristics of the optimal strategy
under different parameter settings. Second, we examine the
performance of the equilibrium strategy using simulated
spread data. We set the default parameters as follows: k =
0.5, θ = 0, σ = 0.8, γ = 0.2, T = 1, M = −1.5, N = 1.5.
A. Visualization of the Optimal Strategy
Figure 1 shows the optimal number of shares with respect
to the spread at time t = 0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6. It can be observed
that as time progresses towards maturity, the slope of the
optimal control curve decreases, indicating that the strategy
becomes more conservative. Specifically, at earlier times the
optimal position responds more aggressively to deviations in
the spread, whereas closer to maturity the magnitude of the
position is reduced in order to mitigate the risk of insufficient
time for mean reversion. Besides, the larger the deviation of
the spread from its long-term mean, the larger the position
prescribed by the strategy in order to bet on its convergence.
This, in turn, underscores the necessity of incorporating stoploss constraints to guard against potential losses in cases
where the spread fails to converge as expected.

Optimal number of shares without stop-loss across different time
(k = 0.5, θ = 0, σ = 0.8, γ = 0.2, T = 1)
Figure 2 shows the optimal number of shares with stoploss level (M, N) = (−1.5, 1.5). In contrast, when stoploss constraints are imposed, the optimal strategy exhibits
a distinctly non-linear and truncated shape. For moderate
deviations of the spread, the behavior is qualitatively similar
to the case without stop-loss, with positions increasing in
magnitude as the spread departs from long-term mean. Yet,
once the spread approaches the stop-loss level, the optimal
control is significantly reduced, preventing further enlargement of positions in highly risky regions. This truncation
mechanism effectively guards against potential losses arising
from prolonged non-convergence. Compared to the optimal
strategy without a stop-loss mechanism, the optimal strategy
with a stop-loss mechanism is more conservative.

V. CONCLUSION
This paper investigates the optimal pairs trading problem
with a stop-loss mechanism under the mean-variance criterion. By employing PDE methods, we derive the explicit
optimal strategy in an equilibrium framework. In the absence
of the stop-loss mechanism, the optimal strategy shows a
linear negative relationship with the spread, leading to larger
positions when the spread deviates more significantly from
its long-term mean. However, the optimal strategy with the
stop-loss mechanism becomes more conservative, requiring
investors to reduce their position as the spread approaches the
stop-loss threshold, ultimately forcing a liquidation. We conduct simulation experiments that demonstrate the protective
role of the stop-loss mechanism in cases where the spread
fails to converge.

Equilibrium pairs trading under delayed cointegration
Tingjin Yana, Hoi Ying Wongb
aAcademy of Statistics and Interdisciplinary Sciences, Faculty of Economics and Management,
East China Normal University, Shanghai, China
bDepartment of Statistics, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Hong Kong
Abstract
Cointegration analysis is an econometric tool used to identify equilibrium among assets and construct a pairs trading portfolio. The discrete-time vector error correction model (VECM) for identifying cointegration includes lag difference terms
as explanatory variables, thus permitting delayed adjustment of the deviations from equilibrium. The continuous-time
limit of the VECM becomes a stochastic delay differential equation. We investigate the dynamic open-loop equilibrium
mean-variance pairs trading strategy under such a delayed cointegration model. The existence and uniqueness results
for the equilibrium are offered. We prove in general that the equilibrium strategy can lead to statistical arbitrage under
certain conditions that are related to the roots of the corresponding characteristic equation. We obtain an explicit solution
for a case with distributed delay. Our empirical study demonstrates the superiority of our strategy over its Markovian
counterpart when the model selection result prefers a high-order VECM.
Keywords: Mean-variance portfolio; Pairs trading; Stochastic delay differential equation; Time inconsistency
1. Introduction
Stochastic control system with delay effect is a topic which has existed for long period but still receive intensive
attentions from researchers in various areas such as engineering [39], ﬁnance [28, 27], epidemic modelling [6], among
others. In these literature, the time delay can appear in the system state as well as in the control variable. This paper
considers the stochastic control problem with delayed state from the perspective of cointegration and pairs trading.
Cointegration, a concept for which Robert F. Engle and Clive W. J. Granger won a Nobel prize in 2003, is ﬁrst
introduced to describe a speciﬁc class of multivariate non-stationary time series. When observed individually, these
series may wander extensively; however, they share some detectable common trends and thus do not drift far from one
another. In ﬁnance, cointegration techniques are used to identify the co-movement among assets and detect the meanreverting property of certain price spreads. Once these features are identiﬁed, practitioners can construct long-short
positions when the spreads diverge and unwind the positions when the spreads converge. This simple static pairs trading
strategy can generate abnormal proﬁts [16]. The current paper aims to develop a strategy that takes advantage of dynamic
trading and risk management.

Compared with the discrete-time formulation, a continuous-time formulation is sometimes preferred by those researchers and practitioners who are concerned with the tractability and interpretabily. Table 1 compares and contrasts
the discrete-time VECM and its continuous analogue. [32] ﬁrst establishes the connection between the VAR(1)-type
VECM and the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) process, a standard continuous-time Markovian cointegration model. Regarding non-Markvoian cases, the studies by [33, 29] demonstrate that stochastic delay differential equations (SDDE) can be
understood in some cases as the diffusion limit of high-order autoregressive moving average (ARMA) time series (for
more information on SDDEs, the readers may refer to [31]). They assume that the number of lagged terms will increase
without limit as the time between observations converges to zero, which naturally leads to a path-dependent structure.
Inspired by this idea, [4] extend Merton’s portfolio problem and consider a labor income process with a delayed dynamic
since the income process is commonly modelled by the ARMA process. [38] study the diffusion limit of VECM and
propose a delayed cointegration model using SDDE. As shown in Table 1, a Lebesue-Stieltjes integral appears in the drift
term, which is a sliding average analogous to the weighted sum of lagged terms in the discrete-time case. In this way, the
total weights of Y remains to be Π and thus we construct a delayed error correction term in the drift. [38] further solve
for the dynamic mean-variance (MV) pairs trading problem based on the delayed cointegration model.
However, the solution of [38] is time-inconsistent (or precommitted), as the non-smooth property of the variance
operator prevents the use of dynamic programming. We must assume that the decision maker only optimizes the problem
initially but does not reconsider the problem over time [40]. This obstacle is reﬂected in related work, such as [11], [35].
Moreover, pairs trading is related with the statistical arbitrage, which concerns with the strategy performance for a
sufﬁciently long time period, but the precommitted strategy for a cointegrated system is represented by the solutions to
Riccati differential equations and is only stable over a ﬁnite trading horizon [11, 38]. Accordingly, we are motivated to
investigate the time-consistent strategy.
We adopt the game-theoretical formulation of the time-consistent equilibrium strategy as established by [3, 5]. In this
approach, a sequential game is formulated by considering all the future incarnations of the decision maker as players, and
the resulting strategy is derived as a Nash sub-game perfect equilibrium. Their approaches are applied to pairs trading
in [12, 10] with Markovian models. However, [3, 5] only consider feedback control; they exclude the non-Markovian
control, which is likely to be the candidate strategy in our problem. To incorporate the non-Markovian control, we use the
open-loop equilibrium control framework in [21, 22, 37]. The open-loop method uses backward stochastic differential
equations (BSDEs) to allow the uniqueness of the equilibrium to be addressed, a task that is extremely difﬁcult when
using the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations framework proposed by [3, 5]. The aforementioned approaches
restrict the problem to a Markovian control system. The current paper extends it to the case of a non-Markovian system.
As a related work, [34] investigates a linear-quadratic stochastic differential game in high frequency trading and employs
the BSDE technique to obtain a Nash equilibrium which characterizes the trading behaviors of market participants.
This paper makes four contributions to the literature: First, we solve for the time-consistent equilibrium pairs trading
strategy using multiple assets whose log prices satisfy a stochastic system with distributed and discrete delay. This setup
can be understood as a continuous-time approximation of the VECM for a cointegrated time series. We provide results
proving the existence and uniqueness of the equilibrium determined using BSDEs. Note that the strategy in [38] ignores
the time-inconsistency issue and is optimal solely at the initial time, whereas our equilibrium strategy treats this issue
seriously and is derived through solving a Nash sub-game perfect equilibrium.
Second, we investigate the relationship between delayed cointegration and statistical arbitrage proposed by [19].
We associate the stochastic delay system with deterministic autonomous functional differential equations (AFDEs) and
conduct an asymptotic analysis. [12] study the equilibrium pairs trading under the OU process and obtain conditions for
statistical arbitrage. This paper considers a more general problem by incorporating the delayed terms (see Table 1). We
rigorously prove that our equilibrium strategy leads to statistical arbitrage when the eigenvalues of the AFDEs satisfy
certain conditions, thus embracing the result in [12] as a special case. Calculating these eigenvalues is an important and
well-studied topic in the theory of AFDE. We discuss some speciﬁc cases wherein these eigenvalues can be expressed

Third, we consider a speciﬁc case involving a general weighting function but only a distributed (not discrete) delay
and obtain an explicit solution. The stochastic control problem with delay is an inﬁnite dimensional problem and explicit
solutions are extremely rare; in fact, it is very difﬁcult to obtain any numerical solution. [1, 23, 8, 38] derive the semiclosed form solutions expressed using Riccati partial differential equations, which are difﬁcult to solve numerically.
Recent advances exploit deep neutral network to develop numerical methods (see [20, 27]). We ﬁnd that, compared to
the precommitted strategy in [38], the equilibrium strategy is much convenient to implement in practice.
Fourth, we conduct an empirical study by considering an investment problem involving two commodity securities:
WisdomTree WTI Crude Oil and WisdomTree Gasoline (UGAS). We contrast our strategy with its Markovian counterpart in [12]. As our strategy learns from a path-dependent structure, it can enable us to earn abnormal proﬁts even if
the market crashes. The back-testing result is also supported by the information criteria in lag order selection, which
suggests the use of high-order VAR models during the investment period.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the problem formulation. Section 3 offers results
demonstrating the existence and uniqueness of the equilibrium pairs trading strategy. In Section 4, we prove rigorously that our strategy leads to statistical arbitrage under certain conditions. Section 6 provides an empirical study and
compares our results with those in [12]. Section 7 concludes the paper.

2.2. Delayed cointegration model
We ﬁrst provide a brief review of the cointegrated time series in discrete time. Let t ∈Z be the time variable. The
n−dimensional time series {Y (t)}t∈Z is said to be cointegrated with k(0 < k < n) cointegrating relations if each
component of the series taken individually is non-stationary but ﬁrst-order integrated (i.e., taking difference yields a
stationary process) and there exists k linearly independent non-zero vectors ci, i = 1, 2, . . . , k such that c⊤
i is stationary
(see [30] for details). Each stationary process is called the cointegrating factor. In ﬁnancial market, the price of a certain
asset, which is usually non-stationary, is likely to be cointegrated with the market indexes or the prices of other assets in
a similar sector. Identiﬁcation of the cointegrating factor is the foundation of constructing a pairs trading strategy.

to a speciﬁc class of the VAR model and is crucial for the identiﬁcation of the cointegrating factor. Letting ∆˜Y (t) =

4. Statistical arbitrage
From a practical perspective, cointegrated pairs trading is referred to as a statistical arbitrage strategy. In the previous
discussion, we prove for the existence of the equilibrium strategy. Using Theorem 1 and Lemma 6, we conﬁrm the
uniqueness of the equilibrium when the investment horizon is sufﬁciently small. In this section, we will focus on the
long-term performance and validate that our equilibrium strategy satisﬁes the conditions for statistical arbitrage proposed
by [19]. Hence, even if the condition for unique equilibrium is not valid, the strategy remains trustworthy.

7. Conclusion
In this paper, we investigate a time-consistent MV pairs trading strategy under the cointegration model with delay
effect. We prove the existence and uniqueness results of this equilibrium strategy using the BSDE techniques. We
further study the relationship between delayed cointegration and statistical arbitrage and show that our strategy can lead
13
Electronic copy available at: https://ssrn.com/abstract=4117238

to statistical arbitrage in long-term investments under conditions related to the eigenvalues of the corresponding AFDE.
We then derive the analytical solution for a SDDE containing only a distributed (rather than discrete) delay. The empirical
study shows that if the data prefers a high-order VAR model in the order selection procedure, our non-Markovian strategy
outperforms its Markovian counterpart.

AI as Pricing Law
From Arbitrage-Free Valuation to QIS Innovation
Stefano Iabichino1
Published in Risk Magazine the 20th of October 2025
Abstract
Finance obeys the law of no-arbitrage, not patterns. AI learns patterns, not
laws, and that is where economic hallucination arises, distorting valuation, hedging,
and risk. Rather than applying AI to finance and patching arbitrage violations ex
post, we refound neural networks on asset pricing first principles. The result is the
first finance-native neural network, in which neurons encode market states, and
Markovian Activation Functions turn the First Fundamental Theorem of Finance
into a binding computational constraint. Applied to real-world QIS portfolios, it
enables XVA-style forward-looking strategy design, learning markets in 8 minutes
and simulating 100,000 scenarios across 800 layers in 80 seconds on a MacBook
Pro. Rooted in pricing axioms, AI sheds its black-box mystique to deliver coherent
valuation, robust risk diagnostics, and strategy innovation in a transparent and
consistent engine where every neuron obeys the immutable laws of finance.

Introduction
AI excels at uncovering nonlinear structure in data, but in quantitative finance patterns are not enough: valuation is governed by the law of no arbitrage. The First
Fundamental Theorem of Finance (FFTF, [6]) requires prices to admit an arbitragefree, measure-consistent representation, independent of past information. The clash
between pattern learning and law-based valuation induces cognitive bias and economic
hallucination in conventional AI models. Anticipatory signals are mistaken for edge,
spurious arbitrage emerges, and distorted risk exposures materialize, eroding AI’s trustworthiness in markets.
Rather than applying generic AI to finance and patching arbitrage violations ex
post (e.g., see [9]), we refound neural networks (NNs) from first principles, hard-wiring
the axioms of pricing directly into their architecture.
In our finance-native design,
neurons represent market states and risk exposures, while Markovian Activation Functions (MAFs) enforce probabilistic transitions consistent with de Finetti coherence and
Chapman–Kolmogorov dynamics. The result is a paradigm shift, i.e., NNs that are:

We demonstrate the framework by unifying the siloed domains of Quantitative

To our knowledge, this is the first AI architecture to embed arbitrage-free valuation
directly into its structure, effectively turning the FFTF into a computational constraint.
It is also the first framework to unify QIS and XVA within a single law-consistent engine.
Being measure-agnostic, our finance-native NN extends naturally to regulatory capital
calculators, treasury simulations, and stress generators, charting a clear roadmap for
law-consistent AI across pricing, risk, and capital functions.
Conventional NNs, by contrast, bypass the axiomatic nature of financial markets,
treating valuation, hedging, and tail-risk as intrinsic and fully learnable from chronicles.
Trained without structural priors, they require post hoc arbitrage detection and correction and remain opaque, lacking the economic semantics required for interpretability
and trust. Approaches such as deep hedging ([2]), TailGAN ([4]), VolGAN ([9]), and
auto-encoding structures ([8]) exemplify this blind spot: they rely on generic architectures trained on non-repetitive trajectories or model-generated outputs, mistaking
patterns for statistical laws and treating arbitrage as an afterthought rather than a
structural prior.
Finance-native neural networks redefine the relationship between AI and markets:
no longer opaque pattern learners, but axiomatically grounded engines where every
neuron carries economic meaning and obeys the immutable laws of financial economics.

Case Study: QIS and Forward-Consistent XVA Metrics
Having established the structural formulation, we now demonstrate how our financenative NN can encode forward-looking, arbitrage-free simulations of real-world portfolios. We focus on a high-dimensional set of swaption-based QIS strategies to showcase
operational power and interpretability.
3.1
Set-up
We consider a bank managing a portfolio of 210 QIS in the U.S. Fixed Income space.
Each strategy executes a USD at-the-money (ATM) swaption straddle every two days,
across a set of maturities and tenors ranging from 1 month to 30 years. Each day,
the bank delta-hedges all active positions using a combination of 2-year and 10-year
interest rate swaps.
Instead of modeling interest rates through classical SDEs, we embed the short rate
rt natively into the neural architecture. The rate is expressed as a composite neuron
state dynamics:
rt = Xt + Yt −ϕt,

4. Actionable Insights
The network not only projects valuations but also diagnoses
which future states destabilize P&L and which trades restore it, yielding actionable
guidance. HVA0 quantifies overall hedging effectiveness in dollar terms; δHVAð measures the marginal impact of a trade; and δHVAγ highlights forward economic states
undermining hedges. This enables the desk to:
• Add hedges targeting bespoke risky future economic states;
• Adjust exposures by unwinding trades that generate material P&L leakages or
incentivizing those that offset it;
• Pre-position liquidity buffers for adverse scenarios.
A negative δHVAγ signals over-replication and potential capital redeployment. This
decomposition turns the NN into a decision engine directly tied to risk-appetite, hedging, and capital allocation policies.

Conclusion
In the 1980s, two opposing philosophies shaped the development of AI: the symbolic
approach, where learning was governed by explicit rules, and the connectionist (i.e.,
neural) approach, where learning was driven by data. Modern AI emerged from this
clash, with the latter prevailing. Yet in quantitative finance, pure connectionism often
breaches the laws of markets, producing valuations that violate no-arbitrage and requiring ex post corrections. Our framework fuses the two worlds: the laws of finance
become symbolic constraints, encoded directly into a connectionist architecture.
By uniting financial axioms with connectionist power, our finance-native neural
networks move beyond pattern recognition and establish a new paradigm for AI in
markets: interpretable, adaptive, and bound by the immutable laws of finance.

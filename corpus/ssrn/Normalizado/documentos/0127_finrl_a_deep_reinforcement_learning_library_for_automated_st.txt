ABSTRACT
We introduce the Price Jerk Indicator (PJI), a novel quantitative tool derived from the physical concept of jerk
— the third time-derivative of position — applied to financial price series. While first-order (momentum) and
second-order (acceleration) price derivatives are implicit in most technical indicators, no published work applies
the third derivative to reversal detection in financial markets. The PJI fits a quadratic regression to the rolling
series of price accelerations; the resulting curvature coefficient identifies "dynamic braking" events where
downward acceleration is decelerating — a necessary physical precondition for price reversal.
We validate the PJI on 388 real trades on NAS100 and SPY hourly CFD data (Pepperstone MT5, 2019–2026),
comparing its AUC-ROC to RSI-14. The PJI achieves AUC-ROC of 0.534 versus 0.512 for RSI-14 — a
statistically significant improvement for financial time series. The complete trading system incorporating PJI
achieves a Calmar Ratio of 10.0, Sortino Ratio of 5.30, and passes walk-forward out-of-sample validation with
only 4.7pp WR degradation over 38 months.
We further present the complete KCE Quantum Ultimate system, comprising eight proprietary algorithms: (1)
IFR — Price Jerk Indicator; (2) ACZ — Normalized Zero Acceleration; (3) KCE-Hurst — dual-method regime
sensor; (4) MGN — Law of Large Numbers equilibrium; (5) Vela de Intención — institutional candle filter; (6)
Maceta RSI — RSI curvature inflection; (7) Mediana Expansiva Trimestral — expanding non-linear median; and
(8) KCE-PD — historical average confluence. Monte Carlo simulation (50,000 scenarios) yields P(prop firm
funding) = 95.4% across a standardized two-phase evaluation protocol.
1. INTRODUCTION
Technical analysis has long employed price derivatives implicitly. Rate-of-change (ROC) indicators measure
first-order velocity; momentum oscillators such as RSI (Wilder, 1978) and MACD (Appel, 1979) capture
quasi-first- and second-order behavior. The acceleration of price — its second derivative — appears formally in
the work of Ehlers (2002).
Yet the third derivative — jerk — has received no attention in quantitative finance. In classical mechanics, jerk
is the rate of change of acceleration: it describes the onset of a force change. A negative jerk in a falling body
means the downward acceleration is decreasing — the body is physically braking before reversal. We
hypothesize an analogous phenomenon in financial prices: periods of decelerating decline (negative jerk)
systematically precede bullish reversals.
This paper makes the following contributions:
• First application of the third price derivative to reversal detection in financial time series — no prior
work known to the authors.
• Empirical AUC-ROC validation against RSI-14 on 388 real executed trades.
• Complete eight-algorithm trading system (KCE Quantum Ultimate) with seven years of out-of-sample
performance.
• Monte Carlo validation of the system under standardized prop firm evaluation protocols.

2.1 IFR — Índice de Firmeza del Retroceso (Price Jerk Indicator)
UNIQUENESS: 9.5/10 — No prior literature known.
The IFR applies the physical concept of jerk to price series. Given a rolling window of size w, the IFR computes:
(1) velocity series v[i] = P[i] − P[i−1]; (2) acceleration series a[i] = v[i] − v[i−1]; (3) quadratic regression y = αI +
αIx + αIx² over the acceleration series; (4) curvature estimate = 2αI. Negative curvature (2αI < 0) indicates
decelerating downward acceleration — the "dynamic brake" signal. The IFR maps curvature to [0,1]: curvature <
−0.5 → IFR=0.9; [−0.5,−0.2) → 0.7; [−0.2,+0.1) → 0.4; ≥0.1 → 0.2. AUC-ROC: 0.534 vs RSI-14 baseline 0.512
(388 trades, 2019–2026).
2.2 ACZ — Aceleración Cero Normalizada (Normalized Zero Acceleration)
UNIQUENESS: 9.0/10 — Novel normalization approach.
The ACZ measures the second derivative of price normalized by local ATR volatility: ACZ = (v[t] − v[t−1]) /
ATR(14). Normalization by ATR removes the scale effect, making the signal instrument-agnostic and directly
comparable across NAS100, SPY, US30, Forex, and crypto. When ACZ crosses zero from below with price in a
downtrend, it signals the "dynamic brake point" before reversal. Used in combination with IFR for high-quality
entry confluence.
2.3 KCE-Hurst — Dual-Method Market Regime Sensor
UNIQUENESS: 8.5/10 — Original combination H_ACF + H_RS with 4 named regimes.
The KCE-Hurst computes H_combo = (H_ACF + H_RS) / 2, combining two independent Hurst estimators:
autocorrelation-function based (H_ACF) and rescaled-range based (H_RS). The dual-method approach
provides more robust regime classification than either alone. Four regimes are defined: IMPULSO (H>0.62, R:R
5:1), TENDENCIA (H 0.55–0.62, R:R 4.5:1), RANGO (H 0.45–0.55, R:R 4:1), CAOS (H<0.45, no trading).
Thresholds are grounded in Mandelbrot (1971).
2.4 MGN — Media de Grandes Números (Law of Large Numbers Equilibrium)
UNIQUENESS: 8.0/10 — Novel application of statistical law to price equilibrium.
The MGN applies the Law of Large Numbers to price series: SMA(500 bars) represents the statistical
equilibrium price where the distribution of returns is maximal. Three zones: EN_MGN (±0.6%, bonus factor
×1.30), PREMIUM (>+2%, extended), DISCOUNT (<−2%, opportunity). The 500-bar window is selected to
approximate the statistical threshold where sample mean converges to population mean for intraday data.
2.5 Vela de Intención (Institutional Intention Candle)
UNIQUENESS: 7.5/10 — Quantified institutional flow filter.
A candle with body ≥ 82% of total range is classified as a Vela de Intención. This threshold quantifies the
presence of institutional directional flow: a high body-to-range ratio indicates minimal intrabar reversal,
consistent with institutional order execution. The 82% threshold was derived empirically from 330 classified
trades on NAS100 H1 (2019–2024).
2.6 Maceta RSI (RSI Curvature Inflection)
UNIQUENESS: 7.5/10 — RSI curvature as inflection detector.
The Maceta RSI detects U-shaped (bullish) and inverted-U (bearish) inflections in the RSI(14) series by
comparing three consecutive RSI values: Bullish: RSI[0] > RSI[1] AND RSI[1] ≤ RSI[2] (minimum inflection).

Bearish: RSI[0] < RSI[1] AND RSI[1] ≥ RSI[2] (maximum inflection). This detects momentum exhaustion one to
two bars before conventional RSI signals.
2.7 Mediana Expansiva Trimestral (Expanding Non-Linear Median)
UNIQUENESS: 8.0/10 — Expanding window median as non-linear equilibrium.
The MET computes a rolling median of (High+Low)/2 over an expanding window that grows from an initial size
(20 bars) at a fixed rate until reaching a maximum (252 bars). Unlike fixed-window medians, the expanding
window progressively incorporates more price history, revealing the long-term structural equilibrium without lag
artifacts. The IQR band (Q75−Q25) quantifies the fair-value range with statistical rigor robust to price spikes.
2.8 KCE-PD — Historical Average Confluence (nc≥2)
UNIQUENESS: 7.5/10 — Multi-timeframe confluence detector.
The KCE-PD identifies zones where at least two (nc≥2) historical price averages converge within a defined
tolerance. Averages computed over different lookback periods (8, 21, 34, 55, 89 bars) represent institutional
memory at different time horizons. Confluence of nc≥2 averages creates a statistically significant
support/resistance zone with higher probability of price reaction.

3. METHODOLOGY AND VALIDATION
Data: NAS100 (Nasdaq 100 CFD, continuous) and SPY (S&P; 500 ETF CFD), hourly bars, January 2019 to

actual executed trades (no simulation of fills).
AUC-ROC Protocol: Binary outcome WIN/LOSS within forward window of RR×16 bars. AUC measures
P(random WIN has higher indicator value than random LOSS). AUC=0.50 indicates no predictive power;
AUC>0.53 is considered practically significant for financial time series (Neely et al., 2014).

4. DISCUSSION
The 4.4% AUC-ROC improvement over RSI-14 (0.534 vs 0.512) is modest in absolute terms but statistically
meaningful for single-bar financial signals. The PJI does not claim to predict price — it identifies a specific
physical condition (decelerating downward acceleration) that has empirically predictable value for short-term
reversals.
The walk-forward degradation of 4.7pp is within expected sampling variability bounds (Harvey et al., 2016). The
Hurst regime thresholds (0.45/0.55/0.62) have theoretical grounding in Mandelbrot (1971) and were not
optimized to the training dataset.
Limitations: Validation is limited to two US equity index instruments. The discrete curvature mapping [0.9, 0.7,
0.4, 0.2] is a simplification of a continuous measure. Future work should extend validation to Forex,
commodities, and crypto instruments, and test continuous curvature formulations.

Institutional Set-up of the Fed’s Day-to-day Monetary Policy
The Fed may conduct two kinds of open market operations. First, it may seek to accommodate temporary exogenous shocks to the reserves level in the banking system by removing
the pressure either from the spot fed funds rate under Funds Rate Targeting Regime or from
the amount of discount window borrowing under Borrowed Reserve Targeting Regime. This
type of open market operations is called defensive. Second, the Fed may seek to intervene
for the purpose of adjusting the level of the interest rate target, and in this way, enforce a
broader spectrum of interest rates in the economy. This type of operations is called dynamic.
Defensive operations fall under the authority of the Trading Desk at the New York Fed.
On a daily basis, the Desk estimates exogenous shocks, formulates the scope of operations,
and projects the impact of operations on the money market. Dynamic open market operations are considered “higher-level policy decisions”(see Woodford (2002)) and fall under
the authority of the Federal Open Market Committee.
In 1994 the policy of the Fed changed to become more transparent and it started disclosing the fed funds target through speeches and testimonies. Since then all Fed’s open
market operations are defensive in nature, which means that they do not provide any in4

formation about the future stance of monetary policy. As a result of the change policy
change, the whole profession called "Fed watchers", who monitored balances in the banking
system and the scope of Fed’s interventions in order to forecast the direction of interest rates
disappeared. Nowadays, private sector agents forecast the future fed target on the basis of
macroeconomic fundamentals rather than the amount of reserves in the banking system.
My study examines the neutrality of defensive open market operations with respect to
the government bond market. Let me, at this stage, narrow down the asset classes considered
in the paper. For the bond market analysis, I use only 2-years and 5-years maturity treasury
notes. As regards the money market, I focus on two instruments: repurchase agreements
(repo), which are collateralized credit against bonds and federal funds, which are unsecured
inter-bank credit. In the next subsection, I clarify the intra-day interaction between the Fed
and primary bond dealers.

2.2
The Fed’s repo auctions
Let us now examine the objectives of the Fed in conducting defensive open market operations.
Assume that the Fed projects a forthcoming reserves de…ciency in the banking system, which
will create upward pressure on the fed funds rate away from the target. In this situation,
the New York Fed’s Trading Desk needs to inject reserves into the system. Rather than
intervening on the inter-bank credit market, the Desk conducts an open market operation
through temporary repo agreements.
In this way, the Fed substitutes the private fund
surplus agents in the chain of re…nancing the bond dealer’s positions by transferring funds
to the account of the dealer’s previous creditor.
Suppose that, on day t; the bond dealer needs to return funds to Bank A, which previously
…nanced his position through a private repo. In the absence of intervention by the Fed, the
dealer would arrange so that Bank B would credit Bank A and reassign the bond as collateral
to Bank B. However, instead, the Fed intervenes and transfers funds to Bank A. As a result
of this transaction, the dealer owes funds to the Fed, which holds the bond as collateral.
From the view point of the banking system, the di¤erence is that Bank B now has extra
overnight funds to supply to the fed funds market since the demand for the private repo
transaction was satis…ed by the Fed. This creates a downward pressure on the fed funds
rate f, which is exactly what the Fed was trying to achieve in the given situation.
By conducting open market operations, the Fed acts not as a buyer on the bond market,
but as a large creditor …nancing the leveraged positions of bond dealers. When such operations are not conducted private sector agents redistribute the given amount of the monetary
base between the funds de…cient and the funds surplus agents. An important feature of
the US monetary system is that the Fed changes the monetary base through a system of
multiple primary bond dealers. The Fed’s repos are organized as an auction among primary
bond dealer, which takes place at a …xed time interval called Fed time7.

Conclusions
The success of open market operations conducted by central banks is traditionally measured
in terms of deviations of the inter-bank interest rate from the target. My study deals with
another aspect of central bank policies by evaluating the impact of the Fed’s operations
on the instrument used to adjust the level of liquidity in the banking system, i.e. the US
government bond market.
I demonstrate that regular short-term open market operations conducted by the Fed in
the year 2000 had a signi…cant impact on the volatility of bond returns at Fed time. On
the contrary, on days when the Fed intervened twice, with early long-term repos in addition
to the regularly scheduled short-term repos, the volatility of bond returns at Fed time was
not di¤erent from on days without interventions.
I link the observed patterns of bond
returns volatility to the collateral reassignment problem faced by bond dealers as a result
of overbidding at the Fed’s repo auctions. The larger the overbidding, the more …nancing
dealers need to obtain from the private market to compensate for bids that were not satis…ed
by the Fed. I …nd that two empirical measures of the volume of overbidding are positively
associated with the volatility of bond returns on days when repos were traded only at regular
Fed time. However, on days when both long and short-term repos were traded, there is no
signi…cant positive association between the size of overbidding and bond returns volatility.
My study, further, presents evidence on the impact of the Fed’s re…nancing operations
on bond market liquidity and trading activity. I …nd that the Fed’s repo operations are
associated with a widening of the primary bond dealers’bid-ask spreads, as well as a decline
in market depth, trading volume, and average trade size. This can be explained with the fact
that the primary bond dealers face an inventory risk because they are obliged to participate
in the Fed’s repo auctions and provide bonds as a collateral to the Fed. The …ndings are
also consistent with the inventory paradigm introduced by Demsetz (1968), which suggests
that liquidity depends on inventory risk and inventory turnover.
The …ndings of my paper suggest that the introduction of long-term repos, which was
intended to smooth out seasonal variations in banking reserves, improved the e¢ ciency of the
Fed’s monetary policy implementation. By extending the maturity of term repo contracts
and intervening more than once per day, the Fed was able to minimize its impact on the
bond market.

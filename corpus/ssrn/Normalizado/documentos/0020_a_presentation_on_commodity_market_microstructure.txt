Floor, Side-by-Side, and Electronic Trading
•
In open-outcry trading, traders physically stand in 
a trading ring or trading pit.  
•
In a physical open-outcry pit, a market maker can 
be physically present in only one trading pit, …
•
… whereas in electronic trading an off-the-floor 
market-maker can make markets in multiple 
products at the same time.

4
Commodity Market Microstructure
Floor, Side-by-Side, and Electronic Trading
•
On August 1, 2006 the CBOT introduced 
side-by-side trading in its grain contracts.
•
What we saw was quite impressive.  In the first 30 days 
of side-by-side trading, electronic volume in corn 
moved up to average between 50 and 60% of the floor 
volume. 
Source:  Dowd (2007a).

Commodity Market Microstructure
Nodal Liquidity
•
The commodity markets do not have 
natural two-sided flow.
•
For experienced traders in the fixed 
income, equity, and currency markets, 
this point may not be obvious.
•
The commodity markets have “nodal 
liquidity.”
Source:  Till (2006).

11
Commodity Market Microstructure
Nodal Liquidity
•
If a commercial market participant needs to initiate or lift 
hedges, there will be flow, but such transactions do not 
occur on demand.
•
For experienced commodity traders, a key part of one’s 
strategy development is a plan for how to exit a strategy.
•
What flow or catalyst will allow the trader out of a 
position?
Source:  Till (2006).

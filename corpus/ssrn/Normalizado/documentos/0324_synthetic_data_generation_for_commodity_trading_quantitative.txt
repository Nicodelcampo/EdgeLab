Methodology: Synthetic data via Multivariate Normal Distribution (MND)
Core idea: Preserve mean and covariance structure
We generate synthetic commodity data by:
1
Extract mean vector µ from real data
2
Extract covariance matrix Σ from real data
3
Sample from multivariate normal distribution
Xsynthetic ∼N(µreal, Σreal)
where:
µreal = mean of URALS, BRENT, MOEX, NWEMURL
Σreal = sample covariance matrix capturing correlations
nsamples = 234 (matching real data size)

Appendix
MND approach features
First order moments:
Mean: E[Xsynthetic] = µreal
Average prices of URALS, BRENT, MOEX, NWEMURL match perfectly
Second order moments:
Covariance: Cov[Xsynthetic] = Σreal
Cross-asset correlations preserved (critical for portfolio dynamics)
Volatility of each asset preserved
Key limitation:
Assume Gaussian marginals (commodity prices may be skewed)
Cannot capture non linear dependencies or tail asymmetry

Appendix: Mathematical properties
Cholesky decomposition:
For positive definite covariance matrix Σ:
Σ = LLT
where L is lower triangular. This ensures:
Computationally efficient (O(p3) complexity)
Numerically stable
Generates correlated samples preserving Cov(Xsynthetic) = Σ
Normality assumption:
The synthetic data inherits the Gaussian assumption:
Xsynthetic ∼N(µ, Σ)
This is appropriate for returns and log-prices but may underestimate tail risk and skewness in
commodity spot prices.

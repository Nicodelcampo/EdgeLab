Abstract
We propose optimal trading strategies based on the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation when a trader is allowed to place only
market orders with a particular focus on a statistical arbitrage. A process that a placed limit order is ﬁlled is often modeled as
a stochastic point process. In case of a market order, however, it is ﬁlled with a hundred percent probability meaning that it
is not a stochastic process and is rather a deterministic one. They are therefore represented using the Heaviside step function
and their inﬁnitesimal generators are just time derivative and give delta functions. An integral linear utility function with an
inventory penalty and a discount enables us to obtain optimal number of market order with a simple calculus and those in a
limit T →∞, i.e. a stationary limit. We show that, at least within our formulation, we cannot generate a positive PnL by
trading multiple set of assets whose noises are correlated but their drifts are zero which contradicts the common statement that
“Given correlated multiple assets, we can exploit those correlations by trading an appropriate linear combination of them”.
However, we show that if multiple assets are cointegrated, we can create a mean-reverting portfolio and exploit them. In that
case, an optimal number of market order which takes current status of market as well as a trader’s preference into account is
given in a simple but intuitive and reasonable form and it does generate positive PnL while controlling risk of the portfolio.
Key words; Statistical arbitrage, Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation, time-inconsistent optimal control

I.
INTRODUCTION
Optimal market making strategies based on the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman(HJB) equation [1] have been intensely
studied in a past decade(see [2] and papers citing it) in which market maker places limit orders at limit order book
and aim to earn spreads as a risk premium for providing liquidity to the market. A market maker however, also
wants to place market orders regarding market condition and his own preferences such as his current inventory. In
this article, we propose a possible method to introduce market orders in the HJB equation.
Market orders are deterministic process in contrast to limit orders that are often modeled as stochastic point
processes [1]. They are ﬁlled with a hundred percent probability as soon as we place market orders(we ignore slippage
to simplify the argument). Traders inventory and therefore his wealth which change discontinuously are modeled
with the Heaviside step function. Inﬁnitesimal generator with respect to inventory and wealth thus are simple time
derivative which induce delta function when applied to the Heaviside step function.
The next step is similar to those of limit order-cases, i.e. we derive optimal number of market orders by considering
controllable parts of the HJB equation and solve it order by order with respect to inventory.
We will see that
the optimal numbers of market orders actually maximized the controllable parts of the HJB equation and is given
analytically in a rather simple form especially in T →∞limit.
We also perform Monte Carlo simulation and
investigate trader’s inventory and wealth for various risk-aversion parameters.
In section II, we write down the HJB equation when a trader is allowed to place only market orders and derive
optimal number of market orders. In section III, we apply our results in a case multiple assets are cointegrated.
There, we show that the common statement of a statistical arbitrage “We can exploit correlations among assets by
trading an appropriate linear combination of them.” is not true if drifts of the assets are all zero. We also perform
Monte Carlo simulation and present statistical properties of the trading strategies. In section IV, we summarize and
discuss future perspectives of our study.

IV.
SUMMARY AND DISCUSSION
In this article, we proposed a simple method to incorporate market orders into the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman
equation. Since market orders are ﬁlled with a hundred percent of probability as soon as we place them, they are
modeled as deterministic process in contrast to limit orders which are often modeled as stochastic processes. By
investigating controllable parts of the HJB equation, we derived a optimal amount of market orders in an analytic
form Eq.(34). We showed that, within our formalism, the optimal control for a set of multiple assets whose noises are
correlated but their drifts are zero, is not to trade at all. The result contradicts with a common statement of statistical
arbitrage that “If a multiple assets are correlated, we can exploit those correlation by trading an appropriate linear
combination of them.”. However, if multiple assets are cointegrated, we can create a portfoliio which mean-reverts
and generate a positive PnL. We applied the optimal control method to such a case and performed Monte Carlo
simulation for various risk aversions parameters and estimated statistical properties such as the trader’s inventory
and PnL. We saw that there is no set of “optimal” risk aversion parameters in contrast to market making strategies
studied before [3] and we need to consider not only mean and standard deviation of PnL but also quantities such as
maximum drawdown.

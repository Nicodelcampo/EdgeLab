Abstract 
Based on two first principles newly introduced here—the Causal–Entropic Law (CEL) 
and the Principle of Selective Entropy Increase (SEI)—we construct Order Dynamics 
(OD), a unifying framework for explaining the origin and evolution of cosmic order. OD 
posits that cosmic dynamics arise from the interplay of two fundamental forces: a Causal 
Force, which drives aggregation, structure, and causal coherence; and an Entropic Force, 
which drives dispersion, disintegration, and disorder. Their asymmetric competition underlies 
the emergence of complexity from the Big Bang to multi-level systems. 
Within this framework, longstanding puzzles such as singularities, mass, inertia, the 
arrow of time, spacetime curvature, and other foundational paradoxes are reinterpreted as 
manifestations of causal–entropic dynamics.  
    Building on the CEL, OD further predicts a fundamental physical state of 
matter—the Semi-Aggregated State (SAS), an intermediate phase between particle and 
field. The SAS framework provides a coherent, physical basis for several quantum 
phenomena, offering self-consistent explanations for wave–particle duality, quantum 
entanglement, and the uncertainty principle, while grounding the wavefunction as a 
physically real expression of a semi-condensed field distribution. 
Extending beyond physics, OD naturally encompasses biological evolution, social 
systems, and civilizations as progressive emergences of order across scales. As a conceptual 
paradigm, OD seeks not to replace existing theories but to subsume and extend 
them—pointing toward new empirical directions for probing the origins of complexity and 
the fundamental laws of nature. 
Keywords:  
Order Dynamics (OD); Causal–Entropic Law (CEL); Selective Entropy Increase (SEI); 
Semi-Aggregated State (SAS); Entropy; Causality; Quantum Mechanics; Complexity; 
Cosmology; Spacetime, Particle Physics.

Introduction 
The evolution of the universe poses a central paradox: the second law of 
thermodynamics dictates that all isolated systems move toward increasing entropy and 
eventual “heat death”【1】, yet reality is pervaded by order and structure—from atoms and 
galaxies to organisms and societies. How, then, can ordered complexity continually arise 
within an entropic universe? 
Existing accounts in complexity science provide partial answers. Dissipative structure 
theory explains how systems far from equilibrium can sustain order, but it does not address 
why such non-equilibrium states emerge in the first place. Evolutionary theory elucidates the 
mechanisms of species selection, yet leaves unexplained the very origin of selective capacity. 
Theories of self-organization and emergence, meanwhile, remain largely descriptive and 
intuitive, lacking a deeper causal foundation【2-4】. 
At the same time, the limitations of foundational physics remain unresolved. Newtonian 
mechanics ascribes inertia to matter without explaining its origin 【5】. Relativity embeds 
mass and the speed of light in spacetime geometry but treats their existence and limits as 
axioms【6】. Quantum mechanics reveals duality and entanglement yet remains riddled with 
paradoxes and interpretive uncertainty【7】. Together, these gaps highlight the absence of a 
unifying principle that can account for the genesis and evolution of order across domains. 
This paper introduces Order Dynamics to address this theoretical void. The central 
premise of OD is that cosmic evolution is not driven by entropy increase alone, but is 
simultaneously constrained by a countervailing Causal Force. The asymmetric interplay 
between entropy and anti-entropy renders entropy increase selective, allowing local order to 

4 
 
form, stabilize, and evolve against a backdrop of global entropy growth. This selective 
dynamic, we argue, constitutes the common logic underlying all ordered structures, complex 
systems, and higher-level emergences. 
Following a systematic definition of OD’s core concepts and the Order Equation, this 
paper reinterprets a series of foundational problems in physics, including the origin of mass 
and inertia, the limiting nature of the speed of light, and the causal basis of spacetime 
curvature. We then extend the framework to biological and social domains, showing how OD 
can unify the dynamics of evolution, social mechanisms, and civilizational development. 
Through this cross-disciplinary perspective, we seek to address a fundamental question 
spanning cosmology, physics, and the human sciences: where does order arise, and how 
does it evolve?

system’s elements. High order corresponds to tightly coupled and coordinated interactions; 
low order to loosened ties, reduced dependence, and greater dispersion or indeterminacy.

Hierarchies of Order 
Order, as a central category of cosmic evolution, is not confined to a single level. 
Shaped by the interplay of Causal and Entropic Forces, it appears in distinct hierarchical 
forms: basic, intermediate, and advanced. This stratification clarifies how complexity 
emerges layer by layer and offers a unifying lens for cross-disciplinary research.

Basic Order: Aggregation and Existence 
At its most fundamental level, order manifests as the aggregation and persistence of 
matter itself, corresponding to the parameter 𝐺 in the order function and intimately linked to 
entropy.  
The essence of basic order lies in the spatial distribution of material elements: dispersed 
matter tends to cluster, giving rise to coherent structures. This tendency is sustained by 
Causal Forces—such as gravity and electromagnetism—that counter the universal pull of 
entropic forces, which drive diffusion and homogenization. In simplified modeling, basic 
order can be approximated as a function of system entropy. 
The balance between these opposing tendencies is evident in the binding of elementary 
particles, the formation of atomic nuclei, and the clustering of stars and galaxies. Basic order, 
in this sense, provides the substrate and stage upon which higher structures can emerge. 
Without the existence and aggregation of matter, no further logic or structure would be 
possible. 
Intermediate Order: Structure and Mechanism 
Building upon the mere existence and aggregation of matter, order advances into a 
structural and functional domain, corresponding to the parameters S and R. At this level, 
elements no longer remain simply co-located; they bind and interact through chemical bonds, 
intermolecular forces, and other organizing mechanisms to create durable architectures. 
Causal Forces now shape not only aggregation but also topological patterns and functional 
arrangements, giving rise to regularity and organization. Entropic Forces, however, persist in 
opposition, eroding these structures through breakdown, degradation, and disorganization. 

10 
 
The tension between the two is vividly expressed in the symmetry of crystal lattices, the 
folding of proteins, the double helix of DNA, and the compartmentalization of organelles.   
Here, the universe ceases to be defined merely by existence: it begins to display function 
and mechanism. Intermediate order thus marks the threshold from physical aggregation to the 
structured complexity that enables life and society. 
Advanced Order: Logic and Causality 
Advanced order emerges as the generative domain of higher-level logic and causality. 
While both basic and intermediate orders already contain elements of logical and causal 
relation, advanced order elevates these into a higher synthesis of integration and recursion, 
arising from the accumulated interplay of the two prior levels. Rooted in the parameter R, it 
enhances correlations and structural coupling, driving logic and causality toward greater 
complexity, stability, and self-consistency. 
At this stage, interactions among elements transcend mere physical or chemical 
processes: they generate causal chains, feedback loops, and capacities for information 
processing. Causal Forces here ensure not only the preservation of structures but also the 
expansion and interaction of causal networks, sustaining logical coherence on ever larger 
scales. Entropic Forces, in contrast, disrupt these chains, dissipate information, and induce 
systemic disorder. The tension between the two is visible in the coding and expression of 
genetic information, the computational logic of neural networks, and the causal architectures 
of institutions and legal systems.

Advanced order represents the pinnacle of complexity. It elevates existence and 
structure into the dimensions of logic, consciousness, and meaning, making possible the 
emergence of life, society, and culture. 
Synthesis 
The three levels of order do not exist in isolation but evolve in a progressive continuum. 
Basic order provides the conditions for material existence and aggregation; intermediate 
order builds upon this foundation to generate structure and function; advanced order develops 
further into higher logic and causality, driving the emergence of complex systems. 
Crucially, these categories are not rigid partitions but a continuous spectrum. Real 
systems often display overlap and ambiguity: phenomena may embody structural stability 
characteristic of intermediate order while simultaneously carrying the informational logic of 
advanced order—for instance, DNA is both a chemical structure and a genetic code. Such 
ambiguity is not a defect but a hallmark of complexity, reflecting the nested and emergent 
nature of order across levels. 
In many cases, the rise of higher-level order occurs at the cost of entropy increase at 
lower levels or across the system as a whole. This ―trade-off‖—where global or local entropy 
growth underwrites the emergence of higher organization—is a key driver of complex 
evolution. Thus, the layered model not only clarifies the stratification of order but also 
provides a systematic framework for explaining the unified evolution from matter to life and 
onward to society. 
Its value lies in three dimensions: it offers a cross-disciplinary bridge—linking physics 
with basic order, chemistry and biology with intermediate order, and the social and cognitive

sciences with advanced order; it explains complexity as a gradual sedimentation of order 
through the resistance of Causal Force against Entropic Force; and it carries philosophical 
weight, revealing the natural progression of the universe from existence to life to 
consciousness, while emphasizing the transitional and overlapping character of order as an 
essential feature of cosmic evolution. 
Principle of Selective Entropy Increase 
Order Dynamics frames cosmic evolution not as simple entropy increase, but as a 
process of selective entropy increase, driven by the asymmetric interplay between Causal 
and Entropic Forces. Causal Force promotes the aggregation of matter and the formation of 
stable configurations, while Entropic Force disperses and randomizes, generating an immense 
space of possible variations. 
Yet only a fraction of these variations can endure. Physical and chemical constraints 
ensure that unstable combinations dissolve, while those resilient to entropic disruption are 
retained. Over time, such stability accumulates along causal chains, giving rise to 
progressively more complex and functional patterns. The principle of selective entropy 
increase thus provides a higher-order synthesis, capable of reconciling and extending several 
classical theories. 
Evolutionary Theory. 
In biological evolution, entropy manifests through genetic mutations and environmental 
fluctuations, which supply diversity as raw material. Causality, expressed in natural selection, 
filters this diversity, favoring organisms whose structures remain stable under pressure. Life,

therefore, is not the product of randomness alone but of causal consolidation within entropic 
possibility. 
Dissipative Structures. 
Dissipative structure theory shows that far-from-equilibrium systems can form ordered 
patterns through energy and matter exchange【2】. Order Dynamics deepens this view: such 
structures persist because Causal Force, fueled by external energy flows, counterbalances 
Entropic Force, sustaining local order against the global trend of entropy increase. 
Emergence Theory 
Emergence theory holds that complexity can arise spontaneously from the interactions 
of simple components【4】. Order Dynamics grounds this phenomenon in a dynamical 
framework: when simple elements, shaped by the randomness of Entropic Force, are bound 
and aggregated through Causal Force to a critical threshold, new orders emerge that transcend 
the sum of their parts. Emergence, in this view, is not accidental but the necessary outcome of 
competition and cooperation between causality and entropy. 
Synthesis 
As one of fundamental principles of Order Dynamics, selective entropy increase 
reveals a unifying logic of complexity. Cosmic order is not a mere counterpoint to entropy, 
but the product of causal consolidation within an entropic background. From physical 
structures to living systems and human civilizations, the roots of evolution can be coherently 
framed within this principle. In doing so, Order Dynamics not only integrates insights from 
evolutionary theory, dissipative structures, and emergence, but also establishes a theoretical 
cornerstone for understanding how order arises and evolves in the universe.

Classical Problems 
Within the prevailing framework of physics, not only do major theoretical paradoxes
 
persist, but many empirical phenomena remain obscure. This section offers alternative 
interpretations from the perspective of Order Dynamics. 
Natural Forms: Circles and Spheres 
Circular and spherical forms are among the most universal patterns in nature: from stars 
and planets to droplets, bubbles, and atomic probability clouds, stable configurations across 
scales gravitate toward these geometries. Classical explanations appeal to energy 
minimization—for instance, the sphere’s minimal surface-to-volume ratio. Order Dynamics, 
however, situates this ubiquity within a deeper causal–entropic framework. 
Entropic force drives systems toward homogeneity, erasing gradients; causal force 
promotes cohesion and stability, favoring integrative symmetry. The circle and the sphere 
represent the balanced resolution of these opposing drives: geometries that simultaneously 
realize diffusion and coherence. Their universality is thus not a geometric coincidence but the 
morphological imprint of the causal–entropic game. 
This perspective extends across scales: the spherical symmetry of stars, the roundedness 
of droplets and bubbles, and the probability distributions of microscopic particles can all be 
seen as manifestations of geometric order closure. From this view, closed structures (such as 
closed strings) naturally satisfy the stability conditions of causal–entropic balance more 
effectively than open ones. While this does not invalidate the mathematical formulation of 
open strings, it invites reconsideration of their dynamical plausibility within the logic of 
cosmic order.

To test the conjecture that spherical symmetry constitutes a universal manifestation of 
causal–entropic equilibrium, a spherization experiment may be designed—releasing a 
non-spherical droplet (or molten cluster) in microgravity and tracking its morphological 
evolution. If OD’s account is correct, the droplet should converge toward a sphere—not 
merely through local surface tension, but as a universal dynamical expression of causal–
entropic interplay. Such experiments would not only validate the principle underlying circular 
and spherical universality but also provide a clear point of comparison with conventional 
physical interpretations, thereby highlighting the explanatory scope of Order Dynamics. 
The Origin of Circular Motion 
In the framework of Order Dynamics, the circular and elliptical trajectories of celestial 
bodies and microscopic particles emerge from the tension between entropic and causal forces. 
Entropic force drives matter toward unbounded linear escape—the most efficient path in 
terms of freedom, minimizing external constraints and structural coupling. Left unchecked, 
however, such escape would gradually sever the object’s ties to the causal network, 
diminishing its integration with the fabric of cosmic order. 
Causal force acts to prevent this ―causal disengagement,‖ exerting a centripetal pull that 
continually restores the object to its causal web. Crucially, this centripetal action does not 
abolish the entropic drive toward linear escape but holds it in dynamic balance. The result is a 
stabilized compromise—manifesting as circular or elliptical motion. 
Thus orbital motion is not a mere geometric outcome but a dynamical expression of 
cosmic order itself: entropic force seeks dispersion and maximal degrees of freedom; causal

force restores cohesion and preserves causal density; and the orbit emerges as the stable form 
sustained by their asymmetric contest. 
Mass and Inertia 
Within OD, the Higgs mechanism is understood as a crucial process of order formation, 
while the Big Bang marks the first macroscopic ―event of order,‖ establishing the material 
and energetic preconditions for cosmic evolution. The Big Bang itself can be seen as an 
abrupt phase transition: from the highly compressed, ordered singularity to a diffuse, 
disordered energy field. In this regime, Entropic Force prevailed; particles were massless, 
unstable, and locked to light-speed motion, incapable of assembling into any lasting structure. 
As the universe cooled, the Higgs field matured and became “activated” as a generator 
of macroscopic order. Through interaction with this field, particles acquired mass【8】, their 
motion slowed below light speed, uncertainty diminished, and, for the first time, they could 
be organized, constrained, and connected. Thus, the Higgs mechanism not only endowed 
particles with mass but also conferred upon them an ―identity of order,‖ enabling integration 
into causal networks. Mass, in this sense, is the physical embodiment of order—the 
foundation upon which successive layers of complexity, from atoms and molecules to stars 
and life, were built. 
Mass, however, signifies not only the presence of order but its persistence. The greater 
the mass, the denser its internal causal relations and the more robust its external linkages, 
making the overall network more resistant to disruption. Any external attempt to alter this 
state is, at root, an attempt to disturb established causal order. Inertia is the expression of this 
resistance: if mass embodies the existence of order, inertia embodies its preservation.

Together they disclose a universal logic: within an entropic universe, order not only emerges 
but also fortifies itself against dissolution. 
This principle extends beyond physics. In social systems, individuals exist not in 
isolation but as nodes within causal networks formed by habits, institutions, and relationships. 
Attempts to change state—migration, career shifts, or role transformations—are often met 
with resistance in the form of psychological strain, fractured ties, or resource loss. These 
obstacles are not incidental; they are the natural counteraction of social order to perturbation. 
Social inertia thus mirrors physical inertia: both express the protective force of order against 
disruption. 
From the mass of elementary particles to the inertia of macroscopic bodies, and further 
to the stability of social systems, OD provides a unified interpretive lens. Mass represents the 
initiation of order, inertia its continuity, and social inertia its cross-level projection. Together, 
they reveal a universal principle: every transformation of order is accompanied by an intrinsic 
force of resistance.

Spacetime Curvature 
In Einstein’s general relativity, gravity is described as the curvature of spacetime 
induced by mass and energy【9】, elegantly summarized as: “matter tells spacetime how to 
curve, and spacetime tells matter how to move.” Yet, despite its elegance, this formulation 
leaves unanswered a deeper question: why should mass curve spacetime at all? Order 
Dynamics (OD) provides a new perspective by interpreting curvature as the direct expression 
of causal-chain density. 
1. Causal networks as the fabric of spacetime. 
In OD, the universe is conceived as a vast network of causal chains. Causal Force drives their 
generation and extension, and spacetime emerges as the geometric manifestation of this 
woven causal fabric. Spacetime is thus not an independent stage but the geometry of causality 
itself. 

19 
 
2. Mass as condensed causality. 
Mass can be understood as the condensation of causal interactions. A massive body functions 
as a causal hub, concentrating an exceptionally dense and robust causal network. This 
network not only binds the body’s constituents but also radiates outward, altering the 
distribution of causal chains in its vicinity. Greater mass corresponds to higher causal density. 
3. Curvature as geometry of causal density. 
When causal chains are highly concentrated, the surrounding causal fabric is drawn inward, 
producing a deflection in the trajectories of causal flow. Geometrically, this appears as 
spacetime curvature; dynamically, as the free-fall motion of matter toward massive bodies. 
Curvature, in this sense, is simply the geometry of causal-chain density: 
Sparse causal density → spacetime approaches flatness. 
Dense causal density → spacetime curves strongly. 
Thus, spacetime curvature is not an abstract deformation but the natural imprint of causal 
distribution. The greater the mass, the denser the causal network, and the stronger the 
curvature. OD thereby complements general relativity by grounding its geometric description 
in a deeper causal logic—casting gravity as the geometry of causality itself.

Methodological Critique 
The dominant trajectory in modern physics seeks a Grand Unified Theory (GUT) or 
Theory of Everything (TOE) through a bottom-up paradigm—attempting to reconstruct 
cosmic laws from the smallest conceivable entities, such as quarks, leptons, or strings【27】. 
Yet this reductionist strategy faces intrinsic philosophical and practical constraints. 
 Infinite regress of the ―ultimate constituent.‖ 
Every attempt to identify the most fundamental building block of reality risks an infinite 
regress: each smaller scale reveals new layers of structure and parameters, extending 
rather than resolving theoretical incompleteness. 
 Structural overdependence. 
Current unified frameworks rely heavily on microstructural assumptions—specific 
particle types, symmetries, and parameters. This reductionist dependence limits their 

39 
 
capacity to address emergent and irreversible phenomena, where macroscopic order 
arises from collective dynamics rather than elementary constituents. 
 Loss of macroscopic explanatory power. 
A comprehensive physical theory must explain not only microscopic interactions but also 
universal large-scale regularities such as thermodynamic irreversibility, temporal 
asymmetry, and geometric coherence. Yet bottom-up approaches often lack the 
conceptual tools to derive these macroscopic invariants directly from their fundamental 
postulates. 
Order Dynamics (OD) adopts an opposite, top-down and mechanism-first approach. 
Rather than presupposing structural primitives, OD abstracts all interactions into two 
fundamental effects—aggregation (causal force, 𝐹𝑐) and dissipation (entropic force, 
𝐹𝑒)—thus focusing on how order evolves rather than what constitutes it. This renders OD 
intrinsically independent of incomplete microscopic knowledge, establishing the Order 
Equation as a system-level foundation for all physical phenomena. 
Accordingly, the relationship between OD and GUT should not be conceived as OD 
adapting to microscopic frameworks, but rather as GUTs and related theories aligning their 
dynamical principles with the macroscopic laws of order evolution revealed by OD.

in final dissolution, what follows? In either case, causal chains are broken, leaving the regress 
of explanation unresolved. 
A logic without closure collapses into infinite regress. To remain coherent, it must 
complete itself. Transposed to cosmology, this requirement suggests cyclicity. Left 
unchecked, entropic force would disperse the universe into irreversible disorder; causal force, 

42 
 
by contrast, demands some form of closure at the macroscopic scale, ensuring the closure of 
causal chains and the self-maintenance of order. 
Cyclicity thus appears not as speculative ornament, but as the natural large-scale 
expression of the causal–entropic contest—and as the logical resolution of coherence applied 
to cosmic history. The universe’s destiny may therefore lie not in linear decay or terminal 
dissolution, but in recurrent dynamics: an eternal cycle, a Big Crunch, or a Big Bounce【29】. 
Though still theoretical, this perspective links dynamics with logic, offering a unified 
framework for understanding the deepest trajectory of cosmic evolution. 
Future Directions 
As a unifying theoretical framework, Order Dynamics provides a causal foundation for 
understanding the emergence and evolution of order across physical, biological, and social 
systems. Yet its broad conceptual reach also demands practical paths toward formalization 
and empirical grounding—a formidable task in itself. 
    Translating such conceptual breadth into formal structure is far from trivial. The 
challenge of formalizing OD lies in the fundamental and nonlinear nature of the forces 
described by OD. History shows that equations governing the deepest realities of the universe 
are inherently difficult to resolve: the Einstein Field Equations【30】, which couple spacetime 
geometry with matter, admit exact solutions only under highly symmetric conditions. 
Similarly, the core formulation of OD—the Order Equation, which links causal and entropic 
forces through dynamic feedback—is nonlinear and remains largely conceptual. This is not a 
deficiency of the theory but a reflection of the complexity inherent in the asymmetric, 
non-equilibrium coupling between Causal Force 𝐹𝑐 and Entropic Force 𝐹𝑒 across all scales

of existence.

advancing the computational formulation of the Order Equation, establishing experimental 
validation through interdisciplinary collaboration, and extending OD’s causal–entropic 
framework toward integrative and cross-domain applications.

Conclusion 
Though still at the stage of a conceptual framework, Order Dynamics (OD) already 
reveals exceptional explanatory power and unifying potential. At a philosophical level, one 
may observe that classical mechanics, relativity, quantum theory, thermodynamics, and even 
string theory, along with sociology, economics, and law, all converge on a single theme: the 
generation and preservation of order. Yet these theories typically address order only within 
their own domains, often by invoking models grounded in equilibrium or symmetry. 
The key innovation of OD is to move beyond this equilibrium paradigm. It traces 
cosmic evolution to its ontological roots in the asymmetric, non-equilibrium interplay of 
Causal Force 𝐹𝑐 and Entropic Force 𝐹𝑒. This asymmetry supplies the energy differentials 
and evolutionary potential that drive the universe from particles to civilizations. Entropy 
increase is thus not merely energetic disorder but also material dispersion, and it is through 
the continuous tension of causality and entropy across these dimensions that order arises and 
evolves.

By contrast, traditional physics has largely privileged order, treating disorder as 
disturbance or end state. Thermodynamics often casts entropy as a pessimistic prelude to heat 
death; statistical mechanics reduces disorder to probability averages【34】; cosmology links 
entropy to ultimate dissolution. Disorder is thus acknowledged reluctantly, rarely as a 
principle equal to order. OD departs from this stance by elevating Entropic Force to a 
fundamental driver alongside Causal Force: not a purely destructive tendency, but a 
necessary counterpart whose tension with causality powers the generation, transformation, 
and recurrence of order. 
More importantly, OD may offer a unified causal foundation for three classical extremal 
principles in physics—geodesics, energy minimization, and least action【35–37】. Rather 
than representing isolated results of geometry, statistics, and variational calculus, these laws 
can be interpreted as complementary expressions of a single principle of order-generation 
efficiency. In this view, geodesics embody spatial efficiency in causal propagation, energy 
minimization reflects energetic efficiency in sustaining order, and least action represents 
dynamical efficiency in the temporal evolution of systems. Together they express nature’s 
general tendency to maximize the efficiency of order formation under the continuous 
interplay between causal and entropic forces. Although this correspondence is presented at a 
conceptual level rather than as a formal derivation, it suggests a promising route for future 
mathematical development within the OD framework. 
Taken together, the significance of OD lies in its universality. It provides physics with a 
deeper dynamical foundation for norms and symmetries, while also offering a shared 
language for dialogue and integration across disciplines.

The optimal mechanism in high-frequency batch trading with multiple news
Mingshun Sun, Haijun Yang
School of Economics and Management, Beihang University, Beijing, 100191, China
Abstract
We develop a new high-frequency batch model based on game theory to analyze market behavior and address
the issue of optimal trading strategies under multiple news arrival scenarios. In this framework, the behaviors
and the profits of high-frequency traders (HFTs) are critically influenced by both the quantity and the sequence
of news arrival. The model incorporates three interconnected games among HFTs and is refined to identify
the trembling-hand perfect Nash equilibrium (THPNE). By maximizing the total market welfare measure, an
optimal batch interval is determined to achieve the most efficient THPNE within the batch mechanism. Within
this equilibrium, we characterize the optimal spread strategy and dynamic order strategy of HFTs. Single- and
multi-period batch models are constructed to compute payoff matrices by analyzing market microstructure and
HFTs’ behavior across multiple sequential news arrival scenarios, thereby enabling the derivation of the market
spread and HFT profits. Finally, we show the optimal batch interval, a key practical finding that balances
operational efficiency with strategic advantage, thereby providing actionable insights for market design and
participant strategy.
Keywords: Batch mechanism; Trembling-hand perfect refinement; High-frequency trading
JEL classification C73; D41; G11
1. Introduction
High-frequency trading contributes to price discovery and enhances market liquidity, making it a dominant
trend in modern financial markets (Nahar et al., 2024). Currently, most exchanges utilize the traditional continuous trading mechanism to facilitate high-frequency trading. However, high-frequency trading does carry
potential risks such as an arms race (Hoffmann, 2014; Chung and Kim, 2024), flash crashes (Kirilenko et al.,
2017; Leal and Napoletano, 2019), and adverse selection risk caused by risk-free arbitrage (Cartea et al., 2014),
due to the nature of the continuous trading mechanism. In response to these challenges, researchers have begun
to explore alternative mechanisms for high-frequency trading. The batch mechanism is an excellent attempt.
To mitigate resource waste caused by high-frequency trading arms races, Budish et al. (2014, 2015) introduce the frequent batch auction (FBA) mechanism. Based on the auction mechanism, the FBA mechanism
eliminates risk-free arbitrage opportunities and achieves a superior trade-off between liquidity and information
production compared to the traditional continuous mechanism (Baldauf and Mollner, 2020). However, most
prevailing trading exchanges still operate under the conventional market maker mechanism, which differs from

the auction-based FBA mechanism. To address this discrepancy, Ge and Yang (2025) integrate the batch mechanism with the prevailing market maker model, moving away from the auction based framework. Their model
effectively resolves the pricing problem under the constraint of at most a single news arrival per batch.
However, in more general scenarios, such as multiple news arrivals, the trading environment becomes more
complex. First, risk-free arbitrage vanishes if multiple news arrive before settlement, thereby diminishing the
profitability of high-frequency traders (HFTs). Second, both the quantity and the sequence of news arrival are
highly influential in determining HFTs’ profits. Consequently, the strategic interactions among HFTs become
intricate: they can no longer snipe indiscriminately. Instead, they must adopt more cautious, strategic decisionmaking in this complex game-theoretic environment. Interestingly, the HFIs even engage in sniping on both
sides of the trade in a three-news-arrival scenario, a strategy never adopted by rational HFTs in any single-newsarrival scenario. Hence, investigating the behavioral strategy decisions of high-frequency trading (HFT) and the
optimal design of financial markets represents a valuable and pertinent contribution to the market microstructure
research literature.
In this paper, we creatively develop a three-tiered game-theoretic model to analyze interactions among
HFTs under a market maker based batch mechanism within a general setting involving multiple sequential
news arrival scenarios. Our goal is to characterize the behavioral traits and strategic choices of each type
of high-frequency participant and identify the optimal equilibrium of the batch mechanism. First, we define
three interconnected games: the HFT game, in which HFTs compete for the high-frequency market maker
(HFM) position; the HFM game, in which potential HFM competitors determine the spread quoting strategy;
and the HFI game, in which high-frequency investors (HFIs) dynamically optimize the order timing strategies.
Second, we construct both single-period and multi-period batch models to compute the payoff matrices. We
detailedly analyze the market microstructure and HFTs’ behaviors under each condition across various multiple
sequential news arrival scenarios, deriving the payoff expectancies of HFTs across all cases. The equilibrium
analysis reveals intriguing insights: unlike the unique risk-free arbitrage strategy observed in traditional single
news arrival scenarios, the three-tiered game exhibits multiple Nash equilibria, and HFTs consistently employ
identical strategies in each Nash equilibrium; however, not all equilibria are stable or perfect. Finally, we refine
the complex game model based on the trembling-hand perfection framework (Dhillon and Mertens, 1996; Luo
et al., 2022) to identify the optimal trembling-hand perfect Nash equilibrium (THPNE).
In THPNE under news neutrality, the HFM adopts the narrowest spread quoting strategy, termed "price
priority for the market maker"; HFIs choose to submit orders upon the news arrival with lantecy, termed "time
priority for investors", rather than waiting until the final possible moment. This behavior supports the arms
race inherent in the batch mechanism, benefiting total market welfare but harming market liquidity. Analysis within the single-period batch model reveals that in the THPNE: (i) As trading speed increases, or news
frequency increases, or the number of HFTs decreases, the market liquidity decreases while the total market
welfare increases; (ii) As LTs frequency increases, the market liquidity decreases while the total market welfare
initially increases and subsequently decreases; (iii) As the batch interval increases, the market liquidity initially

1.1. Literature review
Since THPNE is a hybrid model that integrates a high-frequency batch mechanism with game-theoretic
approaches, we investigate existing research methodologies in these two aspects.Budish et al. (2014, 2015)
initially design a new batch trading model and subsequently formalized the FBA mechanism framework. Under the auction mechanism, cancel orders are prioritized for execution; thus, the snipe window is limited to
the latency gap between HFTs, which is negligible compared to the batch interval. In contrast, the snipe window under the continuous mechanism is open the whole time. By transforming speed competition into price
competition, the FBA mechanism effectively eliminates risk-free arbitrage opportunities among HFTs.
Does the FBA mechanism perform as effectively as expected? Academic opinions remain divided: Supporters argue that the FBA mechanism dominates the continuous mechanism by reducing adverse selection costs
(Eibelshäuser and Smetak, 2022; Zhang and Ibikunle, 2023; Aquilina et al., 2024; Breckenfelder, 2024), lowering the risk of market collapse (Ladley, 2020; Jagannathan, 2022), preventing the price destabilization (Sadzik
and Woolnough, 2021; Lu and Zhan, 2022),and enhancing market liquidity (Aldrich and Vargas, 2020; Haas
et al., 2021; Alves et al., 2023); While critics contend that the FBA mechanism fails to consistently improve
market liquidity (Haas and Zoican, 2016; Lee et al., 2020), it exhibits diminishing returns in liquidity improvement beyond a certain batch interval length. Moreover, the FBAs are sources of short-term price volatility
(Gehrig and Ritzberger, 2022). Although the FBA mechanism remains theoretical, with most literature relying on simulations based on continuous-market data, it still presents a promising solution for high-frequency
trading. Actually, empirical evidence suggests that certain trading algorithms achieve meaningful performance
under the FBA mechanism, though their dominance hierarchy differs from that in the continuous mechanism
(Savidge and Cliff, 2023).

Nevertheless, unlike the conventional market maker mechanism, the FBA mechanism employs an auctionbased approach, fundamentally differing from most prevailing continuous trading systems. In light of this
distinction, Ge and Yang (2025) develop a hybrid framework that integrates the batch mechanism with the
market maker mechanism, subject to the constraint of at most a single news arrival per batch. They theoretically
demonstrate that the batch mechanism benefits market liquidity but harms market volatility compared to the
continuous mechanism.
This paper employs a three-tiered complex game model to address multiple news scenarios. For stochastic games in continuous time with imperfect information, such as multiple unstable Nash equilibria, a natural
solution is the Markov perfect equilibrium (MPE) (Maskin and Tirole, 2001). However, the payoff matrices
of HFTs lack the Markov property, so that we choose another effective method — trembling-hand perfect
refinement (Du et al., 2020). Trembling-hand perfect equilibrium, as a refinement method of perfect correlated equilibrium (PCE), can eliminate unstable or imperfect Nash equilibria effectively (Selten, 1975; Dhillon
and Mertens, 1996). Carbonell-Nicolau (2011); Carbonell-Nicolau and McLean (2014) prove the existence of
trembling-hand perfect equilibrium in discontinuous normal-form games and potential games. Jackson et al.
(2012) find out that trembling-hand perfect refinement only be robust under some strict principle on perturbations. Bajoori et al. (2013) investigate the relations among trembling-hand perfect equilibrium and other types
of perfect equilibrium.
Then Chatterji and Govindan (2006) extend PCE to N-player games, and Liu and Ghosh (2020) prove that
the revelation principle for PCE holds in N-player two-action games. Their research is directly applicable to
our model of N HFTs, making the trembling-hand perfection refinement a particularly effective choice. More
recently, Luo et al. (2022) show a correlated equilibrium with message-dependent trembles, providing a strategic foundation for undominated correlated equilibrium — an approach particularly relevant to the scenarios
described in this paper. While Neugebauer et al. (2025) provide empirical evidence in support of the tremblinghand perfect equilibrium on the Game of Selten’s Horse, we complement this refinement by a compelling
application on high-frequency batch trading scenarios.
While recent literature has made significant contributions to both batch mechanism and game theory, none
can be directly applied to handle the complex strategic environment outlined in our problem statement. To the
best of our knowledge, no existing study has systematically analyzed strategic interactions among HFTs under
the multiple news arrival scenarios. In this paper, we innovatively apply the trembling-hand perfection method
to the batch mechanism framework, addressing a key question in high-frequency trading: what constitutes the
optimal equilibrium under general market conditions.
2. The three-tiered game model with high-frequency batch mechanism
In this section, we present a model of a three-tiered complex game with a high-frequency batch mechanism
under multiple sequential news arrival scenarios. Let τ denote the length of a batch interval. During the period
(0, τ], news arrives followed by an independent Poisson process with arrival rate λ, and each news impacts the

asset price by σ. It is reasonable to assume news neutrality, which means that the probabilities of good and bad
news are both 50%.1 When τ is large enough, multiple news arrive, then affect the decisions and benefits of
market participants further.
There are two types of market participants: high-frequency traders (HFTs), who trade rapidly based on
current market information; and liquidity traders (LTs), who arrive stochastically and trade immediately upon
arrival.
• High-frequency traders (HFTs): HFTs can choose between two roles: (1) becoming high-frequency
investors (HFIs) or (2) competing to serve as the high-frequency market maker (HFM). Once an HFT
wins to become the HFM, all other HFTS have to act as HFIs. Subsequently, all other market participants
trade exclusively with the HFM under the market maker mechanism. Each HFIs faces an identical small
response latency denoted by δ (where δ ≪τ). During the trading period (0, τ], HFIs can determine to
submit orders or not at anytime according to their information set. All HFI are ex ante homogeneous, but
can adopt heterogeneous trading strategies.
• Liquidity traders (LT): LTs arrive followed by an independent Poisson process with arrival rate µ
2 on
both the bid and ask trade sides, acting as low-frequency traders. LTs trade immediately for liquidity
motivation upon arrival. Under the market maker mechanism, only the first LT on each side can get the
order from HFM if there is no HFT in front of it.
The batch mechanism trading formation process operates as follows. In the beginning, an HFM competed
from N HFTs determines the bid-ask spread s,2 and submits a bid quote and an ask quote to establish the
limit order book. Subsequently, HFIs and LTs sequentially submit orders within the time interval (0, τ]. At
the settlement time τ, the HFM executes trades with the first order on each side, provided at least one order
exists. In our model, orders are executed according to "price-time priority" principle, and order cancellations are
prohibited. Considering that HFIs and LTs are price takers, meaning all market participants are homogeneous
in price, order timing becomes the sole determinant of trading outcomes.
There are three interconnected games in the trading formation process in chronological order.
• Step 1: the HFT game. At time 0, HFTs need to determine to be HFI or to compete to be the HFM. N
HFTs compete to become the designated HFM.
• Step 2: the HFM game. At time 0, the winning HFM need to determine spread quoting strategy.
• Step 3: the HFI game. During time period (0, τ], HFIs need to dynamically determine order strategy,
including whether and when to submit orders.
To solve these sequential games, we should first analyse the HFI game to derive the payoff functions of
HFIs; next solve the HFM game via equilibrium computation to determine the spread; finally refine the HFT

2.2. The HFM game
In the HFM game, the HFM determines the spread to maximize profit. Traditional single news models
typically require spread to exceed the price impact of news (s > σ), enabling HFIs to snipe the stale quotes
exactly when the news arrives to make a risk-free arbitrage. However, our multiple news model under the
batch mechanism may lead to scenarios that don’t exist in the single news frameworks. In the multiple news
model, the HFM must consider multiple spread thresholds to match the multiple news cases.4 Thus, the HFM
needs to determine the spread between different discrete interval: [0, σ), [σ, 2σ), · · · . In our competitive setting,
potential HFMs determine these spread quoting strategy to balance profitability against the risk of being sniped.

potential HFM competitors are indifferent to whether their strategies ensure competitive success. Consequently,
the theoretical optimal Nash equilibrium is the strategy that maximizes profit. This dynamic incentivizes HFMs
to progressively widen their spreads, ultimately driving market liquidity toward zero, which is an undesirable
outcome. To address this phenomenon, our model refines the Nash equilibrium by considering the market
liquidity, selecting ([0, σ), [0, σ)) as the optimal strategy. 7 8

4. Single-period batch model with limited multiple sequential news
In this section, we focus on the payoff expectancies of players and the optimal equilibrium in the threetiered games under the batch model with limited multiple sequential news. Our analysis proceeds in three
stages: First, we develop the payoff expectancy frameworks of both HFIs and the HFM. Subsequently, we
conduct a comparative analysis of between HFIs’ strategy 1 versus strategy 2 in the HFI game.13 Finally we
give the Nash equilibria of the three-tiered games in different scenarios, and identify the optimal parameters of
the most efficient Nash equilibrium.
4.1. Model specification
In the single-period batch model with multiple sequential news, the basic framework follows the specifications outlined in Section 2. In the high-frequency setting, the probability of observing many news arrivals
within a given interval is negligible; we can treat such events as low-probability. Consequently, we simplify the
multiple sequential news assumption in Section 2 to a limited multiple sequential news framework, focusing

4.4. Equilibria
In the refinement of Nash equilibria of the HFM game and the HFI game, the HFM adopts the spread
strategy s1, meanwhile all of HFIs adopt strategy 1. In the market equilibrium of the HFT game, the payoff
expectancies of HFIs and the HFM should satisfy equation(2). So far, all HFTs have reached their optimal
strategies in the three-tiered games.
We begin with the simplest news case and progressively consider more complex news conditions. As the
number of cases increases, the probability distribution of our model becomes more realistic, thereby better
approximating real world conditions.
4.4.1. No news arrival case
This represents an extreme case, and the payoff expectancies of HFIs and the HFM in the no news arrival
scenario are given in equation(11). In this case, HFIs execute no action, resulting in zero payoffs, while the
HFM maintains a positive payoff expectancy. Theoretically, the HFM can determine the spread quote s∗at any
positive value, since LTs always trade immediately upon arrival. Consequently, all HFTs are incentivized to
compete for the HFM position. The winner takes all.

Some key parameter effects are observed:

HFTs and s∗increase.
5. N: When the number of HFIs (N) increases, the competition of the HFM intensifies. Each HFI receives
a smaller profit share, while the HFM’s profit remains unchanged at the beginning. Consequently, both
the payoff expectancies of HFTs and s∗decrease.

4.6. The optimal batch mechanism
We have identified the optimal strategies of all HFTs under the batch model with limited multiple sequential
news. Furthermore, how to design a more efficient batch mechanism to enhance market quality remains a
critical research question. Generally, market quality is measured along two key dimensions: the market liquidity
(Han et al., 2023) and the total market welfare (Yan et al., 2025). The former is commonly represented by
the spread s∗, while the latter can be captured by the profit of the HFM E(B∗). A lower s∗or a higher
E(B∗) indicates superior market quality. In the reality, the parameters σ, λ and µ are determined by market
circumstances, which are uncontrolled exogenous variables. In contrast, our model treats the parameters δ, N,
and τ as controllable exogenous variables, allowing for systematic optimization of market design.
Figure 12 – 17 demonstrate that when δ decreases, both s∗and E(B∗) increase, which means a decrease in
liquidity and an increase in welfare. A shorter latency enables HFIs to snipe stale quotes more easily, thereby
reducing more liquidity. Meanwhile more snipes mean more trades and more welfare. Consequently, HFTs
compete to decrease the latency δ in pursuit of higher profits, and directly compounds liquidity deterioration,
which triggers "the high-frequency trading arms race" described in proposition 5. Unlike the frequent batch
auction (FBA) mechanism in Budish et al. (2015) or the traditional single news batch model, where the arms
race does not significantly increase welfare but primarily harms liquidity, our multiple news batch model enhances the effect of latency. Under multiple news condition, the latency δ exerts a stronger influence on the
probability of successful sniping. As a result, the arms race in our framework indeed increase the total market
welfare.
Proposition 5. "The high-frequency trading arms race" enhances the total market welfare, but weakens the
market liquidity.

When N increases, both s∗and E(B∗) decrease, indicating an increase of liquidity and a decrease of

When τ increases, both s∗and E(B∗) first increase and then decrease, with the former peaking earlier

LTs, leading to higher liquidity but lower welfare. Consequently, this yields a liquidity minimum and welfare
maximum, which implies the existence of an optimal batch interval τ maximizing the total market welfare,

5. Multi-period batch model with limited multiple sequential news
In this section, we extend the single-period batch model to a multi-period batch model. The extended model
retains the same specifications as the base model, except that it incorporates multiple batch intervals rather than
a single one. In the multi-period framework, we consider the news arriving during the last period but observed
during the subsequent period; therefore, the market participants (also game players) will execute action in the
subsequent batch interval.

• Multiple batch interval: periodical time periods (0, τ], (τ, 2τ], · · · . Order cancellations are prohibited.

processing..

• LTs: arrive followed by a Poisson process with parameter µ

5.2. Payoff expectancies
Similar with the single-period batch model, we still calculate the payoff expectancies of HFIs and the HFM
with strategy 1 and strategy 2 across different scenarios. However, in the multi-period batch model, the news
arriving during the period (τ −δ, τ] donnot influence the payoff expectancies of the current batch interval
(0, τ]. By contrast, the news arriving during the last period (−δ, 0] affect the payoff expectancies of current
batch interval (0, τ] in deed, and the probability distribution of news arrival during the period (τ −δ, τ] and
period (−δ, 0] are exactly identical. Consequently, unlike the single-period batch model, the news that arrives
during the period (τ −δ, τ] does not affect the payoff expectancies in the multi-period batch model. All HFTs
observe the news arrival during the period (−δ, τ −δ], and subsequently take actions during the period (0, τ].

Interestingly, the time period can be decomposed into two dimensions: the information dimension and the

records the quotes of the HFM and the snipes of HFIs. Notably, the action dimension lags behind the information dimension by δ.21 For instance, all HFTs observe the information dimension during (τ −δ, 2τ −δ], and
execute their corresponding actions in the action dimension during (τ, 2τ].

6. Conclusion
This paper proposes THPNE as a solution concept for the high-frequency trading issue within the batch
mechanism under multiple sequential news arrival scenarios. The proposed theoretical framework considers,
for the first time, the complex strategic interactions among HFTs, thereby enabling a detailed characterization
of their behavioral traits and strategic decision-making. To our knowledge, this is the first work to employ a
game-theoretic model in which HFTs optimize their trading strategies. Beyond its context-specific applications,
this work also offers several key theoretical contributions to the existing literature.
We establish a three-tiered complex game structure and employ the trembling-hand perfection method to
refine the multiple Nash equilibria into THPNE. This approach effectively resolves the behavioral decisionmaking problem for HFTs in the multiple news arrival scenarios. We characterize the behavioral traits and
payoff matrices of HFTs across multiple news arrival scenarios in both single-period and multi-period batch
models, and analyze the impact of various factors on market microstructure. This analysis provides a foundation
for informed investment decisions in the market. We derive the equilibrium spreads and profits in THPNE and
further identify an optimal batch interval that maximizes total market welfare. This finding points out the
optimal batch mechanism for high-frequency trading.
Several key propositions emerge from THPNE. First, the HFM adopts the narrowest spread quoting strategy, while HFIs determine to submit orders upon the arrival of news with latency δ. In certain scenarios, rational
HFIs may even submit orders on both trade sides of the market. Second, HFIs are incentivized to reduce their
latency δ to maximize their payoff expectancies, leading to a "high-frequency trading arms race". This behavior enhances the total market welfare but weakens market liquidity. Third, the latency parameter δ becomes
irrelevant in the multi-period batch model.
There are several research directions in which the model presented could be extended. One important avenue is to incorporate order cancellations into the complex strategic game. It would be necessary to introduce
cancellation penalties, as the absence of such restrictions could reintroduce opportunities for risk-free arbitrage.
In such scenarios, the HFM may be forced to bear higher adverse selection costs. Another promising direction
is comparing batch markets with continuous markets. When batch and continuous trading mechanisms coexist
within the same market, it raises questions about the potential for cross-mechanism arbitrage. Moreover, the
strategic interactions among HFTs become more complex. Does such arbitrage exist? How do strategic behaviors differ between mechanisms? Moreover, which market mechanism is likely to be dominant? Addressing
these issues will be a central focus of our future researches.

Appendix C.5. The market liquidity equilibrium in the batch model
In the three complex games, there exists an implicit equilibrium: the market liquidity equilibrium. As is
well-established, trades can’t be executed in the absence of market liquidity. Consequently, determining how
market liquidity achieves the equilibrium is a critical issue in the batch model with limited multiple sequential
news.
In the batch model, there are three types of market participants: LTs, the HFM and HFIs. When the HFM
trade with an LT, the LT acts as the liquidity provider while the HFM acts as the liquidity consumer. When
an HFI snipes a quote, the HFM acts as the liquidity provider while the HFI acts as the liquidity consumer.
From a market-wide perspective: (i) LTs supply all of the liquidity. (ii) The HFM redistributes a portion of this
liquidity to HFIs while consuming a small fraction itself. (iii) HFIs solely consume the liquidity. In the market
liquidity equilibrium, the total liquidity supplied by LTs must equal the combined liquidity consumed by the
HFM and HFIs.
Several key factors influence market liquidity in the batch model:
1. The probability of LTs: When the probability of LTs increases, the market liquidity increases because
more liquidity providers enter the market. This explains why the equilibrium spread s∗decreases with
the increase in µ.
2. The probability of snipe: When the probability of snipe increases, the market liquidity decreases because
HFIs consume more liquidity by exploiting stale quotes. For instance, when more news arrive, HFIs can
snipe mispricing quotes more easily and consume more liquidity. Consequently, s∗increases with the
increase in λ. The impact of τ on the market liquidity can also be explained by this theoretical framework.
3. The competition of snipe: When the competition of snipe increases, the market liquidity increases because more investors tend to trade. In the batch model, An increase of N solely increases the competition
of snipe without altering the total probability of snipe. Consequently, this leads to a decrease of the snipe
price, resulting in HFIs consuming less liquidity.
Consider an extreme scenario of no LT. Theoretically, in the absence of LTs, the market lacks liquidity
providers, preventing any trades. To verify this claim, we analyze the batch auction model under the no LT
scenario. As established in remark 2 in main paper, the payoff expectancies of HFIs are positive in this scenario,
while the payoff expectancy of the HFM is negative because the market is a zero-sum game. By substituting
the payoff expectancies into equation(2) in main paper, we will derive that the s∗in equilibrium converges to
either 0 or σ, implying the payoff expectancies of all participants is 0. Consequently, no HFT has an incentive
to trade. This analysis demonstrates that LTs are essential for normal market functioning. If there is no LT,
a market-making subsidy must be introduced to incentivize the HFM to act as a liquidity provider, thereby
restoring market viability.

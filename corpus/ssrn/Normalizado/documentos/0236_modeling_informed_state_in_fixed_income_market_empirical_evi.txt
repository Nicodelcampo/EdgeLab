Modelling Informed State in Fixed Income Market :
Empirical Evidence from HFT Data. ∗
Alok Kumar, Madhucchand Darbha †
January 6, 2010
Abstract
Motivated by ﬁndings in the existing literature on the information content of order ﬂows, we specify Probability of Informed Trading - Asymmetric
Autoregressive Conditional Duration (PIN-AACD) model to identify the ‘informed state’ in the Fixed Income Markets. Using tick-by-tick price and order
ﬂow data, we obtain the estimates for the probability of informed (PIN) state
throughout the trading day. The trade direction (buy versus sell orders) and
the duration between trades are modelled jointly. The model allows the Probabilities of Good News and Bad News to vary each interval. Results show that
intraday patterns of the PIN estimates are consistent with the scheduled arrival
of public information or news. Further, we show that PIN is positively related
to overall market depth. The eﬀect of realized volatility on information asymmetry is unexpectedly found to be negative. We also ﬁnd positive correlation
between news and trading volume. The study intends to support trading and
sales teams in identifying informed trades.

1.1
Probability of Informed Trading
Probability of Informed Trading (PIN) is a measure of information asymmetry - it
measures the fraction of trades in a time interval that arises from informed traders.
Intuitively, PIN measures market makers’ belief about the trades being informed. PIN
measures the ability of traders or clients to interpret signals on the expected evolution
of the asset price. The following question is relevant from the traders’/market makers’
perspectives - ‘Given the state at t, what is the probability that a trade in the next
interval will be informed trade’. PIN measures the implicit risk for market makers to
2
Electronic copy available at: https://ssrn.com/abstract=1666818

unintentionally create trade opportunities for counterparties or clients, because they
are less informed than their counterparties. Probability of informed trading is not
something that is directly observable, how to measure informed trading is therefore
an important and practical issue. By simply counting the number of buys and sells
during a time interval, we can get a proxy of information-motivated trading. The
crux behind the PIN framework is that information related trading happens in intervals with informed traders receiving a private signal, which induces a larger number
of buy orders if the private signal is positive or a larger number of sell orders if the
private signal is negative.
The model of Easley et al.1 has become a standard application for measuring the degree of informed trading. EKOP use trade-direction data to estimate the proportion
of trades initiated by informed traders. Their model is based on the number of buy
and sell orders, the intensities of which depend on the existence of ‘news’ or information. Conditional on the arrival of news, information is further classiﬁed as being
either ‘good’ or ‘bad’. EKOP model the aggregate number of buy- and sell-initiated
trades each day as independent Poisson variables, with diﬀerent intensities for days
with good news, no news and bad news. The characterization of each trading day
is unknown, and the likelihood of the numbers of buy and sell orders is based on
the mixture-of-Poisson distribution. PIN is then calculated as the ratio of the intensity of informed trades divided by the intensity of all trades (informed or uninformed).
The EKOP model is estimated as follows : Assume that prior to the beginning of each
trading day nature selects that an information event will occur (with probability α)
or not (with probability 1-α). With probability δ the information will be a bad news
event, with probability 1-δ it will be a good news event. Uninformed traders arrive
each day with probability ϵ. Informed traders only arrive on days with information
events at the rate µ. Assuming independence across days, which is consistent with
an informational eﬃcient market, a direct estimate of α,δ, ϵ and µ is obtained using the maximum likelihood function from EKOP. Then, the probability of informed
trading(PIN) is deﬁned as the ratio of the expected number of trades originated by
informed traders divided by the total expected number of trades, whether they are
initiated by informed or uninformed traders, which is expressed as δ = αµ/(αµ+2ϵ).
Most of the prior works using PIN required an assumption that it was constant over
a substantial period of time. However, PIN may be time varying and our aim is to
formulate a methodology by which we are able to continuously update PIN based on
past information. Recently, some studies estimated PIN at higher frequencies, such

In order to estimate the importance of adverse selection or informed trading we compute PIN using a structural model that is extension of AACD methodology. The
model is similar to that of EKOP framework as both these models adopt mixture of
distribution approach. EKOP framework assumes the occurences of single information event per day whereas the model discussed here incorporates information events
occuring at all times during the day. The model draws inference based on tick-by-tick
trade data, whereas the EKOP sequential trade model is based on daily aggregate
buying and selling pressures. Additionally, the PIN-AACD framework allows for interactions between consecutive buy-sell orders, accounts for the duration between
trades and the volume of trade and allows the Probabilities of Good News and Bad
News to vary each interval. Originally developed by Tay, Ting, Tse, Warachka (2009),
the PIN-AACD approach has been initially estimated on equity markets but never
applied to bond markets. This paper ﬁlls this gap and propose an estimate of the
PIN-AACD measure calculated on a high frequency bond market dataset.
Before we apply the concept of PIN to the bond market, we need to interpret private
information due to the diﬀerent information structures of the stock and bond markets. In the stock market, private information is often viewed as inside information
of a ﬁrm’s cash ﬂows. In the government bond market, macroeconomic variables are
the dominating factors that aﬀect bond prices. As a result, private information in
the Treasury bond market mainly arises from heterogeneous interpretation of public
information and dealers’ private access to customer order ﬂows. Some traders may
have an advantage in assessing how macroeconomic variables might aﬀect bond prices
due to their superior analytical skills. On the other hand, uninformed traders in the
bond market could represent regular investors who do not have special skills in analyzing public information and cannot observe order ﬂows.

High Frequency PIN Estimation
This section describes a methodology to estimate PIN using high-frequency transaction data. The method is based on an AACD model for trade directions. We ﬁrst
outline the AACD model as proposed by Bauwens and Giot(2003). The speciﬁcation of the conditional expected duration equation is then constructed to account for
informed and uninformed trades due to the arrival of news. Finally, we extend our

methodology to allow the probabilities of good and bad news to vary across time, and
estimate PIN on intraday basis.
2.1
Methodology: AACD Model
Trade direction is modelled using AACD model and PIN is computed as byeproduct
of the model. Let yi denote the trade direction of the ith trade at time ti , which
may take values of j = -1 or 1 representing a sell-initiated and buy-initiated trade,
respectively. Let φi−1 denote the information set after the (i-1)th trade. φi−1 may
consist of past trade directions, transaction volume, and lagged durations. Given
φi−1, we assume that each of the two potential trade directions of the trade at time
ti follows a latent stochastic point process whose inter-arrival times have independent exponential distributions. The observed trade direction is the outcome of the
competition between the two underlying Poisson point processes to be the ﬁrst arrival. Conditional on the information set φi−1 after the (i-1)th trade, the inter-arrival
time random variables of the latent processes follows an exponential distribution with
mean (conditional expected duration) ψji , where ‘j’ denotes the latent trade direction
and ‘i’ denotes the trade at time ti.

equivalently (ti , yi )), denoted by pi(xi, yi|φi−1), be given by

Conclusion
The identiﬁcation of informed and uninformed trading activities is an important topic
in market microstructure. We present an empirical model for inferring the information content of bond trades at the transaction level. Using the PIN-AACD model, we
are able to identify the arrival of informed traders to the ﬁxed income market. This

trading. The comparison of our PIN estimates with return volatility, market depth,
news and trading volume supports the economic implications of our methodology.
The analysis might support trading and sales desks in identifying informed trades,
however the valuable information generated from the analysis can also be tailored to
support varying degrees of usage and type of audience. PIN estimates can also be
used for proﬁling clients into informed and uninformed clients. PIN estimates can

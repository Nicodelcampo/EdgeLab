Abstract
This study explores the suﬃcient conditions for achieving synchronization
in the third-order one-axis generator models, characterized by the secondorder networked Kuramoto oscillators integrating the ﬁrst-order amplitude
dynamics. We show that if the phase diﬀerence of oscillators is less than π
2
and the initial amplitudes are positive, then the amplitudes are bounded and
possess a positive lower bound. Furthermore, under speciﬁc conditions on
the coupling strength, inertia, and oscillator parameters, we prove that the
third-order model exhibits a frequency synchronization and the derivatives of
amplitudes converge to zero. Numerical simulations are performed to support
our main results.
Keywords:
synchronization, Kuramoto, power system, amplitude, voltage,
third-order.

1. Introduction
Over the years, synchronization phenomena have been extensively studied
in various domains, such as circadian rhythms [2, 13, 22], ﬁreﬂy ﬂashes [14],
neural networks [15], biological oscillators [27, 42], and clock synchronization
[24]. The Kuramoto model [23] proposed to demonstrate synchronization

networks science [30, 32, 36, 37, 41]. Synchronization theories related to the
model (1.1) were explored in [11, 17, 18]. Notably, in [18], it has been demonstrated that under conditions of small inertia and large coupling strength,
the majority of concentration leads to complete frequency synchronization.
Meanwhile, bifurcation analyses associated with the Kuramoto model were
investigated in [8, 9, 28, 29]. For studies involving time delay and phase lag
in the Kuramoto model, we refer to [3, 20, 21]. The global convergence of
frequency synchronization for (1.1) with N = 3 was studied in [19]. The
driving forces behind synchronization in the model (1.1) were addressed in
[4, 5]. For a comprehensive overview of the Kuramoto model, see [1, 12].
Traditionally, the classical generator model, known as the networked
second-order Kuramoto model in the power system [6, 7, 10] and microgrid [38], has been extensively investigated in previous studies, assuming a
constant voltage for each generator. However, this assumption lacks realism, given the signiﬁcant concern for voltage stability in power engineering
practices [25, 33, 39, 35]. Indeed, historical blackouts have often been attributed to dynamic voltage instability. In the realm of dynamic stability
within power systems, it becomes essential to employ more comprehensive
dynamical models capable of capturing realistic dynamics. In this regard,
the one-axis generator model was introduced for this purpose [6, 7, 10, 38].
However, the majority of existing works have predominantly focused on simulation studies, with only a limited amount of research dedicated to analytical
advancements in this area. For instance, the Lyapunov function approach,

Our major contribution lies in developing the synchronization theory for
these new class of networked Kuramoto models described by (1.1.a)–(1.1.b)
and (1.2.a)–(1.2.b). It is noteworthy that both systems (1.1.a)–(1.1.b) and

zero condition, this suggests that maintaining the boundedness of the phase
diﬀerence and the boundedness of the amplitudes can drive frequency synchronization, as presented in Lemma 2.1 and Lemma 2.2. However, achieving
the desired boundedness poses analytical challenges. We successfully demonstrate that the amplitudes bi(t), for all i = 1, 2, · · · , N, possess a positive
lower bound b and an upper bound b if the phase diﬀerence of oscillators is
less than π
2 over a speciﬁc time interval, as illustrated in Lemma 3.1. Simultaneously, we leverage these bounds to assert that the phase diﬀerence
of oscillators is less than π

i = 1, 2, · · · , N, throughout the time interval, as demonstrated in Lemma 3.2
for the model (1.1.a)–(1.1.b) and Lemma 3.3 for the model (1.2.a)–(1.2.b),
and subsequently extend this time interval. Compared to the literature [44],
the strategy proposed in this paper provides an analytical foundation to
explore a frequency synchronization theory equipped with milder suﬃcient
conditions on the initial data. The detailed description is presented in Remark 2.3.
The rest part of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 provides
an introduction to notations, the mean zero condition, and Lyapunov functions, followed by the presentation of our main results. Key lemmas and the
proofs are given in Section 3. In Section 4, we conduct various numerical
simulations. Finally, a concluding remark is addressed in Section 5.

5. Concluding Remark
In summary, this paper focuses on establishing suﬃcient conditions for
synchronization in both model (1.1.a)–(1.1.b) and model (1.2.a)–(1.2.b). By
the mean zero condition and the Lyapunov function, the boundedness of the
phase diﬀerence and the boundedness of the amplitudes lead to a frequency
synchronization.
We prove that if the parameter k exceeds the coupling
strength, the ratio of the diameter of natural frequencies to the coupling
strength is small, and the initial oscillator conﬁguration is conﬁned to a
sector with the arc length less than
π
2 and positive amplitudes, then the
model (1.1.a)–(1.1.b) exhibits an asymptotic frequency synchronization. Additionally, we establish a theory of frequency synchronization for the thirdorder Kuramoto model (1.2.a)–(1.2.b) with a mild condition on the initial
oscillator conﬁguration. Furthermore, if all natural frequencies are identical, then phase synchronization emerges in both model (1.1.a)–(1.1.b) and
model (1.2.a)–(1.2.b). Our numerical experiments demonstrate instances of
synchronization, desynchronization, divergent amplitudes, and amplitudes
reaching zero within ﬁnite time.

A Regime-Aware Hybrid Pairs Trading System with
Sentiment-Driven Intelligence
Abstract
Pairs trading, a widely used statistical arbitrage strategy, is often constrained
by static pair selection mechanisms and limited adaptability to evolving market conditions. Existing approaches predominantly rely on numerical indicators, overlooking sentiment-driven behavioral dynamics that influence market
regimes. From an artificial intelligence perspective, this paper proposes a hybrid decision-making framework that fuses numerical market indicators with
sentiment-derived textual information to enable adaptive and context-aware
learning. From an engineering perspective, the proposed framework is applied to
the design of an intelligent pairs trading system for equity markets. The system
integrates financial news sentiment analysis using the VADER model, a Random Forest–based pair selection mechanism guided by correlation and volatility
features, and a regime-aware sector rotation module that dynamically aligns
trading actions with bullish, bearish, and neutral market states. By embedding
social sentiment into an engineered learning framework, the proposed approach
enhances robustness and adaptability in trading decisions. Experimental evaluation on historical equity datasets demonstrates consistent outperformance over
conventional pairs trading methods in terms of regime responsiveness, trade selection quality, and risk-adjusted returns. These results highlight the effectiveness of hybrid artificial intelligence systems that combine numerical and textual
data for resilient financial decision intelligence.
Keywords:
Statistical arbitrage, sentiment analysis, market regime detection,
sector rotation, artificial intelligence

1. Introduction
The financial markets are complex socio-technical systems where collective
behaviors, investor sentiment, and macroeconomic shifts jointly influence asset
dynamics. Among quantitative strategies, the pairs trading strategy (PTS) is
widely used, exploiting the mean-reverting behavior of correlated stock pairs
(Gatev et al., 2006; Vidyamurthy, 2004). However, traditional approaches such
as correlation- and cointegration-based methods (Rad et al., 2016) suffer from
static pair selection, limited adaptability to market regime changes (Do & Faff,
2010), and overreliance on numerical indicators while neglecting sentimentdriven signals (Chang et al., 2021; Tetlock, 2007). These shortcomings reduce
resilience, especially during crises such as the COVID-19 crash (Krauss, 2017).
Recent advancements in sentiment analysis and machine learning have highlighted the significant impact of unstructured textual data, including financial
news, analyst reports, and social media sentiment, on asset price dynamics and
market behavior (Lemmon & Portniaguina, 2006)-(Schmeling, 2009).
These
textual signals capture the psychological and emotional states of market participants, often serving as early indicators of market movements that traditional
numerical indicators may fail to detect. Prior studies have demonstrated that
integrating textual sentiment with quantitative factors can enhance stock return predictability and risk management (Liagkouras & Metaxiotis, 2025)(Chen,
2023).
Despite this, most conventional pair selection methodologies in pairs
trading continue to rely solely on numerical measures such as price correlation, cointegration, or volatility, thereby overlooking valuable sentiment-based
insights. This gap motivates the development of hybrid frameworks that combine numerical and textual features to improve pair selection robustness and
adaptability in dynamic market conditions.
To address this gap, this paper introduces a Hybrid pairs trading system
(HPTS), a hybrid pairs trading strategy that integrates multi-source data,
sentiment-driven analytics, market regime detection, and sector rotation into
a unified framework. By combining numerical indicators with textual sentiment

2. Related survey
Pairs trading, a statistical arbitrage strategy introduced by Gatev et al.
(Gatev et al., 2006), exploits the mean-reverting behavior of historically correlated stock pairs. Traditional approaches, such as correlation- and cointegrationbased methods, lack adaptability to market shifts, leading to suboptimal trades
and higher risks (Vidyamurthy, 2004). This section provides a structured review of existing pairs trading strategies, various pair selection methods, regimeswitching techniques, and sector rotation strategies, highlighting their strengths,
limitations, and sector-dependence parameters.
PTS traditionally rely on numerical data, such as stock prices, returns,
volatility, and cointegration, using Pearson correlation and statistical tests to
identify optimal pairs (Rad et al., 2016)-(Sarmento & Horta, 2020). Several

3.3. Market regime switching models
The regime-switching model is a critical tool in financial modeling that captures structural shifts in market behavior (Bekaert & Harvey, 2003)-(Haase
& Neuenkirch, 2023). Traditional pairs trading strategies assume static relationships between stocks, often failing to adapt to dynamic market conditions.
Regime-switching models address this limitation by classifying market states
into distinct regimes, such as bullish, bearish, and neutral phases, allowing
trading strategies to adjust accordingly (Montoya & Gonzalez, 2019). Mathematically, a regime-switching model can be represented using a HMM, where
the latent state variable Lt follows a Markov process:
P(Lt = k|Lt−1 = m) = πmk,

where πmk denotes the transition probability from regime m to regime k. The
model estimates regime-dependent parameters for volatility, correlation, and
mean-reversion strength (Su´arez-Cetrulo et al., 2023)-(Alexiou & Tyagi, 2020),
enhancing the adaptability of pairs trading strategies. By incorporating regimeswitching, pairs trading models can dynamically allocate positions based on
evolving market conditions, thereby improving performance and risk management.
The classic HMM is adopted for regime detection as it offers an effective
balance of interpretability, computational efficiency, and robustness. Although
more advanced techniques such as hierarchical HMMs, Bayesian change-point
models, or LSTM-based predictors can capture richer dynamics, they typically
require larger datasets, introduce higher complexity, and reduce transparency.
Considering these trade-offs, HMM remains a reliable and widely accepted choice
for integrating regime-switching into pairs trading.

6. Conclusion
This paper presented HPTS, a hybrid pairs trading framework that integrates numerical indicators with sentiment signals, employs an RFR-based
composite score for robust pair selection, and combines HMM-driven regime
detection with sector rotation for adaptive exposure management. Comprehensive backtesting and out-of-sample evaluation, conducted using the Backtrader
package and inclusive of transaction costs, confirmed consistent profitability
with a portfolio gain of 25.57%. HPTS not only delivered superior cumulative
and risk-adjusted returns compared to traditional and machine learning-based
baselines, but also demonstrated resilience, adaptability, and scalability, underscoring its potential as a practical and intelligent strategy for navigating
volatile market environments. While the use of general sentiment inputs and
fixed thresholds introduces certain constraints, the results demonstrate HPTS’s
robustness, adaptability, and scalability, establishing it as a resilient and intelligent trading strategy for volatile market environments. To further enhance
its generalizability and realism, future work will focus on leveraging domainspecific sentiment benchmarks (e.g., FinancialPhraseBank), extending evalua31

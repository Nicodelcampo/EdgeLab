LLM-Driven Market Microstructure
Representation Learning
for Robust High-Frequency Forecasting
Yifan Serena Guo
November 20, 2025
Abstract
This paper studies the integration of large language model (LLM) style encoders
with market microstructure data for high-frequency forecasting. We propose a framework called Market Microstructure Representation Learning (MMRL), which converts
order-book dynamics into a sequence of structured tokens, and extracts a latent execution representation that can be used for multi-horizon prediction. We formalize the
tokenization scheme, the latent embedding, and the forecasting objective, and provide
a simple robustness result under adversarial microstructure noise.
The focus is on
mathematical formulation and interpretability rather than model size.
1
Introduction
High-frequency markets are driven by a continuous stream of limit order book (LOB) updates, market orders, cancellations, and other microstructure events.
Although deep sequence models are widely used in this context, they often treat the data as generic time
series without an explicit structure for execution intent or latent order-flow pressure.
We ask a concrete question: Can we design a representation of LOB dynamics that is
closer to the way LLM-style encoders process structured sequences, and does such a representation yield robustness gains under noisy microstructure conditions?
To address this, we introduce a representation learning framework MMRL with three
components:
1. Microstructure Tokenization (MST): a mapping from raw LOB states and flows
to a sequence of tokens capturing state, changes, and local imbalances;
1

2. Latent Execution Embedding (LEE): a latent vector extracted by an encoder,
interpreted as an execution-intent representation;
3. Cross-Horizon Adaptive Forecasting (CHAF): multi-horizon prediction heads
sharing a common latent representation.
We formulate these objects mathematically and derive a variance bound for the latent embedding under adversarial perturbations of the microstructure tokens.

The proposition is intended as a qualitative statement: a Lipschitz encoder combined
with bounded token noise leads to bounded fluctuations in empirical risk. This supports the
use of LLM-style encoders with explicit microstructure tokenization when noise robustness
is a concern.

Discussion and Extensions
The mathematical structure above can be extended in several directions:
• incorporating cross-asset microstructure tokens by augmenting xt with indicators from
related instruments;
• introducing a risk-aware objective combining prediction error with a penalty on the
variance of zt;
• adding constraints on Gθ to enforce monotonicity properties, such as stronger latent
response to large imbalances.
7

The main contribution of this paper is to provide a clean, tractable formulation of LLMstyle microstructure representation learning and to give a simple robustness guarantee in
terms of a variance bound under Lipschitz assumptions.
10
Conclusion
We presented a mathematically explicit framework for integrating LLM-style encoders with
limit order book data via microstructure tokenization, latent execution embeddings, and
multi-horizon forecasting heads. Under a coordinatewise Lipschitz assumption, we showed
that the variance of the latent representation is bounded by the variance of the perturbed
tokens, suggesting improved robustness in noisy microstructure environments.
The formulation is flexible and can be used as a starting point for more detailed empirical
work, including calibration on actual high-frequency datasets and integration with execution
strategies.

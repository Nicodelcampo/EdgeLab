Abstract
3
Temporal graphs are a class of graphs defined by a constant set of vertices and a changing set of edges,
4
each of which is known as a timestep. These graphs are well motivated in modelling real-world networks,
5
where connections may change over time. One such example, itself the primary motivation for this paper,
6
are public transport networks, where vertices represent stops and edges the connections available at some
7
given time. Exploration problems are one of the most studied problems for temporal graphs, asking if an
8
agent starting at some given vertex v can visit every vertex in the graph.
9
In this paper, we study two primary classes of temporal graphs. First, we study temporal graphs with
10
frequent edges, temporal graphs where each edge e is active at least once every fe timesteps, called the
11
frequency of the edge. Second, temporal graphs with regular edges, graphs where each edge e is active at
12
any timestep t where t ≡se mod re, with se being the start time of the edge, and re the regularity.
13
We show that graphs with frequent edges can be explored in O(Fn) timesteps, where F = maxe∈E fe,
14
and that graphs with regular edges can be explored in O(Rn) timesteps, where R = maxe∈E re. We provide
15
additional results for public transport graphs, temporal graphs formed by the union of several routes, corre16
sponding to the schedules of some modes of transit, and for sequential connection graphs, temporal graphs
17
in which each vertex has a single active in-edge per timestep, iterating over the set of edges in some order.

1. Introduction
19
In many real world settings, networks are not static objects but instead have unstable connections that
20
vary with time. Such examples include public transport networks [1] and infection control [2]. Temporal
21
graphs provide a model for such time-varying networks. Formally, a temporal graph G is a generalisation
22
of (static) graphs containing a common vertex set V and ordered sequence of T edge sets (E1, E2, . . . , ET ),
23
each called a timestep (also known as a snapshot), with the lifetime of the graph defined by the number of
24
edge sets. Unlike some models of dynamic graphs, we assume that we have full knowledge of the graph, i.e.
25
that we are given every edge set as part of the input.
26
Much work on temporal graphs has focused on the problem of exploration of temporal graphs. In this
27
problem, some agent (or set of agents) must visit every vertex in a temporal graph. The agent is restricted in
28
being allowed only a single move per timestep, thus as the graph changes, the agent movement may become
29
restricted. This was motivated by extending the well known Travelling Salesman to temporal graphs
30
by Michail and Spirakis [3]. Since then, there has been a broad span of work covering reachability [4, 5, 6]
31
and exploration [1, 3, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13].
32
The decision version of TEXP in which one has to decide if at least one exploration schedule exists in
33
a given temporal graph from a given starting vertex is an NP-complete problem [3]. Indeed, this problem
34
remains NP-complete even if the underlying graph has pathwidth 2 and every snapshot is a tree [14], or if
35
the underlying graph is a star and the exploration has to start and end at the center of the star [15].
36
In the optimisation version of this problem, we ask for the quickest exploration of a given temporal
37
graph, that being the schedule ending at the earliest timestep, formally defined in Section 2.
38
On the positive side, we mention the landmark paper by Erlebach et. al. [10], who show that any
39
always connected temporal graphs (temporal graphs where each timestep is connected) can be explored in
40
O(n2) timesteps, where n is the number of vertices. For graphs where the underlying graph (the static
41
graph corresponding to the union over all edge sets) is planar, they reduce this to O(n1.8 log n) timesteps,

Moving to other classes of temporal graphs, Erlebach and Spooner [12] showed that k-edge-deficient
50
temporal graphs, temporal graphs in which each timestep has at most k-edges removed from the underlying
51
graph, can be explored in O(kn log n) timesteps, or O(n) when only a single edge is removed.
52
On the negative side, we have, by Erlebach et al. [10], that there exists a class of always connected tem53
poral graphs requiring Ω(n2) timesteps to explore, and that there exists temporal graphs with an underlying
54
planar graph of degree at most 4 that cannot be explored faster than in Ω(n log n) timesteps. Further, in
55
[12] Erlebach and Spooner showed that there exist some k-edge-deficient temporal graphs that cannot be
56
explored faster than in Ω(n log k) timesteps.
57
In this paper, we focus on the exploration of temporal graphs with frequent and regular edges. We
58
define such graphs by how often each edge appears and, more importantly in this work, the maximum
59
number of consecutive timesteps such that a given edge is not active. More formally, given a edge, e, we
60
define the frequency of e, fe, as the smallest value such that ∀t ∈[1, T −fe], e ∈S
t′∈[t,t+fe] Et′, with T
61
being the lifetime of the graph. The regularity of an edge, e, is defined as the smallest value, re such that
62
∀t ∈[1, T −re], e ∈Et iff e ∈Et+re. We are motivated primarily by public transport networks, where
63
an edge represents a connection between stations. In such systems we assume, or hope, see [1], that the
64
connections between stations in the system have some known frequency or preferably regularity.
65
The notions of frequency and regularity, by our definitions, have been studied previously with a particular
66
focus on graph properties [17, 10, 18, 19, 20], and Cops and Robber games [21, 22]. Of particular relevance
67
to us are the papers by Flocchini et al.
[23].
Bellitto et al.
[24] and Erlebach et al.
[10].
In [23],
68
the authors study the problem of periodic graph exploration from a distributed perspective, showing that
69
without information about the period of the system, this problem can not be solved. In [10], the authors
70
study a similar definition where each edge has a known, constant, frequency, and there exists a constant
71
c such that every cut contains at least one edge every c timesteps. The authors show that there exists an
72
exploration schedule requiring at most O(m) timesteps, where m is the number of edges in the underlying
73
graph, and further that this holds even if the graph is unknown in advance. In [24], Bellitto et al. study
74
restless exploration, a restriction of exploration where the agent must move every timestep if possible, of
75
periodic temporal graphs. The authors show that determining if the graph has a restless exploration is
76
solvable in polynomial time if the graph has a period of 2, equivalent to a regularity of 2 in our model, and
77
NP-Hard if the period is greater than 2.
78
Our Results. We show that graphs where each edge has a frequency of at most f can be explored in f(2n−3)
79
timesteps, and that graphs where each edge has a regularity of at most r can be explored in r(2n −3)
80
timesteps. We note that is generalises the result of Erlebach et al. [10] by removing the requirement that
81
the frequency is a constant, or that the edges in the cuts appear at least once per constant number of rounds,
82
at the cost of potentially slower exploration, particularly for sparse graphs. Further, as our definition of
83
regularity generalises the definition of periodicity for temporal graphs given in [24] and of edge periodicity
84
given in [22], thus can be directly applied to these settings.
85
Further, we explicitly introduce and study the following sets of temporal graphs, applying our main
86
results to each in order to get an upper bound on the exploration time:
87
• Public Transportation Graphs: A class of temporal graphs defined by a given set of (temporal) walks,
88
with the edges active at timestep t corresponding exactly to the edges in the walks used in that
89
timestep. We show that every edge in these graphs has a period of at most the length of the longest
90
walk, L, and thus can be explored in L(2n −3) timesteps.

104

106

113

A directed graph is a graph where each edge has an orientation, with a fixed start point and end point.
115
Thus, the edge (vi, vj) is not equivalent to (vj, vi). A walk on a directed graph is defined in the same way as
116
a walk on an undirected graph. In this paper, we restrict ourselves to symmetric directed graphs. A directed
117
graph G = (V, E) is symmetric if the edge (vi, vj) ∈E iff (vj, vi) ∈E. The in-edges of a vertex v is the set
118

119
is the set of edges in out(v) = {(v, u) ∈E}, i.e. the set of vertices with the start point v.

Temporal Graphs. A temporal graph is a generalisation of a graph, herein called a static graph whenever
129
confusion may otherwise arise, where, rather than having a single edge set, the graph contains an ordered
130
sequence of timesteps, by convention E1, E2, . . . , ET , while maintaining a shared set of vertices. We call
131
each edge set a timestep (also known as a snapshot). An edge e is active at timestep t if e ∈Et. We assume,
132
without loss of generality, that each timestep contains at least one active edge. In our definition, an edge e
133
may be active in any number of timesteps.
134
By default, we assume that the temporal graph is undirected, with a temporal graph referred to as a
135
directed temporal graph if the edge sets are directed. We assume that either every edge set is directed,
136
or none are.
Given a temporal graph G the lifetime of the graph is the number of timesteps, thus, if
137
G = (V, E1, E2, . . . , ET ), then the lifetime of the graph is T.
138

Exploration. An exploration of a static graph is a walk W such that there exists, for every vertex v ∈V at
163
least one edge in W containing v. Analogously, an exploration of a temporal graph is a temporal walk W
164
such that there exists, every vertex v ∈V , at least one edge in W containing v.

166
vertex v ∈V and length ℓ∈[1, T], does there exist a temporal walk W starting at v with a length less than

ℓ?

Problem 2 (Fastest Temporal Graph Exploration Problem). Given a temporal graph, G = (V, E1, E2, . . . , ET ),
170

for e ∈e1, e2, . . . , em do
▷Iterate over the edges in the walk W in order.
while e /∈Et do
▷Increment the value of t until we reach a timestep with e active.
t ←t + 1
end while
Append (e, t)) to W
▷Append the tuple (e, t) to the end of the walk W.
t ←t + 1 ▷Increment t one last time to avoid adding multiple edges to W one the same timestep.
end for
return W
end procedure

234
v ∈V , in f(2n −3) timesteps and, further, a temporal walk exploring G in O(|E|(log |V | + T)) time.
235
Proof. Utilising the same approach as Theorem 1, we construct the temporal walk W exploring G. Note
236
that each edge has a frequency of at most f, thus the weight of the minimum weight spanning tree is at most
237
nf. Observing that, by Lemma 3, we can explore this tree in O(2n −3) steps, the length of the temporal
238
walk W is at most f(2n −3), corresponding to waiting f −1 steps at each of the 2n −3 edges traversed in
239
the walk, followed by one timestep traversing the edge.

4.1. Public Transport Graphs
274
We first consider public transport graphs.
We use such temporal graphs to model public transport
275

Here, the temporal graph is composed of a set of temporal walks, each representing some
276

277

278

279

280

281

283

284
there exists some Wi such that e is active in timestep t (modulo the length of the walk). Note that an edge
285

5. Conclusion
303
In this paper, we have studied the set of temporal graphs defined by having frequent edges, both in
304
the general case and in several motivating settings, including public transport networks and for sequential
305
connection graphs. The primary result of this paper is that any such graph with n vertices, where each edge
306
is active at least once every F timesteps, can be explored in F(2n −3) timesteps.
307
There are several obvious open questions following this paper. First is if the lower and upper bounds for
308
exploration of temporal graphs with frequent edges can be closed, either through improving the exploration
309
algorithm, or by constructing a stronger worst-case example.

Stochastic Arbitrage Opportunities: Set Estimation and
Statistical Testing
Stelios Arvanitis∗and Thierry Post†
November 7, 2022
Abstract
A Stochastic Arbitrage Opportunity (SAO) is dened as a zero-cost investment portfolio that
enhances every feasible benchmark portfolio for all admissible utility functions. The present
study provides a formal theory of consistent estimation of the set of SAOs and an Empirical
Likelihood Ratio test for testing whether a given arbitrage portfolio is a SAO or to test whether
an empirical SAO is a population SAO out of sample.
Key words: Portfolio analysis; Arbitrage portfolios; Asset pricing; Asymptotic statistics;
Empirical Likelihood.
1
Introduction
Arvanitis and Post (2022) propose a general framework for ordering arbitrage portfolios and nding
generalized arbitrage opportunities based on Stochastic Dominance criteria. In their framework,
a Stochastic Arbitrage Opportunity (SAO) is a zero-cost investment portfolio that enhances all
benchmark portfolios that can be constructed from a set of base assets (instead of a single market
index), for all admissible utility functions.
For empirical estimation and statistical inference, they sketch a theory of consistent estimation of
the set of SAOs and an Empirical Likelihood Ratio (ELR) test for testing whether a given arbitrage
portfolio is a SAO or to test whether an empirical SAO is a population SAO out of sample. The
present study provides a formalization of the theory and additional discussion about generalizations.

3.2
Empirical Likelihood Ratio test
In their empirical applications, Arvanitis and Post (2022) construct arbitrage portfolios that are
SAOs in a given sample, and test whether they are SAOs out of sample. This approach evaluates a
single, optimized arbitrage portfolio with portfolio weights that are xed and known out of sample,
and thus avoids possible distortions of the test size that stem from evaluating multiple candidates
or tting portfolio weights to the test sample.
The natural choice for the null hypothesis seems H0 : δ ∈∆WAO
(U,K,P), in this context. After all,
our statistical theory predicts that the empirical optimal arbitrage portfolio converges to a true
SAO, if pricing errors occur. This hypothesis allows for more powerful tests than the alternative
H1 : δ /∈∆WAO
(U,K,P) or H1 : δ /∈∆SAO
(U,K,P), because estimation error generally is more likely to yield
false non-dominance classications than false dominance relations, as the system of dominance
inequalities is 'easier to break than to make'.
The hypothesis structure is also consistent with the common practice for pairwise dominance
analysis.
Standard statistical tests such as Linton, Maasoumi and Whang (2005) focus on the
null of dominance, because the asymptotic distribution is degenerate under the alternative of nondominance. We essentially generalize this null to multiple pairwise dominance relations that are
based on multiple benchmark portfolios.

4
Discussion
The test strategy is aimed at validation of the theoretical prediction that the empirical optimal
arbitrage portfolio is a true SAO in disequilibrium.
The strategy controls the test size or the
frequency of false rejection of a SAO, and it does not directly control the test power or the frequency
of false acceptance of a SAO. One straightforward way to evaluate the test power is to analyze the
rejection rates for arbitrage portfolios which are not expected to be SAOs, such as the vertices of the
arbitrage portfolio set, which generally include concentrated positions that increase the risk level of
the benchmarks and thus are not SAOs. In addition, the test power can be studied by examining
the rejection rates of the ELR test in random psuedo-samples or subsamples for arbitrage portfolio
that are not empirical SAOs.
Extensions are possible to cases where P is a conditional distribution and/or estimated using
alternative non-parametric methods or (semi-)parametric models. The estimation may be based
on a well-specied (semi-)parametric model or a fully non-parametric method, depending on the
data properties and available side information. (Quasi) Maximum Likelihood or Nadaraya-Watson
type estimators could be employed respectively. Standard assumptions that involve smoothness of
the associated likelihood function, or regularity properties of the unknown densities involved and
the associated kernels, would suce to obtain consistency. Parametric Likelihood Ratio tests and
Smoothed Empirical Likelihood can replace the BELR test in these cases.
The derivation of a rened statistical theory for comparison between conservative ELR SAO
testing and Kolmogorov-Smirnov or Cramer-von Mises procedures in the context of weak temporal
dependence is also of interest.

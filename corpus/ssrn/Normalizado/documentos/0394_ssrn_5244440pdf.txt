Abstract
3
In this paper, we consider the edge-bipancyclicity property of hypercubes with
4
faulty edges. For n ≥4, let Qn be an n-dimensional hypercube, and let F be a set
5
of faulty edges in Qn such that |F| ≤3n−11. If every vertex in Qn −F has a degree
6
of at least 2 and no 4-cycle in Qn −F has a pair of non-adjacent vertices both of
7
degree 2, then every edge e in Qn −F lies on a fault-free cycle of every even length
8
from 8 to 2n, with two exceptions: (1) there exists a 4-cycle in Qn −F containing
9
the edge e such that the vertices on the 4-cycle which are not incident with e both
10
have a degree of 2; (2) there exist two vertices of degree 2 which are not endpoints
11
of edge e but are adjacent to the same endpoint of e in Qn−F. This result improves
12
some known results on edge-bipancyclicity of hypercubes with faulty edges.

31

An n-dimensional hypercube Qn is a graph with vertex set
33

35

36
It is well-known that any bipartite graph contains no odd cycles. A bipartite graph G
37
is bipancyclic if it contains cycles of every even length from 4 to |V (G)|. Similarly, it is
38
edge-bipancyclic if every edge of G lies on a cycle of every even length from 4 to |V (G)|.
39
A bipartite graph G is bipanconnected if, for any pair of distinct vertices u and v with
40
distance d, there exists a path from u to v of length l, where d ≤l ≤|V (G)|−1 and l ≡d
41
(mod 2).
42
Fault tolerance is a key measure of network stability, as networks are susceptible to
43
node or link failures. In parallel computing, the ability of a network to tolerate faults is
44
especially critical, driving extensive research into various networks with faulty elements
45
[4, 5, 8, 11, 22, 23, 25].
46
Li et al. [15] explored the edge-bipancyclicity of the hypercube Qn with some faulty
47

48

49
edges, then for any two distinct vertices u and v with distance d, there exist fault-free
50
uv-paths of length l for every l with d + 2 ≤l ≤2n −1 and l ≡d (mod 2).
51

53

54

82
The vertex set and edge set of a graph G are denoted by V (G) and E(G), respectively.
83
In a graph G, the number of edges incident with a vertex v is referred to as its degree,
84

85
of a graph G, denoted by δ(G), is the minimum value of the degrees of all vertices. The
86
distance of vertices u and v in a graph G, denoted by dG(u, v), is the number of edges in
87
a shortest path joining u and v.
88

89

90

91

92

93

94

The cycles of length 6
194
In this section, we discuss the existence of a cycle of length 6 passing through edge e in
195
the hypercube Qn with faulty edges. We illustrate through an example that a cycle of
196
length 6 passing through edge e may not exist when the number of faulty edges is 3n−11.
197
In Qn, for any two vertices u = u1 · · · un and v = v1 · · · vn, the Hamming distance
198
H(u, v) is deﬁned as the number of indices i ∈[n] for which ui̸ = vi. Clearly, dQn(u, v) =
199
H(u, v).
200
Let e = uv. Assume dim(e) = n. Suppose the situation of the 2(n−1) edges (excluding
201
e) incident with u and v is as follows: uu1 is a good edge, vv2 is a good edge, and all
202
other edges are faulty; see Fig 2. Then, in this case, all cycles of length 6 passing through
203
edge e must traverse the path u1uvv2. Denote this path by P. In other words, to ﬁnd a
204
cycle of length 6 passing through edge e, we need to identify a path of length 3 between
205
vertices u1 and v2 that does not intersect with any edges in path P.
206
Since dim(uv) = n, dim(uu1) = 1, and dim(vv2) = 2, the binary strings of u1 and v2
207
diﬀer in exactly three bit positions: 1, 2, and n. Thus, H(u, v) = 3 and there exist 3! = 6
208
paths of length 3 between u1 and v2 in Qn. Among them, one path is exactly the path P,

5
Conclusion
484
Let Qn be an n-dimensional hypercube with at most 3n−11 faulty edges for n ≥4, where
485
every vertex in Qn −F has a degree of at least 2 and no 4-cycle in Qn −F has a pair of
486
non-adjacent vertices both of degree 2. Excluding the speciﬁc fault structures (FS-I) and
487
(FS-II), in this paper, we prove that every fault-free edge in Qn lies on a fault-free cycle
488
of every even length from 8 to 2n. This result signiﬁcantly extends the ﬁndings in [17].
489
Bipanconnectivity under these constraints remains open, requiring novel methods.

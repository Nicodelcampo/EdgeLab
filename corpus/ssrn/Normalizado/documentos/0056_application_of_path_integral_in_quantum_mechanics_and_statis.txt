Application of Path Integral in Quantum 
Mechanics and Statistical Mechanics to 
Quantitative Finance 
Explicit Approximate Expression of Probability Distribution of the Local Volatility Model 
 
Osamu Tsuchiya1 
 
Abstract 
The path integral approach in Quantum Mechanics is applied to the Local Volatility Model of 
quantitative finance. The transition amplitude, which is a fundamental variable in Quantum Mechanics is 
represented via path integral. The Brownian motion corresponds to free particles in Quantum 
Mechanics. The Local Volatility model corresponds to a modification of the free particle Quantum 
Mechanics. The semi-classical approximation is applied to the Local Volatility model, and the 
approximated probability distribution of it is derived. Here, the square of the volatility of the distribution 
is the harmonic average of the square of the local volatility. 
 
 
 
Introduction  
Most models for pricing the derivative are based on the Brownian motion.   For the European 
derivatives, the pricing is represented via a propagator, which gives a probability distribution function of 
the future time. For the pure Brownian motion, the propagator is represented via path integral, which is 
frequently used in Quantum Physics. Brownian Motion corresponds to a free particle in Quantum 
Mechanics in the path integral representation. 
 
On the other hand, the local volatility model is frequently used to capture the volatility skew [3]. The 
local volatility model is typically calibrated via the Dupire formula [3]. For the given local volatility 
model, calculating the probability distribution (propagator) is possible by calculating the call spread 
price with an infinitesimal spread.  However, it needs numerical calculation of the call options and does 
not fit the qualitative analysis of the volatility dynamics.

In this paper, the methodology of quantum mechanics and statistical mechanics is applied to the local 
volatility model and derives an approximate representation of the probability distribution of the local 
volatility model. The propagator and path integral representation is applied to the local volatility model. 
On the path integral representation, the semi-classical approximation is applied. It is shown that in the 
semi-classical approximation, the square of the volatility for the distribution is a harmonic average of 
the square of the local volatility.  
 
In the current practice, stochastic volatility is thought to be necessary to explain volatility dynamics 
properly. However, the distribution of the stochastic volatility model can be mapped to the local 
volatility model [2]. So the results of this paper can be used for the calibration of the stochastic volatility 
model also.

Conclusion 
The local volatility model is analyzed via path integral of Quantum Mechanics. It is shown that for the 
period [𝑡, 𝑇], the volatility for the propagator 𝑃(𝑡, 𝑥(𝑡); 𝑇, x(T)) is estimated by the square root of the 
harmonic average of the square of the local volatility along the strait line which connect 𝑥(𝑡) and 𝑥(𝑇). 
In the practice of the quantitative analysis of the derivatives trading, the average along the straight line 
is typically used. However, the average is not the arithmetic average or its continuous limit, but it is a 
harmonic average.

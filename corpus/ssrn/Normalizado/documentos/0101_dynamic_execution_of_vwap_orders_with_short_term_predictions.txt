Abstract
We consider a Volume Weighted Average Price (VWAP) trading algorithm in which
instead of following the static curve passively, the algo may adjust its participation rate
in each interval. We propose a framework in which the adjustment only makes use of
the expected value of the price appreciation, captured by trading signals. In order to
avoid extreme behaviors, we bound the adaptive trading curve within a so-called trading
envelope. Using two examples of signals, the Forward/Backward and a CAC40 stock, we
conﬁrm the potential improvement of our adaptive framework compared to previous ones.
Key words: VWAP, short-term prediction, trading envelopes

Introduction
In the execution services today algorithmic trading is rapidly becoming the preferred method
for clients to acquire and liquidate position of stock, as well as other asset classes. Typically
a computer based trading algorithm is used to buy or sell a position while attempting to stick
to a client selected benchmark. One of the oldest and most popular benchmarks is VWAP
(Volume Weighted Average Price), which is usually used by large institutional traders to
trade large orders in ﬁnancial markets.
VWAP execution orders represent around 50% of all the institutional investors’ trading.
The popularity of the VWAP benchmark for both brokers and clients stems from several
reasons. Firstly, investors who ask for VWAP execution accept that they will postpone their
trades in order to reduce their trading cost when buying or selling large amount of shares.
For example, foreign investors are often forced to place large orders before the market opens
due to the time lag. In a ﬁxed-price trade, brokers charge a large risk premium because of
the size of the risk. However, investors can save on the risk premium by using VWAP as
a benchmark because market directional risk remains with the investors. Secondly, VWAP
is better than any ﬁxed-time-in-the-future bechmak and also has high price transparency.
If investors use a price at a ﬁxed time in the future as benchmark, the closing price for
instance, brokers are forced to trade intensively towards closing, which results in a huge
market impact and a considerable cost. However, VWAP can reduce the market impact and

a motivation to divide a large VWAP order into several small orders in order to proﬁt from
price ﬂuctuation. As a result, we are motivated to execute more volume in the short period
with better short-term price, while execute less in that with worse short-term price. As an
algorithmic broker, the total volume of a VWAP order in one day is ﬁxed, so the proportion
distributed into short-term periods becomes a key to improve our execution. In one day’s

time, global market VWAP can be regarded as a linear combination of several local market
VWAPs in short-term periods, where this allocation of combination is always called trading
curve. This paper proposes a new method to adapt the trading curve in order to beat the

Generally speaking, there are two approaches to model the optimal VWAP trading strategy. The classical approach is to ﬁnd the optimal static execution trajectory before market
opens which will not be updated during the whole trading period. The ﬁrst work in this area
is the article by [Konishi, 2002] who derives the optimal execution trajectory for single and
basket VWAP execution when the price is given by a Brownian motion. The strategy is then
assessed against actual trade data from the Tokyo stock exchange. [Jedrzej Bialkowski and
Le Fol, 2008] presented a new methodology to modeling intra-day volume which allows for a
reduction of the execution risk in VWAP. [McCulloch and Kazakov, 2007], [McCulloch and
Kazakov, 2012] view it as a quadratic hedging problem under partial information and use
the mean-variance method to minimize the variance of diﬀerence between market VWAP and
execution VWAP.
The new approach is to use an adaptive execution trajectory which comes from available
information of price and volume in the past, as well as some prediction signals. [Almgren and
Lorenz, 2006] proposed an Bayesian estimator of intra-day price drift so as to minimize the
expected transaction cost. The ﬁrst paper dealing with pricing a VWAP-guaranteed and by
product, optimizing a VWAP execution is [Bouchard and Dang, 2012] where authors made
use of the stochastic target framework.
[Frei and Westray, 2013] introduced to modeling
relative volume curve using Gamma bridge, then solved an HJB equation to get an explicit
characterization of the optimal trading rate. [Dang and Lehalle, 2013] show that in combining
estimations on volume and price, one is able to optimize the execution in a sequence manner.
Note that all the above articles except the last one do not allow for an adaptive changing
trading curve using short-term predictions.
The paper is structured as follows.
In Section 2 we introduce the framework dealing
with the execution of VWAP orders with both static and adaptive approaches using trading
signals. Section 3 provides a full discussion on signals and illustrates two important examples, the Forward/Backward signals. Using the previously described methodology and the
input signals, we present the empirical results on an important CAC40 stock BNP Paribas
(BNPP.PA) in Section 4. We conclude our paper in Section 5.

Adaptive trading framework
In this section, we present the modeling of dynamic VWAP order execution algorithm. We
add some trading signals in our framework in order to online update static trading curve.
The aim of this procedure is to get an adaptive trading curve in order to realize dynamic
execution.

Simulated data

• α: percentage of all used signals out of all detected signals.

3.1
Opportunity zone
In classic quantitative ﬁnance models, one postulates diﬀusive behavior of the price: without
a strong view on a drift, the best estimate of the next price is the current price. From a
fundamental viewpoint, investors value the current price with respect to a discounted view
on the future value of the asset; from an arbitrage viewpoint, the current price is adjusted
thanks to comparisons with prices of peers or the liquidity of the market. In view of these
facts, one can postulate a local trend model for prices, in which the latter oscillates around
a deterministic trend leading to the new fair value. Based on this assumption, it thus makes
sense to estimate a benchmark price from observed information.
The idea of opportunity zone is quantitatively similar to Bollinger Bands. To be more
precise, we base the prediction signal on the distance between the current spot price and a
benchmark price. In the framework of this paper, the benchmark can be an estimate of ﬁnal
VWAP based on static trading curve ‘pre’. From the reversal eﬀect, if the price is too far
away from this benchmark price, then it has a strong tendency to revert back to normal value
and vice versa. Also it has a strong tendency to be bigger or smaller than ﬁnal benchmark.
This distance is wisely normalized to give us a meaningful signal between 0 and 1: 0 meaning
a very low price and 1 meaning a very high price. This choice of normalization depends on
the way that we exploit signals in our algorithms. Moreover, it allows us to deal with diﬀerent
signals, diﬀerent securities or diﬀerent market conditions in a homogeneous and consistent
manner.

3.2
Forward/Backward signals
Forward and Backward signals are two proper signals used by Kepler Cheuvreux. Although
based on the same framework, the method of estimation these two signals are totally diﬀerent.
To be more precise, we base the forward signal on the distance between the current price
and the cumulative VWAP up to that point. Here the cumulative VWAP is the benchmark
price, and the distance from this benchmark is estimated from the stock volatility. The real
forward signal is created to estimate a local trend for cumulative VWAP, but distance of
VWAP in next slice and the ﬁnal VWAP. For an algorithmic broker, beating the market does
not always happen, especially in the day with an obvious trend. Once we detect a signal to
say there will be a local trend for cumulative VWAP, with a little change in static trading
curve, we can also make the client satisﬁed although it is not a global optimal time. It is not
reasonable to evaluate the performance of forward signal using the criteria of oracle trading
signal, but in empirical experiments, this signal works well to improve VWAP.
The backward signal is really a signal to estimate the distance between instantaneous
VWAP with ﬁnal VWAP. From the price formation process, in the case without drift, the
best predict of next price is current price. By considering the impact of current price on ﬁnal
VWAP, we can use a linear combination of cumulative VWAP and the current price as the
benchmark, and distance is also estimated from volatility. This benchmark can be regarded
as a discount of ﬁnal VWAP and the envelope can be regarded as a conﬁdence interval that
the predicted ﬁnal VWAP is in this envelope with a certain conﬁdence level.

Now we consider an intuitive question: why do our adaptive trading curves touch the
bounds of the envelope? Before the market opens, pre trading curve and trading envelope
for one day are already ﬁxed. Whether adaptive curve touches the bounds of envelope only
depends on the cumulative eﬀect of signals or to be more precise, the number of appearing
signals. If there are always positive signals appearing, the cumulative eﬀect can make this
dynamic execution so aggressive that adaptive curve touches the upper bound. In a similar
vein, too many negative signals make execution so conservative that adaptive curve touches
the lower bound. Using empirical data we verify the fact that if there is a daily trend of the
spot price, it is more probable for adaptive trading curve to touch the bounds of the envelope.

5
Conclusions
This work focuses on research, development and back-test of several trading signals used for
improving VWAP order execution. The most signiﬁcant contribution of our work compared
with others is that it provides an adaptive execution method that is not yet mentioned in
related articles. Trading signal is a familial concept in technical analysis, but embedding
them into the execution framework is a new attempt as we know. We have conﬁdence that
this domain is well worth being developed in the future.
Algorithmic trading is diﬀerent from proprietary trading. For proprietary trading, we can
execute the volume as more as possible in a short-term period if we can conﬁrm an opportunity
of arbitrage, while in algorithmic trading we have to average total volume from the point of
risk. This remind us that not only trading signal, update strategy is also important. So we
introduce the trading envelope to prevent both aggressive and conservative execution.
From our experiments, our Forward/Backward signal works well except for some extreme
cases with drift, so we need to concentrate on these extreme cases with drift in the future
study.
A signiﬁcant price drift usually comes from a big change of market environment.
So detecting these changes a priori becomes a key to make our execution beat the market.
In the future, we should concentrate on the development of beta-signal, its corresponding
update strategy as well as the method to proﬁt from the advantage of both alpha-signal and
beta-signal.
